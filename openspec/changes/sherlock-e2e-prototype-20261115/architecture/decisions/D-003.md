# D-003: Proven 100-bit security

- Status: accepted
- Authority: OWNER
- Legacy IDs: hard rule; OD-4; OD-19; G1 to G5

## Context and Problem Statement

The design claims a security level only after the qualification gate passes on the frozen keys.

## Decision Drivers

- TERMINOLOGY.md hard rules: proven 100-bit security with no unproved conjecture.
- OD-19: unique decoding for the internal and `L` layers.
- D3 weakest term 101.0110 bits on the T10m keys (LOOP.md row T10m).

## Considered Options

- 100 bits proven on every layer and term, claimed after G1 to G5.
- List decoding with a conjectured bound.

## Decision Outcome

100 bits proven, unique decoding, claimed only after the gate.

Authority: OWNER. Owner ruling (hard rule, LOOP.md line 102); OD-19 owner ruling 2026-11-12.

### Consequences

- Good: One security statement for every program.
- Bad: The T12 gate must rerun after every rekey.

## Confirmation

- outcome: RKY-005 (completion), SQG-014 to SQG-016 on the T25 keys. Status: planned unless the cited requirement reads MEASURED.
- conformance: SQG-001 to SQG-013 (processor-final); slice B SSG requirements. Status: planned unless the cited requirement reads MEASURED.

## Pros and Cons of the Options

### 100 bits proven on every layer and term, claimed after G1 to G5

- Good: No conjecture in the claim.
- Bad: Unique decoding costs queries and wrap rows.

### List decoding with a conjectured bound

- Good: k = 20 becomes possible (wrap-diet study).
- Bad: Against OD-19 and OD-24; a conjecture enters the claim.
