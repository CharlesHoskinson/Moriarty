# D-001: No circuit above k = 21

- Status: accepted
- Authority: OWNER
- Legacy IDs: hard rule "never k = 22"; CPR-001; memory rule 2026-11-11

## Context and Problem Statement

The wrap, the decider and every BLS12-381 circuit have a row budget. A design that does not fit can grow the domain or change a layer.

## Decision Drivers

- CPR-001 (processor-final): no BLS12-381 circuit at k = 22 or above.
- Wrap proving time and memory on boxes the user owns (T14, T14b).
- MEASURED wrap rows 1,685,845 at k = 21 on the T10m keys (LOOP.md row T10m).

## Considered Options

- Keep every circuit at k at most 21; a design that does not fit changes a layer.
- Allow k = 22 as a fallback.

## Decision Outcome

Keep every circuit at k at most 21.

Authority: OWNER. Owner ruling 2026-11-11, restated in LOOP.md line 102 (hard rules). A change needs the owner.

### Consequences

- Good: One bound for every lane and every rekey.
- Bad: Checks compete for wrap rows; T25 must re-measure.

## Confirmation

- outcome: RKY-003 (completion): wrap rows at k = 21 on the T25 keys, measured. Status: planned unless the cited requirement reads MEASURED.
- conformance: CPR-001, MYC-CHIP-007, MYC-KEY-009, MYC-ACC-002; slice C WRP requirements. Status: planned unless the cited requirement reads MEASURED.

## Pros and Cons of the Options

### Keep every circuit at k at most 21; a design that does not fit changes a layer

- Good: The wrap stays provable on a laptop GPU and the fallback is a layer change (LW-014).
- Bad: Each new wrap check must fit 1,782,573 rows (the 85 percent gate).

### Allow k = 22 as a fallback

- Good: More rows for checks.
- Bad: The owner refused it; proving cost doubles.
