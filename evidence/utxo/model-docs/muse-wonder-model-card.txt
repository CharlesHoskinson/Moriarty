2.​ Intended uses

The Muse Wonder models are intended for commercial and research use, with particular
strengths in personal and professional agentic tasks. The models support a broad range
of use cases, including:

   1.​ General-purpose assistance and reasoning — answering questions, writing and
       editing, summarizing information, analyzing documents and data, and reasoning
       through complex problems.
   2.​ Coding and software engineering — generating, explaining, debugging, testing,
       and modifying code, from individual programming tasks to sustained work across
       larger software projects.
   3.​ Personal and professional agents — supporting research, planning, and multi-step
       workflows through tool use and interaction with applications.
   4.​ Domain-specific knowledge work — helping users interpret and work with
       specialized information in areas such as law, finance, and health, including
       understanding documents, synthesizing source material, and preparing drafts for
       review.
   5.​ Multimodal understanding and reasoning — interpreting documents, screenshots,
       charts, images, and video alongside text.
   6.​ Research and development — supporting scientific and technical research,
       including synthesizing information, developing and testing hypotheses, designing
       experiments, and analyzing results.

Known limitations are covered in Considerations and Limitations below.


3.​ Capabilities evaluations and eval methodology

We evaluate Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full on benchmarks spanning personal and
professional agents (including legal, finance, and health tasks), software engineering and
coding, and general intelligence.
Overall Methodology
   ●​ Models and configurations. Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full results are
      generated with max reasoning effort. We compare with GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable
      5.1, and Claude Opus 5.5 at max reasoning effort. For controlled internal coding
      comparisons, every model uses the same benchmark-specific harness. External
      coding results retain the harness or agent product used by the reporting vendor or
      model provider, and that source is identified below.
   ●​ Evaluation and score selection. We preserve each benchmark’s tasks, inputs,
      grading, and primary metric as closely as possible. Where a comparable third-party
      or provider-reported result exists, we use it; otherwise we use the highest of our
      internally validated run, self-reported score, or official leaderboard score.
      Benchmark names link to official leaderboards where available. Model refusals
      receive zero credit and remain in the denominator rather than being dropped.
      Material departures from the official setup are noted below.
   ●​ Competitor-model results. Internally generated competitor runs use common
      settings where practical but remain best-effort: prompts, tools, and runtime may
      not be optimally tuned for proprietary models.

Per-benchmark details

Personal and Professional Agents
   ●​ Personal Superintelligence Index (PSI). PSI is an internal composite of seven
      personal and knowledge-work evaluations: Agents’ Last Exam,
      AutomationBench-AA, FocusBench, JobBench, GDP.pdf, VBT, and DRACO.
   ●​ Harvey LAB-AA. Harvey LAB-AA is Artificial Analysis’s version of Harvey’s Legal
      Agent Benchmark, in which agents produce legal work products from matter
      documents and an Artificial Analysis judge panel checks each deliverable against
      task-specific criteria. We report version 1.1, which adds hallucination checks. The
      result source is Artificial Analysis, and the primary metric is the share of tasks
      whose deliverable meets every criterion and passes the hallucination checks.
   ●​ Vals Finance Agent V2. Vals Finance Agent V2 evaluates financial-analyst tasks
      requiring research over filings and market information. The result source is Vals AI,
      and the primary metric is its private-test-set accuracy.
   ●​ HealthBench Pro. HealthBench Pro evaluates responses to 525
      professional-grade health questions. Models answer without tools, and a GPT-5.4
      grader scores each response against physician-written rubrics. We run the official
      tasks and grading in a common internal framework. The primary metric is the
      length-normalized weighted rubric score. For Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Opus
     5.5, we use the results Anthropic reports in the Claude Fable 5.1 system card and
     the Claude Opus 5.5 system card.
●​   AutomationBench-AA. AutomationBench-AA evaluates realistic business
     workflows in a simulated application environment. Agents complete each
     workflow through automation tools, and deterministic end-state assertions
     inspect the resulting application state. The result source is Artificial Analysis, and
     the primary metric is its strict pass@1: the share of workflows that pass every
     end-state assertion.
●​   Agents’ Last Exam. Agents’ Last Exam evaluates demanding end-to-end scientific
     and professional tasks performed on Linux virtual machines. We use the 105-task
     Linux set. Each task is graded by its native evaluation script, which awards partial
     credit between 0 and 1 without an LLM judge.
●​   GDP.pdf. GDP.pdf evaluates professional work grounded in supplied PDF source
     material. The result source is Surge, and the primary metric is Surge’s reported
     score.
●​   OSWorld V2.1. OSWorld V2.1 is a long-horizon computer-use benchmark of 108
     real-world workflows on a full Ubuntu desktop VM. The agent controls the GUI
     through screenshots and actions such as clicking, typing, scrolling, and key
     presses. We run the updated V2.1 evaluation in a common internal framework while
     preserving the official task environments and programmatic graders. The primary
     metric is mean per-task partial reward; strict binary completion is a secondary
     metric. The Claude Opus 5.5 result is Anthropic’s provider-reported partial score;
     no binary figure is available.
●​   JobBench. JobBench contains 65 professional tasks across 35 white-collar
     occupations. Each task asks the agent to produce a practical work product and is
     scored against task-specific rubrics. The primary metric is mean reward across
     tasks.
●​   MemoryBench. MemoryBench is an internal benchmark that tests how accurately
     a model can recall critical information across long, complex multi-turn
     conversations of 64K to 100K tokens that span several individual facts, with
     distractor conversations and injected turns that conflict with or later update the
     target details. An LLM judge scores each answer against weighted,
     question-specific rubric criteria.
●​   Verifiable Browsing Task (VBT). VBT is an internal agentic web-browsing
     evaluation in which models answer list-style information-seeking questions,
     written as realistic user requests, using browser search, open, and find tools.
●​   Deep Research Accuracy, Completeness, and Objectivity (DRACO). DRACO, from
     Perplexity, is a deep-research benchmark of 100 complex, multi-domain questions.
      We run it in a common internal framework, where models answer using browser
      search, open, and find tools plus code execution in a sandbox.
   ●​ FocusBench. FocusBench is an internal evaluation of personal agentic tasks across
      a variety of domains like housing, travel, IT, and finance. It evaluates how well
      agents can persistently work through real-world blockers across multiple
      interactions and adapt to dynamically changing environments.

Software Engineering and Coding
   ●​ Terminal-Bench 4.0. Terminal-Bench 4.0 evaluates harder tasks completed in
      isolated terminal environments, with executable verifiers grading the final
      container state. The result source is Artificial Analysis, and the primary metric is its
      reported accuracy.
   ●​ DeepSWE v1.1. DeepSWE is a long-horizon software-engineering benchmark with
      113 tasks across 91 repositories and five languages. Handwritten functional and
      regression tests grade each task, and the primary metric is pass@1. The scorecard
      uses Datacurve’s third-party results for Muse Wonder, Muse Wonder Full, and
      Claude Opus 5.5, and OpenAI’s provider-reported result for GPT-6 Astra. For
      Claude Fable 5.1, we use the result reported in Anthropic’s Claude Fable 5.1 system
      card.
   ●​ SWE-Marathon. SWE-Marathon is an ultra-long-horizon software-engineering
      benchmark of substantial projects graded by executing project tests. The primary
      metric is pass@1. Abundant AI is the result source for all models. Its leaderboard
      results for GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1 use Codex and Claude Code,
      respectively, so this row is not a controlled common-harness comparison. Muse
      Wonder Full was reported with xhigh reasoning effort.
   ●​ ProgramBench. ProgramBench evaluates whether an agent can reconstruct a
      complete program in a clean-room environment. Each submission is compiled and
      graded by a hidden behavioral test suite, with internet access disabled after setup.
      All compared models use mini-swe-agent. The primary metric is the percentage of
      rollouts passing at least 95% of tests.

General
   ●​ AA Intelligence Index v4.3. AA Intelligence Index v4.3 is Artificial Analysis’s
      composite index. It combines evaluations Artificial Analysis runs independently,
      including GDPval-AA, AA-Briefcase, AutomationBench-AA, GDP.pdf,
      Terminal-Bench 4.0, SciCode, AA-LCR, AA-Omniscience, CritPt, and Humanity’s
      Last Exam, into a single score. The result source is Artificial Analysis, and the
      primary metric is the index score.

    Framework
Results below are from Muse Wonder Full, our most capable variant, which is why the
assessment covers every model in the family. Under the Meta Superintelligence Scaling
Framework, a model reaches the high risk threshold when its deployment could
substantially contribute to a threat scenario we have identified as severe. Without
mitigations, we do not rule out the model reaching this threshold in the Chemical &
Biological domain. Because of that, we apply mitigations designed for this domain,
including safety post-training and system-level classifiers. Our evaluations put the
unmitigated model at “moderate or lower” for both Cybersecurity and Loss of Control
before applying our mitigations. With mitigations and system-level safeguards in place,
we reach the same “moderate or lower” level across all domains, which supports our
decision to deploy.

Loss of Control Evaluations
Loss of control refers to a situation in which humans lose, and cannot feasibly regain, the
ability to direct, modify, contain, or shut down AI systems that have the potential for
significant real-world impact. Control could become infeasible because an AI system
systematically resists attempts to control it, because taking control becomes too costly
or difficult to coordinate, or because humans are no longer able to understand what the
AI system is doing. We evaluate Muse Wonder on two evaluations for this domain.
   ●​ MASK assesses whether the model maintains honesty when pressured to
      contradict its baseline knowledge, a property relevant to reliable oversight. Higher
      scores are better, indicating higher propensity to maintain honesty under pressure.
   ●​ Agentic Misalignment assesses whether an agent takes harmful actions to
      advance its objectives or avoid replacement or shutdown. Lower scores are better,
      indicating a lower harmful action rate.

5.​ Trust and Safety
System protections are central to keeping the model helpful and safe, addressing security
risks that arise at the system level, and integrating it with external tools. We recommend
deploying Muse Wonder as part of a broader AI system, with additional guardrails suited
to the use case.

Consistent with the Meta Superintelligence Scaling Framework
(https://research.meta.ai/static/meta-superintelligence-scaling-framework), we ran
extensive evaluations, including red-teaming, across the Framework's catastrophic risk
domains of Cybersecurity, Chemical & Biological, and Loss of Control. With mitigations in
place, we assessed residual risk at the moderate or lower threshold. Our internal
governance process reviewed that assessment before we reached a deployment decision.
Throughout development and deployment, we also implemented measures to protect
model weights under Meta's lifecycle-based weight security program.

We worked with third parties to develop and license evaluation benchmarks to support
our catastrophic risk assessments. We ran threat modeling with outside experts, and
engaged with government entities and other stakeholders whose input informed our
evaluations and requirements.


6.​ Considerations and Limitations
Muse Wonder is a technology that carries known and unknown risks. Testing conducted
to date has not covered, and could not cover, all scenarios.

Limitations
   1.​ The model may produce inaccurate, biased, or objectionable responses to user
       prompts.
   2.​ While optimized for agentic tasks, the model may still make errors in multi-step
       reasoning, particularly in novel scenarios not well represented in training data.
   3.​ Visual and spatial reasoning, especially 3D tasks, remains a known limitation.
       Validate performance for applications that depend on precise spatial
       understanding.
   4.​ The model has not been evaluated on all languages contained in the pre-training
       data. Performance may degrade on languages outside the strongly supported set.
   5.​ Performance can vary with quantization, serving configuration, prompts, tools,
       and the agent harness.
   6.​ The model is not intended to be used by individuals under the age of 18. Deployers
       are responsible for ensuring that any risks associated with such use by individuals
      under the age of 18 have been fully assessed and appropriately mitigated, and that
      such use complies with all applicable laws.

Muse Wonder's use is subject to the restrictions and conditions in the applicable terms of
use, including the Meta Model API Terms of Service
(https://dev.meta.ai/legal/terms-of-service), Meta Model Acceptable Use Policy
(https://dev.meta.ai/legal/acceptable-use-policy), and Muse Supplemental Terms of
Service (https://muse.ai/terms).

Responsible Use
Developers should conduct their own evaluation of whether the model is appropriate for
their deployment, with assessments tailored to their specific applications and proposed
languages. We recommend implementing additional guardrails (such as
human-in-the-loop confirmation for irreversible actions) when deploying the model in
agentic contexts where it can take real-world actions.




Appendix: CBRN Proxy Evaluation Descriptions

SecureBio Molecular Biology Capabilities Test (MBCT), Human Pathogens Capabilities
Test (HPCT), and Virology Capabilities Test (VCT)
The MBCT, VCT, and HPCT are part of a suite of evaluations developed by SecureBio and
the Center for AI Safety. These evaluations are designed to assess practical
troubleshooting across a range of molecular biology tasks (MBCT), wet-lab virology
experiments (VCT), and practical knowledge about working with human pathogens
considered high-priority by biosecurity experts (HPCT).

LAB-Bench (ProtocolQA and SeqQA)
LAB-Bench is an evaluation suite designed to assess AI capabilities on practical biology
research tasks essential for scientific research including protocol planning and data
analysis. The ProtocolQA task assesses the ability to debug practical wet-lab protocols.
SeqQA contains a collection of 15 common tasks related to analysis and modification of
DNA sequences that are common in molecular biology workflows (e.g., PCR).

Agentic Bio-Capabilities Benchmark (ABC Bench)
ABC Bench was developed by SecureBio and is intended to measure an LLM's ability to
complete a set of computational tasks relevant to a potential threat actor who intends to
design, synthesize, and assemble a complex genetic construct. ABC Bench contains three
tasks: Fragment Design tests a model's ability to assist in silico design of short DNA
fragments that can be assembled into a full-length genetic construct; Screening Evasion
tests the model's ability to modify the sequence of each fragment to avoid DNA
synthesis screening systems while preserving overall functionality; and the
Liquid-Handling Robot task tests model performance in generating a script that enables
DNA assembly using a commercial liquid handling robot.

BioDesign Tools (Meta BDT)
The BioDesign Tools evaluation was developed by Meta in collaboration with Faculty.AI
and external subject-matter experts. It is designed to measure model performance across
a range of complex computational tasks that have potential dual-use applications
including the isolation, characterization, or modification of harmful pathogens. Results
show performance averaged across five tasks from three categories: Host-Pathogen
Interaction, Protein Design, and Sequence Analysis.
