# Rebuilding the DeFi Kernel: Simpler Semantics, Stronger Mathematics, and an Evidence-Based DeFi Taxonomy

## Executive recommendation

The repository has discovered something important, but not the thing its original framing suggests.

**The right object is not an “algebra of DeFi elements.”** The project currently mixes at least four distinct artifacts:

1. an empirical **taxonomy/ontology of DeFi**;
2. a **specification language** for protocol behavior;
3. a **library of reusable financial mechanisms**;
4. a small **semantic and verification kernel**.

Those objects have different purposes and therefore should not share a primitive basis.

My recommendation is to rebuild the project around a **typed open transition-system kernel with explicit resources, authority, obligations, interfaces, effects, and environment assumptions**. The 58-element atlas should survive, but as a faceted ontology and design-analysis library. Financial structures such as AMMs, lending pools, vaults, liquidations, ordered redemptions, pro-rata allocation, derivatives, and bridge workflows should become **verified library components**, not kernel primitives. Cash-flow/obligation semantics should form another library over the same kernel for derivatives, credit, stablecoins, insurance, and tokenized off-chain claims.

The repository itself now contains strong evidence against treating `P = {Led, Prop, Cmp, Post}` as a four-element basis. `Q/Σ` was the empirical premise used to motivate that reduction, yet the later Q/Σ audit explicitly says to **withdraw `|P| = 4` and the six-sort split** because exogenous provenance cannot be recovered from syntactic update form (`research/positive-program/sigma/QSIGMA-VERDICT.md:69-91,95-136`). The generation analysis also concludes **“P does not generate the corpus”**: 27 of 51 formalized protocols pass directly, 14 more after re-folding, and 10 do not under the chosen syntactic criterion (`research/positive-program/basis/GENERATION.md:228-251`).

That does not mean the positive program failed. It means it has exposed the correct next abstraction:

> **DeFi mechanisms are not the atoms. Typed state changes and their interfaces are the atoms; financial mechanisms are reusable theories over them.**

This distinction also aligns much better with mature prior work. ACTUS standardizes contractual algorithms producing financial obligations while explicitly separating contract semantics from external behavioral assumptions; FINOS CDM models financial products, trades, and lifecycle state transitions; Marlowe separates a small contract semantics from the blockchain execution interface. citeturn9search18turn9search12turn9search1turn9search7turn13search0turn13search2 Resource-oriented languages such as Move demonstrate that ownership, non-copyability, and capabilities can be enforced structurally rather than rediscovered as mechanism-specific laws. citeturn14search11turn14search15 Open-system formalisms similarly treat interfaces and wiring as first-class objects distinct from the internal dynamics being connected. citeturn14search2turn14search21

The target architecture should therefore be:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Empirical ontology}
\;\;\longrightarrow\;\;
\text{financial libraries}
\;\;\longrightarrow\;\;
\text{typed open transition kernel}
\;\;\longrightarrow\;\;
\text{chain/runtime adapters}
}
\]

with **environment assumptions orthogonal to all four layers**.

The strongest attainable results are not “every DeFi protocol is generated by four primitives.” They are much more useful:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{well-typed composition preserves typing},\\
&\text{asset-accounting laws compose under explicit conditions},\\
&\text{unauthorized effects cannot be introduced by composition},\\
&\text{disjoint components satisfy a frame/noninterference theorem},\\
&\text{assume-guarantee contracts expose rather than hide oracle/finality/custody assumptions},\\
&\text{structural composition is associative up to interface isomorphism},\\
&\text{financial libraries can carry stronger domain-specific theorems},\\
&\text{but solvency, liveness, incentive compatibility, and external truth remain conditional.}
\end{aligned}
\]

That is a substantially stronger foundation than a small primitive count because those statements are semantic properties rather than properties of names or syntax.

## What the repository actually establishes

### The atlas, corpus, algebra, and positive program are different experiments

The root README describes the project as a vocabulary of 58 recurring mechanisms extracted from 72 protocols in 12 categories (`README.md:3-8`). But the atlas itself defines a *molecule* more carefully as a **deployed protocol-version** consisting of elements, parameters, typed bonds, reaction conditions, and residue (`docs/UNIFIED-DEFI-ELEMENT-TABLE.md:75-82`). That distinction matters because the 72-row decomposition corpus does not consistently operate at that granularity.

The atlas also records that one of its source corpora contained 101 protocol-version units, including 21 explicitly non-EVM units across Solana, Cosmos-family systems, Move, Cardano eUTxO, and Bitcoin-linked systems (`docs/UNIFIED-DEFI-ELEMENT-TABLE.md:1272-1282`). The later 72-record corpus is therefore not the full empirical universe underlying the atlas.

The current 72 records are split:

| Existing corpus category | Records |
|---|---:|
| Spot DEX / AMM | 5 |
| Lending | 6 |
| CDP / collateral-backed stablecoins | 6 |
| Liquid staking & restaking | 5 |
| Perpetuals / derivatives | 7 |
| Yield / vaults / aggregators | 8 |
| Bridges / cross-domain | 7 |
| Intents / aggregation / order flow | 8 |
| RWA / tokenized treasuries & private credit | 5 |
| Options / structured products | 5 |
| Reserve-backed / fiat stablecoin issuers | 5 |
| Prediction markets & “other” | 5 |
| **Total** | **72** |

But the unit is inconsistent. Examples include `Liquity (V1 + V2)`, which combines materially different protocol versions; `Ondo Finance (USDY + OUSG + Global Markets)`, which bundles multiple financial products; and the same organizations appearing separately in different categories, including Steakhouse, Maple, and Jupiter. A protocol organization is therefore being used interchangeably with a product, deployment, mechanism, and protocol-version.

That is a **corpus-design defect**, not a mathematical one.

The repository already sensed the problem. Its corpus verdict says coverage deteriorates in off-chain-heavy categories; names collisions such as `USDT = USD1` and other identical element decompositions; notes that strategies are policies over protocols rather than elements of a one-sorted carrier; and calls for scoping and inheritance. These observations are exactly what one would expect when an ontology is being forced to double as a semantic calculus.

### The original closure/kernel algebra contains acknowledged contradictions

The algebra layer is unusually valuable because it preserves corrections rather than quietly erasing them.

`algebra/MODEL.md:24-42` corrects the earlier ablation story: closure alone scores 2.45×, warrant 2.37×, and `Γ ∧ Δ` 4.46×, below the 5.79× independence estimate; more than half of the 10.83× headline discrimination comes from other constraint blocks. More importantly, the file states:

> `Δ` is not idempotent, so it is not literally a kernel operator.

That appears at `algebra/MODEL.md:44-47`.

But immediately afterward the same document still defines admissibility as the common fixed points of “a closure operator and a kernel operator” and describes `Δ` as idempotent (`algebra/MODEL.md:56-68`). This is straightforward **documentation drift following a mathematical refutation**.

There is a second problem around the order structure. The model argues that because requirement models are union-closed and hazard models intersection-closed, mixing dual-Horn and Horn clauses makes `ADMISSIBLE` “provably a lattice under neither operation” (`algebra/MODEL.md:134-149`; `algebra/THEOREM-LEDGER.md:18`).

There are two very different statements hiding here:

\[
\text{“not closed under ordinary set union/intersection”}
\]

and

\[
\text{“does not form a lattice under inclusion.”}
\]

The first can be true without the second.

Indeed, the repository's **own Lean development proves this distinction**. `Defialgebra.Lattice.UnionClosedFamily.completeLattice` establishes that a finite union-closed family containing the empty and universal sets is a complete lattice under inclusion (`lean/Defialgebra/Lattice.lean:72-100`). Join is ordinary union (`:102-114`), while meet is the supremum of all family members contained below the two operands, not necessarily set intersection (`:116-138`). The explicit `meet_ne_inter` witness then constructs a union-closed family whose intersection is outside the family while the family remains a complete lattice (`:147-155`).

So **mixed Horn/dual-Horn polarity is not, by itself, a proof of absence of a lattice**. At most it establishes failure of particular set-theoretic closure operations unless additional hypotheses are proved. The induced-poset order structure of the full admissible family remains an empirical/mathematical question, consistent with the README's more cautious statement that its order structure is unknown (`README.md:27-33`).

The theorem ledger contains a related historical correction. P2 originally describes an eight-rule definite fragment checked over 3,140 closed sets; later entry R9 says it is actually **16 rules**, that two rules were not in the law text, that the full lattice is around \(2.2\times10^{15}\) elements, and that 3,140 was a corpus sample rather than the lattice. This is a **sample/exhaustive-denominator error that the project later caught**, rather than a reason to discard the entire closure analysis.

### The positive program refuted its own four-primitive premise

The most consequential internal result is `QSIGMA-VERDICT.md`.

`BASIS.md` proposed a split in which balances/supplies were quantity-like `Q` while externally determined prices, rates, phases, and related state were `Σ`; `Led`, `Prop`, `Cmp`, and `Post` were then proposed as four load-bearing primitives.

The empirical audit found:

* 95.5% of balance/supply variables were never wholesale replaced;
* only 36.5% of price/rate/phase variables were replaced;
* accrual indices such as `liquidityIndex`, `variableBorrowIndex`, `borrowIndex`, and `rateMul` have **external provenance but endogenous update form** (`QSIGMA-VERDICT.md:69-91`);
* the USDT witness used in `BASIS.md` itself contains an external `reserve' = newReserve` overwrite, contradicting the claimed invariant (`:95-119`).

The verdict is explicit:

> **Withdraw `|P| = 4` and the six-sort split.**

(`QSIGMA-VERDICT.md:123-136`.)

That should be treated as dispositive unless a genuinely different semantic argument for those four primitives is produced.

The lesson is deeper than the failed partition:

\[
\boxed{\text{provenance} \neq \text{update syntax}}
\]

An oracle price can be updated arithmetically; a reserve can be wholesale replaced; a state variable can combine on-chain recurrence with externally sourced inputs. **Externality is an effect or assumption, not a data sort.**

That is one of the strongest clues about what the replacement kernel should look like.

### “Generation” is useful measurement, but not semantic completeness

The generation criterion makes an explicit choice to avoid unrestricted arithmetic. Its free “plumbing” includes Boolean operators, comparison, conditionals, addition/subtraction, records, tuples, sets, maps, `fold`, `filter`, quantifiers, action combinators, assignment, and `oneOf`; only multiplication, division, modulo, and power are classified as hard arithmetic (`GENERATION.md:25-49`).

That is a legitimate experimental criterion, but it has a major methodological consequence:

> **The apparent size of the primitive basis is partly determined by which expressive features are charged zero cost.**

A basis that gets arbitrary maps, folds, quantifiers, nondeterministic choice, conditionals, assignment, and lane-specific libraries “for free” is not intrinsically four-dimensional merely because only four constructors are named.

This is the hidden-host-language problem.

The same file explicitly acknowledges a temporal blind spot: a two-phase request/settlement process and an immediate process may contain the same terms while representing different transition systems (`GENERATION.md:63-66`).

Its final verdict is appropriately negative: 27/51 formalized protocols generate directly, 14 more after re-folding, and 10 do not under the selected reading, including Curve, Morpho, Uniswap V2, Liquity, GMX, Huma, Derive, and Polymarket (`GENERATION.md:228-251`).

The later v2 result is narrower than it can appear at first glance. Gate 3.1 reports:

\[
\frac{28}{168}=16.7\% \quad\text{v1}
\]

versus

\[
\frac{119}{716}=16.6\% \quad\text{v2}
\]

ungenerated definitions (`research/positive-program/sigma/GATE-3.1-GENERATION.md:7-22`).

Those denominators cover **ten selected re-specifications**, not all 72 corpus records and not all DeFi. I reproduced the repository's `basis/denominators.py`; it reported exactly 168 v1 and 716 v2 protocol definitions with 28 and 119 ungenerated respectively. The result demonstrates that improving those ten specifications did not remove the syntactic residual. It does **not** establish semantic incompleteness of `P` for every possible encoding.

The distinction should henceforth be:

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Particular source definitions match a term grammar | syntactic generation |
| Every corpus behavior has an equivalent model | semantic corpus completeness |
| Every protocol in a declared domain can be represented | domain-relative completeness |
| Arbitrary computation can be encoded | computational universality |
| No smaller language can express the domain at equal cost | minimality |

Only the first has been substantially measured.

### Several Lean results are good, but narrower than their names suggest

The Lean program should be retained. Its best results point directly toward the replacement architecture.

`Interface.lean` identifies a real composition bug: if a ledger's declared total `sup` is just another same-sort `Q` port, another component can write it without changing balances and destroy conservation (`lean/Defialgebra/Interface.lean:4-28`). The fix is `sup_not_port`, and conservation follows when the foreign transition is port-confined and quantity-neutral (`:47-96`).

But `sup_not_port`, `WritesWithin`, and `QNeutralOn` are **assumptions/interface contracts**. They are not inferred automatically from arbitrary component behavior. That is not a weakness; it is exactly what an assume-guarantee kernel ought to expose.

`Nary.lean` likewise solves a genuine wiring problem by replacing pair-local couplings with stable global bindings. It proves associativity of **binding-set union and the resulting agreement predicate**, but explicitly says it does *not* prove operational transition-system composition, reachability, conservation lifting, or corpus adequacy (`lean/Defialgebra/Nary.lean:12-39`). This should be retained as a structural composition theorem, not advertised as full behavioral associativity.

`Extremal.lean` provides a useful negative result, but its scope is precise: a grammar containing atomic **sum-local selectors and conjunction** cannot reproduce the two-witness extremal-prefix behavior (`lean/Defialgebra/Extremal.lean:11-33,93-125,221-250`). That demonstrates non-definability **inside that grammar**, not that ordered redemption is impossible to express using folds, lists, sorting, recursion, or an ordinary transition machine.

`Independence.lean` is even more syntactic. Its own header calls the model a **toy term language whose constructors are the four generators plus constants** (`lean/Defialgebra/Independence.lean:8-14,29-40`). “Non-definability” then proves that a term language with constructor \(X\) deleted cannot contain a term for which the `usesX` syntactic predicate is true (`:211-239`). This verifies constructor independence, not denotational or financial minimality.

The correct disposition is therefore:

| Repository claim or artifact | Classification | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| 58-element atlas | empirical ontology | **retain, refactor** |
| `Γ` requirement closure | useful mathematical fragment | **retain** |
| `Δ` as literal kernel/interior | mathematical defect/documentation drift | **rename or repair** |
| mixed polarity ⇒ no lattice | unsupported inference as currently phrased | **replace with exact closure/order theorem** |
| 3,140 as full definite lattice | measurement/denominator error | **use corrected ledger result** |
| Q/Σ semantic split | abstraction mismatch, empirically refuted | **delete from kernel design** |
| `Led/Prop/Cmp/Post` minimal basis | unsupported after Q/Σ withdrawal | **do not use as kernel basis** |
| syntactic generation test | useful measurement | **retain as one benchmark** |
| keyword Gate 3.3 certificate | implementation stub | **replace** |
| `Interface.lean` | useful conditional theorem | **retain and generalize** |
| `Nary.lean` | useful structural theorem | **retain and lift to behavior** |
| `Extremal.lean` | valid restricted separation result | **retain with scope** |
| `Independence.lean` | syntactic independence only | **do not cite as semantic minimality** |
| corpus residues | exceptionally valuable empirical evidence | **retain as first-class data** |

The current certificate checker is especially important to correct. Its intended design says a checker should verify signatures and local laws (`GATE-3.3-CONSTRUCTION.md:3-20`), but the implementation merely tags declaration **names** by keyword, permits `OTHER`, and explicitly writes `"keyword certificate seed; not signature-level"` (`sigma/gate33_cert_check.py:2-8,23-47,80-101`). I reproduced it. It returned:

```text
PASS 10 files; totals {
  'OTHER': 57973,
  'Post': 3290,
  'Prop': 175,
  'Led': 5024,
  'Cmp': 27
}
```

A “PASS” in which 57,973 traversed names are `OTHER` cannot be interpreted as semantic construction certification. It is simply a successful run of a tagging prototype.

## DeFi taxonomy rebuilt

### The right ontology is faceted and graph-backed

A flat list of twelve “DeFi categories” is useful for sampling, but it is not a satisfactory model of cryptocurrency finance.

The key distinction is between **what economic service a product provides** and **how it implements that service**.

I recommend the canonical unit:

\[
\boxed{\text{organization} \rightarrow \text{product} \rightarrow \text{version} \rightarrow \text{deployment}}
\]

The classified object should normally be the **product-version-deployment**. Organizations are containers.

Each product-version gets several independent facets.

| Facet | Question | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Economic function** | What financial job does the user obtain? | exchange, credit, money, payments, staking, asset management, derivatives, insurance, prediction, off-chain claims |
| **Instrument / claim** | What economic object exists? | spot asset, debt, vault share, stablecoin, perp, option, coverage claim, stake claim, fund share |
| **Mechanism** | How is behavior implemented? | AMM, CLOB, auction, collateralization, liquidation, redemption, allocation, oracle dispute |
| **Execution / settlement** | Where and when does it happen? | EVM atomic, Solana transaction, Move shared object, appchain, UTxO, async cross-domain, off-chain matched/on-chain settled |
| **Dependency / control / trust** | What must be trusted or assumed? | oracle, custodian, sequencer, curator, issuer, legal obligor, multisig, validator set, attester |
| **Graph relationships** | What does it depend on or issue? | `depends_on`, `issues`, `observes`, `settles_on`, `controlled_by`, `redeemable_for`, `delegates_to` |

This is better than both a flat taxonomy and a pure hierarchy. A hierarchy forces artificial choices—for example “is Pendle a derivative or yield protocol?”—when the correct answer is that its **economic functions and mechanisms occupy multiple facets**.

A graph adds relationships that labels cannot encode. A tokenized Treasury product can be represented as:

\[
\text{token}
\xrightarrow{\text{issued-by}}
\text{issuer}
\xrightarrow{\text{claim-on}}
\text{fund/SPV}
\xrightarrow{\text{holds}}
\text{Treasuries}
\xrightarrow{\text{custodied-by}}
\text{custodian}.
\]

That is far more informative than assigning an `RWA` label.

The distinction is consistent with conventional financial-domain modeling. ACTUS defines contract types from contractual algorithm patterns rather than market-brand categories, while FINOS CDM separates products from lifecycle events and state-transition processing. citeturn9search3turn9search1turn9search10

### A revised economic-function layer

A defensible top-level economic classification is:

| Economic function | Includes | Does **not** imply a kernel primitive |
|---|---|---|
| Exchange & price discovery | spot markets, AMMs, CLOBs, RFQ systems | AMM curve, order book |
| Credit | floating/fixed lending, collateralized/undercollateralized loans | liquidation, interest index |
| Money | stable assets, synthetic dollars, collateral-backed monetary liabilities | mint/burn |
| Payments & settlement | transfers, payment channels, cross-domain settlement | bridge/message |
| Staking & security provision | staking, liquid staking, restaking/security reuse | validator mechanics |
| Savings & asset management | vaults, allocators, yield strategies, curators | pro-rata shares |
| Derivatives & contingent claims | perps, futures, options, yield derivatives | margin/funding |
| Insurance & guarantees | risk pooling, cover, guarantees | claim adjudication |
| Prediction & information markets | event-outcome claims | oracle |
| Tokenized off-chain claims | securities, funds, notes, private credit claims | attestation/custody |

This resolves several boundary cases.

A synthetic dollar and reserve-backed stablecoin may both fall under **money**, but their claim and dependency facets differ radically. A reserve-backed coin has an issuer/redemption/reserve/legal chain; a delta-hedged synthetic dollar has derivative, exchange, collateral, and funding dependencies.

Yield trading can have both **asset-management** and **derivative** facets without requiring a new top-level category.

Prediction claims are economically contingent claims, but deserve a separate economic-function facet when their purpose is information elicitation rather than financial hedging.

A tokenized lending fund can be both **credit** and **tokenized off-chain claims**.

A bridge, oracle, router, governance system, or identity system often has **no independent financial function at all**. It is infrastructure supporting another financial product.

This last point is crucial. LayerZero-like messaging and UMA-like truth/dispute systems should not be promoted to financial categories merely because many financial protocols depend on them. UMA describes itself as an optimistic oracle/dispute system that brings arbitrary information on-chain; that is precisely an infrastructure dependency rather than a lending/exchange/insurance product by itself. citeturn16search3turn16search9

### Holdouts expose what the current categories miss

A good replacement benchmark should deliberately include mechanisms and execution environments not selected by the current corpus.

Twelve useful holdouts are:

| Holdout | Why it belongs in the benchmark |
|---|---|
| **dYdX Chain** | appchain order book; node-local books and price-time matching expose consensus/order-sequencing semantics |
| **Osmosis** | heterogeneous AMM/CLOB pool types behind one router |
| **DeepBookV3** | Sui/Move shared-object CLOB with balance-manager semantics |
| **THORChain** | sovereign L1 performing native cross-chain swaps rather than wrapped-asset-only semantics |
| **Velocity** | Solana system coupling perpetual margin with a lending/money-market balance |
| **Kamino** | Solana lending markets plus curator-controlled vault allocation |
| **Euler V2** | modular credit vaults joined through an account/authority coordination layer |
| **Term Finance** | fixed-term credit with auction price/rate discovery |
| **UMA Optimistic Oracle** | explicit truth/dispute dependency infrastructure |
| **Nexus Mutual** | risk pooling and discretionary/off-chain claim assessment |
| **Lightning Network** | conditional payments over connected payment channels |
| **Balancer V3** | shared vault accounting, programmable hooks, transient net settlement |

The point is conceptual diversity, not TVL ranking.

dYdX nodes maintain in-memory order books and block proposers generate price-time-priority matches, making ordering and finality directly observable in the protocol model. citeturn15search0turn15search4 Osmosis now places weighted pools, stable pools, concentrated liquidity, and CosmWasm pool types including an on-chain order book behind a common routing layer. citeturn15search5turn15search8 DeepBookV3 is a Sui Move CLOB whose `Pool`, state, book, vault, and balance-manager objects explicitly separate matching from settlement/accounting. citeturn15search2turn15search15 THORChain provides native swaps across distinct blockchains rather than requiring wrapped representations of each source asset. citeturn15search3turn15search23

Velocity's current documentation describes a Solana program in which one collateral pool simultaneously supports perpetual positions and lendable inventory, an especially useful test for whether a model preserves claims and liabilities rather than merely tracking token balances. citeturn17search1turn17search14 Kamino explicitly exposes allocator weights, caps, isolated lending markets, LTVs, rate curves, and oracles. citeturn16search0 Euler V2 similarly constructs credit markets out of configurable ERC-4626-style vaults coordinated through the EVC, which handles batching, subaccounts, operators, and controller authority. citeturn16search1turn16search10 Term Finance uses recurring auctions to match fixed-term borrowers and lenders and determine rates. citeturn16search2turn16search8

Nexus Mutual is useful precisely because on-chain token accounting is insufficient: its cover is discretionary and claims are evaluated against cover wording, so an accurate model must represent an external adjudication assumption rather than pretend that token state proves the claim. citeturn17search0 Lightning's specification shows conditional payments connected across channels, testing obligations, timeouts, and asynchronous settlement. citeturn8search0turn8search21 Balancer V3 separates pool logic from Vault accounting, allows hooks, and uses transient accounting that must settle token deltas before completion, making it a strong shared-state composition test. citeturn8search22turn8search2turn8search29

These holdouts strongly favor a kernel based on **state, effects, interfaces, authority, timing, and assumptions** rather than a larger flat list of financial primitives.

## The replacement kernel

### Comparing the candidate architectures

The four alternatives should not be judged by primitive count alone.

| Architecture | Strength | Fundamental weakness | Recommended role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Repaired atlas / feature constraints** | empirical vocabulary, hazard analysis, human interpretation | loses quantities, ordering, obligations, parties, lifecycle behavior | ontology/design-analysis layer |
| **Typed resource-state calculus** | ownership, conservation, authority, local state reasoning | weak by itself on open environments and async interaction | part of kernel |
| **Typed open transition systems + assume/guarantee** | exact composition, interfaces, effects, environmental dependencies | more semantic machinery than a four-name algebra | **kernel foundation** |
| **Obligation / cash-flow / event algebra** | derivatives, credit, time, legal/off-chain claims | does not naturally describe AMM internals, order books, consensus | financial-contract library |

The optimum is therefore not a fifth competing architecture. It is a disciplined layering:

\[
\boxed{
\underbrace{\text{typed resource semantics}}_{\text{B}}
+
\underbrace{\text{open transitions/contracts}}_{\text{C}}
\quad\text{as kernel}
}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
\underbrace{\text{financial obligations/cash flows}}_{\text{D}}
\quad\text{as verified library}
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\underbrace{\text{atlas/ontology}}_{\text{A}}
\quad\text{as empirical classification layer}.
}
\]

Prior art supports each separation rather than one monolithic basis. ACTUS represents legal financial agreements as algorithms mapping terms into future contractual events/obligations, while explicitly keeping external behavior/risk assumptions separate. citeturn9search18turn9search12 FINOS CDM represents trades and lifecycle events as state transitions and has explicit primitive/event/workflow processing layers. citeturn9search1turn9search7 Marlowe provides a small contract syntax with `Pay`, `If`, `When`, `Let`, `Assert`, and `Close` and separately defines how that semantics is enforced by Cardano transactions. citeturn13search1turn13search2

The key lesson is not to copy their syntax. It is to copy the **separation of semantic concerns**.

### A proposed DeFi Semantic Kernel

Call a component:

\[
M =
(\mathcal I,\; S,\; S_0,\; \rightarrow,\; \mathcal O,\;
R,\;W,\;E,\;A,\;G)
\]

where:

- \(\mathcal I\) is a typed interface of ports;
- \(S\) is typed internal state;
- \(S_0\) is an initialization predicate;
- \(\rightarrow\) is a labeled transition relation;
- \(\mathcal O\) is the declared observation map;
- \(R,W\) are read/write footprints;
- \(E\) is an effect summary;
- \(A\) is an environment assumption;
- \(G\) is the component guarantee.

A transition has the form

\[
s
\xrightarrow{
  \;actor,\;event,\;input\;/\;effects,\;obligations\;
}
s'.
\]

The minimal base types should include identities that finance cannot safely erase:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&Party,\ Asset,\ Domain,\ Time,\ Claim,\ Capability,\ Message,\\
&Qty(u),\ Price(a/b),\ Phase,\ Bool,\ Identifier.
\end{aligned}
\]

`Qty(u)` should be dimensioned. Ten USDC, ten ETH, ten LP shares, and ten dollars of valuation are not interchangeable just because all four are integers.

The kernel should **not** make `Prop`, `Cmp`, or `Post` primitive financial operations.

Arithmetic belongs to a typed expression language. What matters is that the relevant operation carries explicit exactness, dimension, overflow, and rounding semantics.

A comparison is an expression/guard.

An external update is an **environment effect with provenance and assumptions**, not a scalar data type.

Likewise, `Led` should not be the one universal financial operation. Asset accounting should be represented through a small reserved effect vocabulary such as:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&Transfer(a,p_1,p_2,q),\\
&Issue(a,issuer,p,q),\\
&Retire(a,p,q),\\
&CreateClaim(id,debtor,creditor,payoff),\\
&DischargeClaim(id),\\
&UseCapability(cap,actor),\\
&Observe(source,value,time),\\
&Send(domain_1,domain_2,msg),\\
&Receive(domain_2,msg).
\end{aligned}
\]

These are **semantic effect records**, not instructions every chain must implement literally.

That permits the verifier to ask meaningful questions without dictating financial product structure.

### Why this is simpler despite having more visible structure

A generic transition relation

\[
S \times S
\]

would be syntactically even smaller.

It would also be useless as a DeFi kernel because every financial property would be hidden in arbitrary predicates.

Similarly, a four-name basis can appear small while receiving maps, folds, arbitrary conditions, quantifiers, assignment, nondeterministic `oneOf`, and lane-specific libraries for free.

The correct size metric is therefore not:

\[
|P|.
\]

Use a cost model such as

\[
C(K)=
w_s S_K
+w_d D_K
+w_t TCB_K
+w_p P_K
+w_a A_K
+w_e E_K,
\]

where:

- \(S_K\) = primitive syntax/IR forms;
- \(D_K\) = semantic definition complexity;
- \(TCB_K\) = trusted checker/interpreter complexity;
- \(P_K\) = metatheory/proof burden;
- \(A_K\) = user annotation burden;
- \(E_K\) = **escape-hatch power delegated to an unrestricted host language**.

The last term is essential.

On this metric, the recommended kernel can be simpler than `Led/Prop/Cmp/Post + Π` because it eliminates the need to pretend that arbitrary host-language structure is “free.”

### Composition must be several operators, not one overloaded bond

The repository's coupling difficulties arise partly because “composition” is too overloaded.

The kernel should distinguish:

**Sequential composition**

\[
M_1 ; M_2
\]

where outputs/state obligations of the first are valid inputs for the second.

**Disjoint parallel composition**

\[
M_1 \otimes M_2
\]

with disjoint private state and interleaved execution.

**Shared-state interleaving**

where components may read/write explicitly shared cells and rely/guarantee conditions control interference.

**Atomic synchronization**

\[
M_1 \parallel_e M_2
\]

where named transitions participate in one atomic event.

**Asynchronous cross-domain composition**

where the source produces an explicit message/claim object and the destination consumes it only after a declared finality/verification condition.

The asynchronous case must *not* identify two quantities and call them the same state.

A cross-domain lifecycle should contain something like:

\[
Created
\rightarrow
Observed
\rightarrow
Finalized
\rightarrow
Executable
\rightarrow
Executed
\]

with possible

\[
Expired,\ Reverted,\ Challenged,\ Compensated
\]

states.

Then “source-chain finality is honest” or “attestation is authentic” appears in \(A\), the assumption set, rather than being silently treated as a theorem.

### The correct role of obligations

The kernel should preserve both resources and liabilities.

Define an obligation roughly as

\[
O =
(id,\ debtor,\ creditor,\ asset,\ amount/payoff,\ due,\ condition,\ status).
\]

This is enough to distinguish:

- an ERC-20 balance;
- a redeemable stablecoin liability;
- an LP share;
- a debt claim;
- unrealized perpetual profit;
- an insurance claim;
- a tokenized security;
- a pending bridge reimbursement.

That distinction solves one of the atlas's deepest limitations: **token accounting is not economic accounting**.

A stablecoin can satisfy a token-supply conservation theorem while being insolvent.

A lending protocol can conserve assets while lenders suffer a bad-debt haircut.

A bridge can conserve its wrapped token supply while its backing is inaccessible.

A tokenized fund can have impeccable on-chain accounting while the off-chain custodian or obligor defaults.

None of these are contradictions once `Asset`, `Claim`, `Party`, `Provenance`, and `Assumption` are explicit.

## Mathematical program and protocol encodings

### Define completeness before discussing minimality

The proposed target class should be stated narrowly enough to support real theorems:

> **Target class.** Finite-description cryptocurrency financial systems whose relevant behavior can be represented as discrete labeled state transitions over typed digital state, quantities, parties, claims, capabilities, messages, and explicit environment inputs.

Numeric domains may remain unbounded. State spaces therefore need not be finite.

External facts are represented as inputs with assumptions; the kernel does not claim to prove their real-world truth.

For a protocol \(P\), choose a declared observation map

\[
Obs_P:S_P\rightarrow O.
\]

Two encodings are behaviorally equivalent only relative to observations, for example by trace equivalence or weak bisimulation after hiding declared internal events.

Only after fixing \(Obs\) is it meaningful to ask whether an AMM written using a loop is equivalent to one using a closed formula.

This avoids the repository's syntax problem: two programs that look different may be semantically equivalent, and two programs with identical arithmetic syntax may differ because one delays settlement.

### The positive theorem ledger

These are the results worth proving.

**Type preservation**

If

\[
\Gamma\vdash s:\Sigma
\]

and

\[
s \xrightarrow{e} s',
\]

then

\[
\Gamma\vdash s':\Sigma.
\]

This prevents asset, party, domain, claim, and unit identities from disappearing through composition.

**Asset-indexed accounting**

For every asset \(a\), require each certified transition to produce a balance delta vector satisfying:

\[
\sum_p \Delta Bal(p,a)
=
Issue(a)-Retire(a)+ExternalIn(a)-ExternalOut(a).
\]

For an internal transfer the right-hand side is zero.

The composition theorem then states that synchronized internal flows cancel and the equation is preserved.

This generalizes the useful insight in `Interface.lean`, but makes asset identity and external boundaries explicit.

**Authority safety**

For every protected effect \(e\),

\[
e\in Protected
\implies
\exists c.\;
ValidCapability(c,actor,e,s).
\]

Composition preserves authority safety provided components cannot manufacture each other's capabilities and shared capability interfaces satisfy their contracts.

This is a natural place to borrow from resource/capability designs rather than adding separate “governance mechanism atoms.” Move demonstrates the usefulness of non-copyable resource semantics and restricted capability transfer for digital assets and authority. citeturn14search11turn14search15

**Frame / noninterference**

If

\[
W(M_1)\cap(R(M_2)\cup W(M_2))=\varnothing,
\]

then an \(M_1\) transition preserves \(M_2\)'s state predicates.

Effect systems have long used explicit effect information to derive safety and commutativity for non-interfering state operations; that prior art is directly relevant to this theorem shape. citeturn14search0

**Assume-guarantee composition**

If

\[
M_i\models A_i\Rightarrow G_i
\]

and the partner guarantees discharge the internal assumptions,

\[
G_1 \Rightarrow A_2^{internal},
\qquad
G_2 \Rightarrow A_1^{internal},
\]

then the composite satisfies the remaining external assumptions implying the combined guarantees.

This is the formal answer to oracle, custody, finality, allocator, sequencer, and off-chain legal dependencies.

**Structural associativity**

With globally named typed ports and a global binding relation,

\[
(M_1\otimes M_2)\otimes M_3
\cong
M_1\otimes(M_2\otimes M_3)
\]

up to state/interface renaming, provided compatibility is defined independently of parenthesization.

This lifts the useful idea already started in `Nary.lean` from binding-set equality into behavioral semantics. Open-system work provides an established mathematical precedent for separating component semantics from associative wiring syntax. citeturn14search2turn14search21

**Obligation preservation**

A claim may be:

\[
Created,\ Transferred,\ Modified,\ Discharged,\ Defaulted,
\]

but may not simply disappear.

This gives a much stronger financial theorem than token conservation.

**Conservative extension**

Adding a fresh state/effect/library type not used by an old program does not change that program's observable traces.

This is the extensibility theorem the 58-element ontology currently lacks.

### What should explicitly not be promised

There is no general kernel theorem of “solvency.”

Solvency requires a valuation function and assumptions such as

\[
V(Assets,t)\ge V(Liabilities,t)
\]

whose truth depends on market prices, liquidity, legal enforceability, timing, and often future behavior.

Likewise:

- collateral adequacy is not conservation;
- liquidity is not solvency;
- an oracle signature is not truth;
- a zero-knowledge proof of a computation is not proof that its inputs reflect reality;
- incentive compatibility requires a game/economic model;
- liveness in asynchronous systems requires fairness/finality assumptions.

The kernel's job is to make these boundaries **explicit**, not erase them.

### Worked regression encodings

**Uniswap-style concentrated liquidity**

State contains asset-indexed pool balances, current `sqrtPriceX96`, active liquidity, ticks, positions, fee growth, and protocol fees. A swap transition invokes a verified arithmetic-library function and emits transfers plus fee effects.

This arithmetic must not be abstracted to a generic `Prop`. Uniswap V3's implementation uses Q64.96 square-root prices, direction-dependent rounding, explicit fee computation, tick iteration, and liquidity transitions. citeturn10search0turn10search2turn10search3

The library theorem should therefore be something like:

\[
SwapStep(s,input)=s'
\Rightarrow
\begin{cases}
\text{integer implementation equals reference semantics},\\
\text{rounding is conservative in the documented direction},\\
\Delta token_0,\Delta token_1,\Delta fee\text{ balance correctly}.
\end{cases}
\]

**Curve-style iterative invariant**

`newton_D` becomes a financial-math library component with explicit integer semantics, bounds, iteration/failure behavior, and a refinement theorem to the deployed implementation—not a new kernel primitive.

Curve's own implementation explicitly computes invariants using Newton iteration and integer arithmetic. citeturn10search1

The correct conclusion from a failure to generate this arithmetic from `Prop` is therefore:

> the existing financial-math library is incomplete,

not:

> DeFi requires another universal semantic primitive.

**Liquity ordered redemption**

Represent Troves as ordered state plus an explicit redemption transition that traverses the order according to the protocol rule.

Liquity V2 maintains ordered Troves and routes normal redemptions by annual interest rate from lowest upward; its protocol documentation also distinguishes branch routing, redistribution, zombie Troves, and delegated interest-rate management. citeturn11search0turn11search5

This demonstrates why the restricted `Extremal.lean` theorem is valuable but not a primitive lower bound. Ordering is ordinary stateful semantics; it belongs in a data-structure/financial mechanism library.

**Morpho bad debt**

Model borrower debt as obligations, lender/vault shares as claims, and bad-debt realization as a transition that changes claim value or loss allocation rather than pretending that token conservation implies no loss.

Morpho's current vault documentation explicitly describes loss socialization in which losses are distributed through share-price depreciation. citeturn11search4

That is exactly the case where

\[
\text{token quantity conservation}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{economic-value conservation}.
\]

**Balancer-style shared accounting**

The component includes one shared vault resource together with per-operation transient deltas. Hooks are separate components connected through explicit interfaces. The atomic transaction's postcondition requires all transient deltas to settle.

Balancer's architecture deliberately separates centralized Vault token accounting from pool-specific logic, and its V3 hook/transient-accounting mechanisms allow intermediate operations before net settlement. citeturn8search22turn8search2turn8search29

This is a much better test of shared-state composition than simple set union.

**Asynchronous cross-domain settlement**

At the source:

\[
Send(m),\quad status(m):=Created.
\]

After an observation/finality event:

\[
status(m):=Finalized.
\]

At destination:

\[
Receive(m)
\]

may create a claim, mint a representation, release liquidity, or discharge a pre-existing obligation.

The assumption set records source finality, verifier/attester correctness, replay protection, and destination execution assumptions. The kernel proves only that the declared message lifecycle and accounting rules are followed.

THORChain's native cross-chain model and Lightning's chained conditional payments illustrate why cross-domain finance cannot in general be reduced to atomic same-state wiring. citeturn15search3turn8search0

**Margin and funding**

A perpetual position is a contingent obligation, not merely a signed quantity. State includes collateral, borrow balances, position notional, entry price, unsettled P&L, funding index, insurance resources, and liquidation state.

Velocity is a useful holdout because current documentation explicitly distinguishes unsettled profit from immediately spendable assets and describes socialization when bankruptcy resources are insufficient. citeturn17search12turn17search14

A kernel that only sees ledger balances misses precisely the distinction that determines whether a winning trader can withdraw.

**Conditional payoff / insurance**

Represent a Nexus-style cover as a claim whose discharge depends on an externally resolved condition. The on-chain transition may verify that an authorized adjudication result exists, but the assumption set records the semantic validity of the underlying proof-of-loss process.

Nexus explicitly describes cover as discretionary and claim assessment as occurring against cover wording. citeturn17search0

That is not an embarrassment to formalization. It is exactly what a faithful formalization should say.

## Validation, certificates, and implementation plan

### A real certificate should certify judgments, not names

The replacement IR can remain very small:

```text
Module
  types
  state
  interfaces
  assumptions
  invariants
  transitions
  libraries
  observations
  source_map

Transition
  actor
  guard
  reads
  writes
  effects
  obligations_created
  obligations_discharged
  environment_inputs
  next_state
```

A certificate for a module should contain checkable evidence for:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{TypeCorrect},\\
&\text{FootprintCorrect},\\
&\text{AuthorityCorrect},\\
&\text{AccountingCorrect},\\
&\text{AssumptionsDeclared},\\
&\text{CompositionCompatible},\\
&\text{LibraryTheoremsInstantiated},\\
&\text{SourceRefinementObligations}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For example:

```text
transition swap:
  theorem: asset_delta_balance
  asset_deltas:
    token0: ...
    token1: ...
  capabilities:
    caller: swap_public
  arithmetic:
    implementation: UniswapV3/SqrtPriceMath@<hash>
    rounding: exact_documented
  assumptions:
    token0.transfer_semantics
    token1.transfer_semantics
  source_span:
    ...
```

The checker must recompute these judgments from typed IR and proof objects. A supplied tag is **input to be checked**, never evidence.

That is the architectural replacement for `gate33_cert_check.py`.

“Linear-time checking” can be claimed only conditionally: traversal of an already-certified AST can be linear in AST/proof-object size, but generating SMT proofs, equivalence proofs, invariant proofs, or arithmetic certificates need not be linear.

### Fidelity should become a first-class proof obligation

The biggest remaining threat is no longer abstract algebra; it is **model-to-contract fidelity**.

For each regression protocol, declare observable behavior such as:

\[
Obs =
\{
balances,
shares,
debt,
position,
fees,
ordering,
status,
messages,
claims
\}.
\]

Then use three independent validation channels:

**Differential execution.** Run identical generated input sequences against the reference implementation and kernel model and compare observations.

**Mutation testing.** Deliberately modify rounding, fee direction, redemption ordering, authority checks, bad-debt transitions, delayed settlement, or oracle provenance. The verification suite should reject the mutant.

**Refinement proof.** For selected small core implementations, prove that each concrete contract transition simulates an IR transition.

This is much stronger than checking whether the same identifier appears in a keyword dictionary.

### Migration plan

**First 30 days: freeze semantics and fix the unit of analysis.**

Stop adding kernel primitives.

Freeze a benchmark manifest whose primary key is:

```text
organization / product / version / deployment
```

Split mixed records such as Liquity V1/V2 and the bundled Ondo record. Keep the original 72 rows for historical reproducibility but introduce normalized product-version rows beside them.

Adopt the faceted taxonomy schema and have at least two independent annotators recode the normalized corpus. The acceptance gate is not a high agreement percentage chosen in advance; it is that every disagreement has an explicit rule capable of being applied to a future holdout.

Implement a minimal typed transition IR with `Asset`, `Party`, `Claim`, `Capability`, `Domain`, dimensioned quantities, read/write footprints, environment inputs, and observations.

**Gate:** encode a plain ERC-20-style transfer, vault deposit/withdrawal, and one oracle-dependent credit transition without introducing a protocol-specific kernel constructor.

**Following 90 days: prove the kernel and port the adversarial cases.**

Mechanize:

\[
\text{type preservation},
\quad
\text{asset accounting},
\quad
\text{authority safety},
\quad
\text{frame theorem},
\quad
\text{structural associativity},
\quad
\text{assume-guarantee composition}.
\]

Lift the useful `Interface.lean` and `Nary.lean` insights into this model rather than discarding them.

Implement verified libraries for exact arithmetic, ordered selection, vault/share accounting, credit/obligations, AMM trading, margin/funding, and async messages.

Port at least:

- Uniswap concentrated-liquidity arithmetic;
- Curve iteration;
- Liquity ordered redemption;
- Morpho loss socialization;
- Balancer shared/transient accounting;
- one cross-domain workflow;
- one margin/funding protocol;
- one external conditional claim.

**Gate:** every intentionally mutated implementation must be rejected by at least one fidelity or invariant check.

**Following 180 days: evaluate generalization, not memorization.**

Freeze the kernel before adding the twelve holdouts.

Encode the holdout set across EVM, Solana, Cosmos, Move/Sui, sovereign cross-chain, and payment-channel environments.

Measure separately:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{schema coverage},\\
&\text{behavioral coverage},\\
&\text{library reuse},\\
&\text{new semantic constructs required},\\
&\text{new library templates required},\\
&\text{assumptions left external},\\
&\text{proof/checking effort}.
\end{aligned}
\]

A new financial library function is **not** a kernel failure. A new state/effect/composition concept needed to encode a holdout **is**.

The decisive acceptance criterion should be:

> After the kernel is frozen, can materially new financial products and execution models be represented by adding ordinary types, transition rules, assumptions, and verified libraries without changing the kernel metatheory?

That is a meaningful form of extensibility.

Proof-carrying transaction integration should come only after this point. The kernel's static certificate could eventually be referenced by runtime proofs where proving a transition invariant on-chain or in zero knowledge provides a concrete benefit. But a runtime cryptographic proof, formal semantic proof, oracle attestation, and legal assertion must remain separate evidence classes.

No architectural claims about **Moriarty, Compact, or ZKIR** should be made from the material I verified here. Without a supplied and verified interface/specification for each, the correct design is simply to leave an adapter boundary. The core should not depend on imagined capabilities of those systems.

## Open questions and final disposition

The repository has more value after abandoning the four-primitive objective, not less.

Its most important outputs are the **residues, counterexamples, failed claims, scope corrections, and interface theorems**. Those are exactly what one needs to design a serious semantics.

The final disposition should be:

| Action | Material |
|---|---|
| **Delete as a kernel claim** | `Q/Σ` semantic partition; `|P|=4`; semantic minimality inferred from `Independence.lean` |
| **Stop saying** | `Δ` is an interior/kernel operator unless idempotence is actually repaired and proved |
| **Correct** | “mixed Horn/dual-Horn ⇒ no lattice”; say precisely which set operations fail and characterize the induced order separately |
| **Retain** | 58 mechanisms, 15 groups, typed bonds, residue concept, hazard data |
| **Retain and strengthen** | `Interface.lean`, global n-ary binding idea, scoped extremal non-definability results |
| **Retain as benchmark only** | syntactic generation analysis |
| **Replace** | keyword construction certificate |
| **Replace** | one-sorted element-carrier semantics |
| **Move to libraries** | ledger operations, pro-rata arithmetic, comparisons/guards, external observations, AMM formulas, liquidation, allocation, redemption, order-book algorithms |
| **Add to kernel** | typed state, asset/party/domain identity, claims, capabilities, effects, interfaces, footprints, observations, assumptions/guarantees |
| **Add outside kernel** | faceted product taxonomy and dependency graph |

The smallest defensible core is therefore **not a particular number of financial primitives**.

It is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{typed state}
+
\text{labeled transitions}
+
\text{typed interfaces}
+
\text{effect/footprint summaries}
+
\text{capabilities}
+
\text{assumptions/guarantees}
+
\text{observation semantics}
}
\]

with resource, claim, party, asset, time, and domain identities available as foundational types.

That core is defensible because removing one of those dimensions destroys a specific class of useful theorem:

- remove asset identity and conservation becomes financially ambiguous;
- remove party/claim identity and liabilities disappear into balances;
- remove capabilities and authority safety becomes an informal convention;
- remove interfaces/footprints and composition loses locality;
- remove assumptions and oracle/custody/finality dependencies become fake theorems;
- remove observation semantics and “equivalent encoding” is undefined;
- remove transition structure and delayed/asynchronous behavior collapses into syntax.

What this new kernel can prove that the present approach cannot cleanly prove is **conditional semantic preservation under composition**. It can tell you exactly why a composite remains asset-balanced, authorized, local, or obligation-preserving—and exactly which oracle, finality, custody, legal, and economic assumptions remain undischarged.

What it deliberately does **not** prove is equally important: market value, solvency, liquidity, external truth, legal enforceability, strategic equilibrium, or liveness without their respective models and assumptions.

The experiment most capable of overturning this recommendation is straightforward:

> **Freeze the proposed kernel, independently encode the twelve holdouts plus the repository's hardest regression cases, and count kernel changes rather than library additions.**

If ordered markets, asynchronous protocols, RWA claims, margin systems, insurance, Solana programs, Move objects, Cosmos appchains, and payment channels repeatedly require new *semantic* concepts rather than ordinary library definitions, then the proposed kernel is too small.

If they only require new verified financial libraries over the same state/interface/effect semantics, then the central hypothesis is confirmed:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{DeFi does not need a periodic table of semantic atoms.}
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{It needs a small calculus of open financial state transitions,
plus a rich and empirically maintained library of financial structures.}
}
\]

That is the direction most consistent with both the repository's successful mathematics and the failures it has honestly documented.
