Model Information
Description
Gemini 4 is the next generation in the Gemini series, a suite of highly-capable, natively multimodal, reasoning models. Gemini 4 Argon delivers frontier performance to help in complex workflows across real-world software engineering, enterprise knowledge work like legal and finance, and cybersecurity defense.
Model dependencies
Gemini 4 Argon, a new frontier model, is not a modification or a fine-tune of a prior model.
Inputs
Text strings (e.g., a question, a prompt, document(s) to be summarized), images, audio, and video files, with a combined input-output token context window of up to 1M.
Outputs
Text, with up to a 1M token input + output.
Architecture
Gemini 4 Argon is a large scale sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) (

Vaswani et al., 2017
) with native multimodal support for text, vision, and audio inputs.
Model Data
Training Dataset
The pre-training dataset was a large-scale, diverse collection of data encompassing a wide range of domains and modalities, which included publicly-available web-documents, text, code, images, audio (including speech and other audio types) and video. The post-training dataset included different types of instruction tuning data, reinforcement learning data, and human-preference data. Gemini 4 Argon was trained using reinforcement learning techniques that can leverage multi-step reasoning, problem-solving and theorem-proving data.
The training dataset also includes: publicly available datasets that are readily downloadable; data obtained by crawlers; licensed data accessed via commercial agreements; user data (i.e., data collected from users of Google products and services to train models, along with user interactions with the model) in accordance with Google’s relevant terms of service, privacy policy, service-specific policies, and pursuant to user controls, where appropriate; other datasets that Google acquires or generates in the course of its business operations, or directly from its workforce; and generated synthetic data.
Training Data Processing
Data filtering and preprocessing included techniques such as deduplication, honoring robots.txt, safety filtering in-line with
Google's commitment to advancing AI safely and responsibly
, and quality filtering to mitigate risks and improve training data reliability. This process involves, on a case-by-case basis, filtering harmful content, text, and other modalities, including filtering content that is pornographic, violent, or violative of child sexual abuse material (CSAM) laws.
Implementation and Sustainability
Hardware
Gemini 4 Argon was trained using
Google’s Tensor Processing Units
(TPUs). TPUs are specifically designed to handle the massive computations involved in training LLMs and can speed up training considerably compared to CPUs. TPUs often come with large amounts of high-bandwidth memory, allowing for the handling of large models and batch sizes during training, which can lead to better model quality. TPU Pods (large clusters of TPUs) also provide a scalable solution for handling the growing complexity of large foundation models. Training can be distributed across multiple TPU devices for faster and more efficient processing.
The efficiencies gained through the use of TPUs are aligned with Google's
commitment to operate sustainably
.

Intended Usage and Limitations
Benefit and Intended Usage
Gemini 4 Argon is our latest frontier model, excels at deep work in complex, real-world tasks across domains including coding, finance, legal, cybersecurity, and science.
Argon combines frontier reasoning, deep visual perception, expanded domain knowledge, and stronger writing capabilities to help with technical and enterprise workflows.
It is particularly well-suited for applications that require:
multi-turn deep investigations
complex engineering tasks
deep visual understanding
cybersecurity defense
multimodal document analysis and synthesis
deep domain knowledge and strong writing capabilities (e.g. legal and financial analysis, science)
scientific or mathematical reasoning
Known Limitations
Gemini 4 Argon may exhibit some of the general limitations of foundation models, such as hallucinations. There may also be occasional slowness or timeout issues. The knowledge cutoff for Gemini 4 is March 2026, but in some domains the model’s knowledge may be less up to date.
Acceptable Usage
Google’s Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy
applies to uses of the model in accordance with the applicable terms of service. Additionally, the model should not be integrated into certain systems (also found in
Google’s Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy
), including those that: (1) engage in dangerous or illicit activities, or otherwise violate applicable laws or regulations, (2) compromise the security of others’ or Google’s services, (3) engage in sexually explicit, violent, hateful, or harmful activities, (4) engage in misinformation, misrepresentation, or misleading activities.

Evaluation Approach
Gemini 4 Argon was developed in partnership with internal safety, security, and responsibility teams. A range of evaluations and red teaming activities were conducted to help improve the model and inform decision-making. These evaluations and activities align with
Google's AI Principles
and
responsible AI approach
, as well as Google's Generative AI policies (e.g.
Gen AI Prohibited Use Policy
and the
Gemini API Additional Terms of Service
).
Evaluation types included but were not limited to:
Training/Development Evaluations
including automated and human evaluations carried out continuously throughout and after the model’s training, to monitor its progress and performance;
Human Red Teaming
conducted by specialist teams who sit outside of the model development team, across the policies and desiderata, deliberately trying to spot weaknesses and ensure the model adheres to safety policies and desired outcomes;
Automated Red Teaming
to dynamically evaluate Gemini for safety and security considerations at scale, complementing human red teaming and static evaluations;
Ethics & Safety Reviews
were conducted ahead of the model’s release
In addition, we perform testing following the guidelines in
Google DeepMind’s Frontier Safety Framework
(FSF).
Safety Policies
Gemini’s safety policies aim to prevent our models from generating harmful content, including:
Content related to child sexual abuse material and exploitation
Dangerous content (e.g., promoting suicide, or instructing in activities that could cause real-world harm)
Sexually explicit content
Hate & harassment (e.g., dehumanizing members of protected groups)
Medical advice and information that runs contrary to scientific or medical consensus

Human Red Teaming Results
We conduct manual red teaming by specialist teams who sit outside of the model development team in Google’s Trust & Safety organization. High-level findings are fed back to the model team. For child safety evaluations, Gemini 4 Argon satisfied required launch thresholds, which were developed by expert teams to protect children online and meet
Google’s commitments to child safety
across our models and Google products. For content safety policies generally, including child safety, we saw similar or improved safety performance compared to Gemini 3.1 Pro. Additionally, the scope of red teaming covered potential issues outside of our strict policies, compared performance to Gemini 3.1 Pro, and found no egregious concerns.
Frontier Safety Assessment
In line with our
Frontier Safety Framework
(April 2026), we conducted critical capability assessments of Gemini 4 Argon to understand the inherent risk of the model, that is, whether a model may,
without
appropriate mitigations, contribute to the risks identified across the four risk domains. The results of the critical capability assessments, as measured against the relevant T/CCLs set out in our FSF, are summarized in the table below.
As outlined in our FSF, models reaching TCLs or CCLs will be subject to residual risk assessments to evaluate whether the deployment mitigations have brought the residual risk to an acceptable level. For models reaching CCLs, the residual risk assessment will be informed by a supplemental safety case outlining the mitigations.
For Gemini 4 Argon, drawing on internal and external results, the inherent capability thresholds that the model may have reached and our response are as follows:
CBRN CCL:
We cannot rule out that Gemini 4 Argon has reached this CCL for biological information risk, and we therefore developed a safety case for its biorisk capabilities that we assess lower risk to below the biological CCL level.
Cybersecurity CCL:
We cannot rule out that Gemini 4 Argon has reached this CCL, and we therefore developed a safety case for its cyberoffensive capabilities that we assess lower risk to below the cybersecurity CCL level.
Stealth and Situational Awareness TCL:
We cannot rule out that Gemini 4 Argon has reached this TCL, and we therefore performed a residual risk assessment for this risk that we assess lower risk to below the TCL level.
We continually work to improve the coverage and robustness of our frontier safety safeguards. The relevant safety cases and residual risk assessments outline the mitigations we have put in place.
