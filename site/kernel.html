Moriarty and cross-chain settlement

The Federated DeFi Kernel

The Federated DeFi Kernel coordinates financial agreements across chains and services. Moriarty gives those agreements a precise meaning: what the owner authorizes, what each participant owes, and what must hold before settlement. The kernel carries that meaning through the rest of the work, from the search for a route to the completion or recovery of each stage, while the signed agreement continues to govern every use of the owner’s assets.

Moriarty is a bounded financial language for Midnight. Its programs compile to ZKIRv3 for execution through Midnight’s native proof infrastructure. Developers can author, compile, prove and deploy programs without approval from the project or a hosted service, subject to Midnight’s validity rules, resource costs and asset-owner authorization. Because execution is finite and every financial effect is explicit, a program’s complete behavior can be stated and proven.

The owner’s signed intention specifies which assets may leave an account, where value must arrive, the permitted fees and liabilities, and the evidence required before a stage proceeds. It also says which duties survive partial settlement or failure. These conditions give a solver room to choose how to fulfill an agreement while preserving what the participants authorized. The language reference defines the syntax and semantics, and the requirements reference records the obligations that the language and its backend must satisfy.

An agreement settled entirely on Midnight can use the ledger’s own proof and state-transition rules. Cross-chain agreements also depend on events beyond that ledger: a transfer elsewhere, a document attested by an issuer, or another participant’s signature. Each event has its own timing and evidence, so a delay can leave an agreement partly fulfilled without establishing that the remaining work failed.

The kernel coordinates this continuing work. It gathers candidate routes, collects evidence, reserves spending authority, submits effects and reconciles observations with the agreement. Its recovery paths carry forward the costs already incurred and the duties still owed. This coordination lets an agreement span independently settling systems without treating them as a single atomic transaction.

Using the kernel is optional. Direct Midnight execution avoids federation dependencies for local agreements; the federated path reaches other chains and services through explicitly chosen evidence, custody and signing arrangements. Those arrangements add trust and availability assumptions that the agreement must account for. They confer no authority to change its signed constraints, and a federation’s willingness to serve an agreement is never a condition of using Moriarty.

Consider a buyer exchanging A for B under conditional settlement with programmable escrow. The buyer permits partial fills, but payment requires the provider’s signature, the recipient’s acceptance and a fresh document attestation. What the buyer fixes and what the solver chooses

The buyer fixes the assets, their settlement domains, the recipient and the financial limits, while leaving the choice of route, venue and intermediary to the solver within those constraints. A solver can improve the route without asking the buyer to authorize every implementation detail. It cannot substitute another recipient or acquire additional spending authority by finding a more convenient path.

The funding line is there because the accounting has to say what counts. Moving 11 A into escrow is a precondition of the illustration rather than one of its effects, so the agreement excludes it from the gross cap explicitly; left unstated, the same 11 A could be counted twice, or not at all.

Why the account keeps its quantities apart

The gross cap of 11 A limits cumulative debit, including fees. The separate 1 A fee cap limits the portion spent on fees, so each fee consumes capacity under both bounds. A later return of principal is a separate effect; it does not erase the earlier debit or replenish fees already spent.

Reservations account for exposure before an attempt’s outcome is known. Reserving capacity prevents another attempt from using the same authority while the first remains unresolved. A reservation records a commitment against the buyer’s authority and says nothing about where the assets are; that question is answered by the custody record and by authenticated observations.

On the receiving side, confirmed receipt counts only B that the recipient authentically holds, which is a different fact from B that a destination reports having sent. Custody is the last confirmed, accounted balance of the buyer’s A under escrow. While an external attempt is unresolved, the current location of the amount it covers is unknown, because the destination may already have executed; the custody figure is a record, not a live reading.

The account also carries accepted duties: what a consenting party still owes and how each duty is discharged. Duties survive partial progress, so delivering the first half of an order does not shrink the obligation to deliver the second, and a failed attempt turns a conditional remedy from dormant to pending instead of making it vanish.

After the first fill, 5.5 A has been spent and 10 B received. Reserving another 5.5 A for the second fill brings spent plus reserved to the 11 A cap. A competing solver therefore has no remaining capacity to reserve, even while the second transfer’s outcome is unknown.

Who does what

Where each responsibility sits

The owner authorizes the agreement, Moriarty defines its financial rules, and the kernel coordinates its execution. Midnight and the external systems enforce conditions within their respective domains. Their different responsibilities determine where each condition must be checked.

The CAKE framework separates chain abstraction into Applications, Permission, Solvers and Settlement, and the responsibilities above fall along the same lines: the application expresses the agreement, permission is the asset owner’s and the application’s authority, solvers propose executions, and settlement systems enforce the rules they accept. A federation can coordinate these activities without becoming a language-wide deployment authority.

Moriarty expresses the evidence an agreement requires, while issuers and settlement systems provide observations under their own authority. A document predicate can establish that a named issuer attested a specified document; physical delivery requires evidence appropriate to that separate claim. Similarly, transaction inclusion, successful execution, finality and receipt by the intended recipient answer different questions. The kernel reconciles those observations against the agreement, and Midnight enforces the native proof relation for each stage together with its own state-consumption rules. Foreign effects remain subject to the destination’s enforcement and the adapter’s evidence assumptions.

How the federation shares responsibility

The federation is a configured membership of operators that shares the work and authority of coordination. Where it provides threshold custody, members hold signing shares and combine them under the signing protocol to authorize an external effect. A membership epoch identifies the operator set and threshold to which that signature belongs. The protocol also specifies how operators order work, handle conflicting statements, respond to unavailable members and change membership without losing the agreement’s outstanding duties.

Solvers supply candidate executions to this coordination system, and proposing a route confers no signing share. Within the federation, ZK proofs bind execution to the agreement, MPC distributes signing authority among the members, and TEE attestations identify the measured environments in which configured work ran; each contributes a distinct assurance under its own assumptions.

What acceptance has to establish

Acceptance binds the execution to the agreement and its history. The required claims address different ways a transaction could violate the participants’ intention, so satisfying one cannot compensate for failing another:

  1. Contract properties. The agreement’s required properties hold over their stated domain: the named recipient, the gross and fee caps, the completion goal.
  2. Intent refinement. The chosen execution stays within the owner’s authenticated authorization. A committed route may do less than the intention allows, never more or other.
  3. Transition validity. The next state and its financial effects follow the program’s rules: each outcome, including failure, retains exactly the effects and duties its phase policy specifies.
  4. History compliance. The stage extends a compliant history from a legitimate origin: the identifiers it consumes were unconsumed, and its predecessors were themselves accepted.

Acceptance depends on these claims rather than on who assembled the transaction. The compiler must preserve them from the developer’s source program through Core semantics into the native constraints Midnight verifies. This correspondence gives the proof its financial meaning: the verified relation must be the agreement the developer expressed, with the owner’s authorization bound into it.

How these map onto the fixture

The explorer’s candidate checks illustrate contract properties and intent refinement. Its account changes illustrate transition validity, while the consumed identifiers and predecessor records illustrate history compliance. In the language, each obligation has an enforcement point in native constraints, authenticated state or a justified ledger mechanism.

When an outcome is unknown

Waiting on an external result

The second fill has been submitted to another chain, its deadline has passed, and nothing has come back. What the agreement may do next depends entirely on what it later learns, and the design refuses to let elapsed time count as learning something.

A deadline is a fact about the agreement, not about the other chain. When it passes, the set of transitions that may now be attempted changes: reconciliation may begin, a recovery path may become eligible, a solver may be released from further work. What the deadline does not do is establish that the destination failed to execute. The transfer may have been included and finalized a moment after the last observation, or it may be sitting in a queue, or it may never have left. Until an authenticated observation arrives, the reservation stays exactly where it was, the buyer’s remaining authority stays encumbered by an attempt that may yet land, and the custody figure remains the last confirmed balance, which says nothing about where the reserved 5.5 A is now.

Suppose the destination eventually reports an authenticated success. The reservation becomes an actual debit, the receipt of 10 B is confirmed, and the attempt’s identifier is consumed, so a second copy of the same result cannot pay twice and no later report of failure can release the debit or make the receipt unhappen. That much is accounting, and it happens as soon as the result is authenticated.

Whether the fill is also an accepted stage of the agreement is a separate judgment. The predicates that release a fill can change between submission and acceptance: an attestation that held when the attempt went out can expire, or become unavailable, by the time the acceptance check runs. If the document attestation or another predicate is missing at that moment, the external result is still accounted and its identifier still consumed, because the money really moved, but the fill is not accepted and the delivery duty stays open. Restoring the missing predicate later reconciles the same observed result exactly once, with no new debit and no new receipt. The account records what happened in the world; acceptance records whether what happened satisfied the agreement, and the two are allowed to disagree for a while.

Suppose instead the destination reports an authenticated failure. This fixture’s evidence policy treats that report as establishing that the principal did not execute, which is more than every adapter can promise: where an adapter cannot, a failure report by itself would not establish that the principal never left, and the reservation would have to stay. Here it does establish nonexecution, so the reservation releases and the buyer’s account shows 6 A gross with 1 A of it fees. The fee the destination retained is real spending. It counts against the gross cap and exhausts the fee cap, which is why the specified second fill cannot be retried: 5 A of gross capacity remains, but its 0.5 A fee would exceed the exhausted fee cap. The remedy duty the provider accepted at the start is now pending rather than discharged, and only an authenticated remedy transition discharges it; on the successful branch the same conditional duty is discharged by failure-free completion, because no remedy ever became due. The fixture specifies the duty and its discharge rule but no remedy payout amount, so the failure branch records the duty without issuing a refund. An executable remedy must specify its asset, amount and release conditions in the signed terms.

Suppose, finally, that reconciliation finds nothing. Then nothing changes, and that is the correct result. The buyer’s remaining authority is still reserved against an attempt that may yet land or may already have executed; the escrow figure is still the last confirmed balance; reconciliation may continue. A refund at this point would be an invented recovery. A recovery grant alone does not make one safe, because a remedy needs signed authority, authenticated evidence, assets actually under control, remaining resources and an exclusive consumption rule, and an elapsed deadline supplies none of them. In the explorer, a refund attempted on the strength of a timeout and a recovery signature is refused for exactly that reason.

In none of these endings does the first fill come back. It was an accepted prefix, its debit and fee are permanent, and whatever happens to the second attempt cannot unwind it. A compensating payment or a cancellation is a new authorized action with its own costs, and calling it a rollback would misdescribe both the ledger and the agreement.

What each form of evidence establishes

Evidence under its own assumptions

Zero knowledge, threshold signing and attested hardware establish different facts. Their value depends on what each checks and where the resulting evidence is enforced.

The agreement releases a fill only when the provider has signed, the recipient has accepted and a fresh attestation of the shipping document has arrived from the authorized issuer. The attestation shows that a document satisfied a named predicate at a named freshness; it does not show that the document is legally true or that goods were delivered. The explorer treats a missing attestation as unknown, never as false, and an attestation in an unsupported format as establishing nothing either way, because a predicate that has not been evaluated is a different state from one that failed.

A zero-knowledge proof establishes that an encoded relation holds for the public statement it is bound to, and, under the proof system’s separate zero-knowledge assumptions, discloses no witness information beyond that statement and its intended disclosure. That is a strong claim about the relation and a narrow claim about privacy: public effects, metadata and authorized outputs remain visible as specified, and a valid proof hands nobody the witness the next stage will need.

A threshold signature establishes that a signature over exactly these bytes verifies under the group key for the bound membership epoch. A threshold such as the inspector’s illustrative 3-of-5 counts signing shares, and a share can belong to a corrupt participant. With t required shares and at most f corrupt, a valid signature needs at least t − f honest shares under the protocol’s assumptions, and that those honest participants checked the policy before signing is a further assumption about their behavior, because the signature itself records no such check.

A TEE attestation establishes that a report signed under the vendor root identifies the measured code and configuration that produced it, together with whatever report data the enclave bound. Any claim that this code ran on this input and produced this output depends on the measured code binding its input and output into that report, on the attestation being fresh for this epoch and stage, on sealed state not having been rolled back, and on trusting both the vendor root and the measured code itself.

Because each mechanism establishes something different, combining them helps only when they all speak to the same statement. Every mechanism has to bind the same signed intention, program identity, settlement domain, stage, epoch and complete effects, or evidence for one intention can be presented for another, a proof of a weaker relation can stand in for the pinned one, and a prover can bind only the flattering part of what happened. Even a common statement is not enough on its own; it has to be enforced at the point where a stage is accepted. Where one operator holds the keys, hardware or code behind more than one mechanism, those checks share a failure and are correlated dependencies rather than multiplied protection. Where the destination decides

A foreign account that accepts a threshold signature alone honors any transfer the threshold signs. If the illustrative 3-of-5 signer set is compromised, that destination accepts transfers that bypass the honest signers’ policy checks. Midnight can refuse to accept such a transfer as a stage of the agreement, but that refusal cannot reverse the foreign transfer.

A destination that also verifies a named relation and enforces a consumption rule narrows this exposure to the extent of the checks it performs. Its adapter must identify those checks precisely, including the bytes and identities they bind. The agreement can then rely on the destination’s actual enforcement rather than treating every external transfer as if Midnight governed it.

People and AI solvers

The same authority, whoever proposes the route

An AI solver searches for executions that satisfy the agreement using the same bounded authority as any other solver. Delegation determines which operations it can request, while the agreement continues to govern spending, recipients, evidence and settlement.

The Open Wallet Standard (OWS) gives solvers a common interface for wallet operations. Moriarty’s authorization rules limit the assets, destinations, amounts and operations that a delegation exposes through that interface. A solver therefore receives the authority needed for its assignment without acquiring an unrestricted signing key. The explorer’s proposer label leaves these constraints unchanged: choosing a human or AI proposer does not alter the financial agreement.

A paid service request adds another settlement obligation. An x402 payment receipt establishes payment for the request, while result availability and delivery to the authorized recipient require their own evidence. If a connection drops, reconciliation uses the existing request identity to find the result already paid for. A further charge requires fresh authorization; a retry cannot silently turn the same request into another purchase.

A fixed request, retried once
  1. request R1 authorized Scoped to one paid query, one price, one recipient. Its logical identity is fixed.
  2. payment observed The x402 receipt for R1 is authenticated. The request is paid for and not yet delivered.
  3. connection drops Whether the result is available, and whether it was delivered, are both unknown.
  4. retry, same R1 Reconciliation asks for the result of R1. It does not authorize a second charge.
  5. result delivered Delivery to the authorized recipient is recorded against R1: one request, one payment, one delivery.
How a continuation carries history

After the first fill, the agreement is a continuation. It names its accepted predecessors, the duties that remain, the identifiers already consumed and the next stage it may take. Whoever takes that next stage needs the private witness it depends on, and a valid proof of the previous stage does not hand the next participant that witness.

Continuation proofs carry compliance across stages while each stage’s execution and its predecessor fan-in stay bounded. Two stages may share an ancestor, but no claim may be consumed twice. The history itself can keep growing, one bounded stage at a time, within the work and authority limits its signed episode declares, and a successor episode continues it without resetting a lifetime budget the owner already signed; the remaining duties and consumed identifiers travel with it.
