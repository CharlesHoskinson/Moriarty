The Federated DeFi Kernel

You define the agreement. Solvers find possible routes. The kernel coordinates the work across participants and chains, while each accepted stage must satisfy the agreement.

Most of that work happens in more than one transaction. A first delivery can land while a second is still crossing another chain, and the agreement has to say what is spent, what is merely reserved, what has arrived and who still owes what. This page walks one such agreement through its good, bad and unresolved endings. The examples sign nothing and submit nothing. The kernel and its integrations remain development work, and a Moriarty program can also run directly on Midnight without any federation.

B is the token issued by fixture-issuer-b on foreign-illustration, reference asset-b-ref-01. The same symbol on a different domain or from a different issuer would be a different asset. Nothing here is a market price.

Outcome, not route

The buyer has not said which venue to use or which chain to cross. They have said what they will accept. A solver proposes the route, and every route is measured against the same signed constraints.

Six quantities kept apart

  • Actual gross debit. Left the buyer for good.
  • Incurred fees. Part of the debit, with its own cap.
  • Active reservation. Exposure against authority. Not a debit, and not proof of escrow.
  • Confirmed receipt. B that the recipient authentically holds.
  • Custody. The last confirmed, accounted balance of the buyer’s A under escrow. While an external attempt is unresolved, the current location of the amount it covers is unknown; a reservation is not custody.
  • Accepted duties. What a consenting party still owes, and how it is discharged.

Who does what

A responsibility map, not a deployment.

Five parties touch one agreement. Each can do something the others cannot, and each has a limit the reader must not forget.

Two paths, one language

A direct path runs a Moriarty program on Midnight with no federation at all. The federated path adds coordination for work that reaches other participants and chains. Authoring, compiling, proving and deploying need no project membership, reviewer approval or deployment license on either path. Asset-owner authorization and application conditions still apply.

Four concerns, not four steps

The CAKE framework names Applications, Permission, Solvers and Settlement. They cross these lanes rather than forming a pipeline. Permission means owner and application authority. It is not a deployment admission scheme, and no lane is allowed to skip a check because another lane ran first.

Four acceptance judgments, kept apart

Before Midnight accepts a stage, four different questions have answers, and no one of them stands in for another. The explorer below shows illustrative rule checks for each. They are JavaScript in a web page and produce none of the native proof obligations the real judgments carry.

  1. Contract properties. The agreement’s required properties hold over their stated domain: the named recipient, the gross and fee caps, the completion goal.
  2. Intent refinement. The chosen execution stays within the owner’s authenticated authorization. A committed route may do less than the intention allows, never more or other.
  3. Transition validity. The next state and its financial effects follow the program’s rules: each event moves the account only by its stated effect, and a rejected event moves nothing.
  4. History compliance. The stage extends a compliant history from a legitimate origin: the identifiers it consumes were unconsumed, and its predecessors were themselves accepted.
How these map onto the fixture

The candidate checks in step 1 are contract-property and intent-refinement rules. The reducer’s refusal to move the account on a rejected event, and its per-event delta, are the transition-validity rule. The consumed-identifier list and the attempt and fill identifiers are the history-compliance rule. In the target design each obligation needs an enforcement point in native constraints, authenticated state or a justified ledger mechanism. A host-side check, an unused Boolean or a label saying “verified” cannot supply a missing obligation, and the checks on this page are host-side checks.

Candidates that never reach the account

  • Wrong recipient. Same amounts, delivered to the solver’s own account. It fails the named recipient check and cannot be committed. The account stays at zero.
  • Excessive fee. The same 5.5 A per fill, but 0.75 A of it is fee. Gross stays within 11 A and fees would reach 1.5 A against the 1 A cap. It fails the fee check alone.

When an outcome is unknown

Unknown is a state, not a failure.

A deadline tells you which transitions may now be attempted. It does not tell you what the other chain did.

Late success

The reservation becomes an actual debit. The receipt is confirmed. The attempt’s identifier is consumed, so a second copy of the same result cannot pay twice, and no later report of failure can release the debit or make the receipt unhappen.

Acceptance as a stage of the agreement is a separate judgment. If the document attestation or another predicate is missing, the external result is still accounted and consumed, but the fill is not accepted and the delivery duty stays open. Restoring the predicate reconciles the same observed result exactly once.

Authenticated failure

The evidence policy in this fixture establishes that the principal did not execute, so the reservation releases. The fee the destination retained is real spending, and it counts against both caps.

Not every real adapter has this failure policy. The remedy duty the provider accepted is now pending, not discharged.

Still unknown

Nothing changes. The buyer’s remaining authority is still reserved against an attempt that may yet land, or may already have executed. The escrow figure is the last confirmed balance; the current location of the reserved amount is unknown. Reconciliation may continue.

A recovery grant alone does not make a refund safe. A remedy needs signed authority, authenticated evidence, controlled assets, remaining resources and an exclusive consumption rule. Absent those, the outcome stays unresolved.

Recovery never erases a committed prefix. The first fill is done whatever happens to the second. A compensating payment or a cancellation is a new authorized action with its own costs, and it may not be described as a rollback.

What each form of evidence establishes

Three mechanisms, one statement, separate assumptions.

Zero knowledge, threshold signing and attested hardware each establish something specific under its own assumptions. Combining them can add protection when the required checks bind the same statement and are enforced at the relevant acceptance point; common binding is necessary, not sufficient. Where one operator controls several of them, those checks share a failure and become correlated dependencies. The distinct proof, threshold and hardware assumptions still need separate analysis.

Where one operator controls the keys, hardware or code behind several mechanisms, those checks share a failure and are correlated dependencies rather than multiplied protection; the distinct assumptions still stand on their own.

Where the destination decides

A foreign account that accepts a threshold signature alone will honor any transfer the threshold signs. If that toy 3-of-5 is compromised, the honest signers’ policy checks are bypassed at that destination, and a local exclusive-state rule on Midnight cannot retroactively stop the foreign effect. A hypothetical destination that also checks a named relation and a consumption rule narrows this, and only for the checks it actually performs. There is no generic proof-enforcing chain that checks every Moriarty property for you.

People and AI solvers

The same authority, whoever proposes the route.

An AI solver can search faster. It cannot spend more, pay a different recipient or skip a condition, because solver identity changes no hard constraint.

Delegation through a wallet standard

The planned Open Wallet Standard integration gives solvers a common interface for wallet operations. The program defines the assets, destinations, amounts and operations a solver may touch. Interoperability must preserve those limits rather than handing over an unrestricted key.

Paying a service is not receiving its result

An x402 payment receipt establishes payment. It does not prove the requested computation finished or that the result reached the owner. Payment, availability and delivery are three events with three records.

A fixed request, retried once
  1. request R1 authorized Scoped to one paid query, one price, one recipient. Logical identity fixed.
  2. payment observed The x402 receipt for R1 is authenticated. Paid, not delivered.
  3. connection drops Result availability is unknown. Delivery is unknown.
  4. retry, same R1 Reconciliation asks for the result of R1. It does not authorize a second charge.
  5. result delivered Delivery to the authorized recipient is recorded against R1. One payment, one delivery, one request.

Reconciliation alone never authorizes another charge. A new billable request needs its own authorization. OWS and x402 are planned integrations, and no interoperability has been demonstrated.

How a continuation carries history

After the first fill, the agreement is a continuation. It names its accepted predecessors, the duties that remain, the identifiers already consumed and the next stage it may take. Whoever takes that next stage needs the private witness it depends on. A valid proof does not hand the next participant its witness.

Two stages sharing an ancestor are not a double spend. What would be a double spend is two stages consuming the same identifier, which the consumption rule forbids. Each stage and its predecessor fan-in remain bounded; a growing finite history can continue through authenticated stages within the signed episode and lifetime budgets, which is not a promise of unbounded execution or storage. Native recursive history and private handoff remain requirements work. ZKIRv3 is the target today, and any later native interface is a planning assumption rather than a release fact.
