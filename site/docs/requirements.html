Moriarty requirements

The central question is what must remain true when a financial intention passes from its owner, through a solver and a proof, to an accepted change of state.

Research and design requirements, dated September 19, 2026. The paragraphs below state obligations to implement and establish; they do not report completed features. Midnight ZKIRv3 is the current compilation target. ZKIRv4 names the proposed next-version requirements workstream, without asserting an upstream release.

An intention specifies a space of permitted executions: their outcomes, authority, costs, disclosures and remaining duties. Moriarty's purpose is to make acceptance depend on satisfying those constraints. This first requires a language in which they can be stated, then a correspondence between that language and the native proof relation, and finally a ledger rule that connects the proved effects to the state actually consumed.

The optional Federated DeFi Kernel coordinates solvers, evidence, constrained signatures and execution on other chains. Its zero-knowledge proofs, multi-party computation and trusted execution environments carry different assumptions, which each supported adapter must expose. The language also supports a direct Midnight path: federation membership cannot become permission to author, compile, prove or deploy a supported program.

Moriarty Programming Language Requirements

The MPLRs identify behavior that must survive a change of implementation technique. They leave particular type systems, calculi and proof representations open for investigation. Their stable identifiers let a proposed construction be judged against the same obligation as an alternative.

MPLR-001 · Staged and partial transaction semantics

A transaction that crosses several settlement steps needs a state for each accepted prefix. Moriarty must record the effects already committed together with the authority and duties that remain, so that a successful debit followed by an unresolved withdrawal is represented as partial progress. Completion is a further judgment about the whole obligation.

MPLR-002 · Typed persistent continuations

Suspension leaves a computation for another participant or event to resume. That continuation must have a type and bind the program, predecessor, awaited events, authority and outstanding duties. Resumption checks these bindings and the consumption rule, preventing an unrelated callback or replay from spending the same reserved authority twice.

MPLR-003 · Conditional settlement with composable evidence

Submission and settlement are distinct moments when delivery depends on several conditions. The language must express the required combination of authenticated signatures, document predicates, proofs, recipient actions and other supported predicates, and withhold the specified delivery until that combination holds at the policy-defined settlement point. A document hash identifies evidence; its presence alone does not establish the required property.

MPLR-004 · Partial fulfillment and residual duties

A partial fill is acceptable only within a policy that permits partial fulfillment. Each fill must discharge the amount it actually satisfies while carrying forward cumulative limits, unused authority and remaining liabilities. Splitting an execution therefore cannot reset its fee budget or turn a partly paid debt into a completed agreement.

MPLR-005 · Refunds and compensation

Recovery depends on which effects have already become irreversible. A refund can return assets still under control; compensation requires a new authorized action after an earlier effect has committed. The state must retain unrecovered duties and reconcile late delivery, so that recovery neither rewrites history nor pays a second refund for the same obligation.

MPLR-006 · Authenticated asynchronous outcomes

An asynchronous result acquires financial meaning through its origin, its relation to the waiting request and the finality it establishes. A resumed stage must authenticate all three and enforce its evidence-consumption rule. Duplicate delivery must be harmless or rejected, and a success flag for another transaction must confer no authority over this one.

MPLR-007 · Joins and late results

Joining branches requires separate answers to whether enough results are available and whether the required outcome has succeeded. A quorum or first-success policy may permit progress, but duties attached to unselected and late branches remain in the state. In particular, choosing one result cannot make another branch's committed debit or refund obligation disappear.

MPLR-008 · Stage-specific authority and consent

Authority belongs to a stage under a signed lifecycle policy. Moriarty must check its scope, expiry and revocation conditions at the point of use, including the scope of any counterparty's consent. A callback inherits only the authority granted to it; changing the recipient or enlarging that authority requires the applicable authorization.

MPLR-009 · Cumulative work fees and reserves

The cost of a workflow includes its accepted stages, reserved resources and any fees that the target retains on failure. These amounts must remain within the signed cumulative bounds as execution advances or retries. Stage subdivision is consequently an accounting refinement, never a means of obtaining a fresh budget for work already authorized once.

MPLR-010 · Time finality and unresolved outcomes

A deadline determines which policy applies; missing evidence does not by itself establish that a foreign action failed to execute. The language must distinguish known failure, final success and an unresolved outcome, retaining duties until the relevant delivery, cancellation, nonexecution or recovery is established. A late successful transfer cannot justify a second payout merely because a timer expired.

MPLR-011 · Causality concurrency and interference

A continuation resumes in a world that may have changed. Its proof must preserve the causal relation to its predecessor and revalidate the state and authority on which the next action depends. Concurrent workflows must therefore coordinate consumption of shared reserves, rather than treating an earlier simulation snapshot as continuing entitlement.

MPLR-012 · Simulation and assurance status

A tool's report must identify the judgment for which it has evidence. Simulation, local evaluation, proof verification and ledger acceptance cover different objects, and omitted external steps remain unresolved. This distinction lets a developer inspect a predicted execution without mistaking the prediction for proof that its payments or external calls occurred.

MPLR-013 · Private evidence and continuations

Private state introduces a second obligation beside proof validity: an authorized successor must be able to obtain the witness needed to continue. The language must state each participant's permitted observations and the handoff conditions, including missing-witness behavior. Proving a document property must not silently disclose fields, status information or identifiers that the declared privacy policy protects.

MPLR-014 · Certified lowering of staged effects

Compilation must connect an accepted target execution to a permitted source execution. For each supported staged program, that correspondence must preserve permitted traces, conditions, rejection, costs and every retained effect under the pinned Midnight ZKIRv3 model. As ZR06 and ZR11 also require, correct output from an honest witness generator is only one case; the argument must also exclude invalid behavior produced by other satisfying witnesses.

MPLR-015 · Open programmable financial workflows

A language is useful beyond its examples when a developer can compose its supported constructs into a new program. Moriarty must validate that program through public, versioned language and proof rules. A catalog of approved loans or swaps cannot replace those rules, and absence from a project registry must not invalidate an otherwise supported program.

MPLR-016 · Application authority versus project access

An application may restrict its counterparties or trusted evidence issuers through an authenticated policy. Such a restriction concerns that application's authority, while access to authoring, compilation, proving and deployment remains permissionless. Project review records, hosted-service credentials and federation membership must never become universal prerequisites for valid Moriarty programs.

MPLR-017 · Complete staged financial accounting

An accepted stage needs a complete account of what changed. Its authenticated effects must identify assets, custody, authorized supply changes and fees, while liabilities follow their own typed evolution rules. Internal balance conservation establishes neither external delivery nor the discharge of debt; each of those claims needs the corresponding effect and evidence.

MPLR-018 · Inspectable conditional intent

Authorization is meaningful only if the participant can inspect the object being authorized. Signing and reporting tools must expose the canonical conditions, destination, evidence policy, permitted partial outcomes and recovery rules, and bind that presentation to the proved object. An interface must not promise an automatic refund when the signed policy permits an unresolved outcome or compensation instead.

MPLR-019 · Consent to introduction of obligations

Creating or increasing an enforceable obligation requires the affected party's consent to its material terms, either directly or through a valid prior authorization. Sending value to an address alone cannot create that consent. This requirement concerns the formation of duties and does not demand interaction from every recipient of a passive positive-value receipt.

MPLR-020 · Certified primitive substitution

A certified primitive replaces a reference expression only under a stated equivalence. Its version must bind the preserved semantics, preconditions, failure behavior and relevant host-to-target correspondence. The same substitution must remain justified when used inside a proof, so a faster implementation or a familiar primitive name cannot serve as its certificate.

MPLR-021 · Bounded resource certificates

Bounded execution requires a checked resource bound for the artifact actually admitted, including components chosen after initial compilation. The certificate must use a named cost model and cover the structures the verifier will consume. A successful benchmark can inform that model, but it cannot establish a bound for every admitted input or guarantee that an asynchronous workflow eventually completes.

MPLR-022 · Program and evidence commitment separation

Program identity, semantic version, signed intention, state and evidence perform different roles in acceptance. Their commitments must preserve those roles and constrain every allowed substitution of program or witness. Source, intermediate and target artifacts may have different hashes; the obligation is to prove their authorized correspondence, not to mistake different representations for identical bytes.

MPLR-023 · Mandatory conditions constrain acceptance

A mandatory condition matters only if its failure prevents the protected effect. The compiler and verifier must establish that connection across every supported branch and verification guard. Merely computing a Boolean, displaying a check, or supplying a proof whose result is discarded leaves the intended restriction unenforced.

MPLR-024 · Explicit settlement domains

Atomicity is a claim about a particular settlement domain and a specified set of effects. Moriarty must name that boundary and retain separate pending and final states for relocation or execution on independent chains. A locally atomic transition cannot by itself make a foreign transfer part of the same atomic event; that transfer retains its own pending and final states.

MPLR-025 · Netting preserves gross economics

Netting changes how obligations are discharged, but must preserve the economics the parties authorized. An explicit relation between gross and net obligations must account for each asset, party, fee, liability and residual duty. A smaller net transfer must not conceal an excessive gross debit or erase a claim that was never included in the authorized netting.

MPLR-026 · Behavioral contracts for settlement implementations

Two settlement implementations are interchangeable only for the behavior their caller requires. Acceptance must establish the financial and authority postconditions of the selected implementation, including the effects through which it satisfies them. A matching interface or method name leaves this behavioral obligation open.

MPLR-027 · Authenticated origins and recursive compliance

Recursive compliance begins with an authenticated origin and continues through compatible predecessor statements. Each accepted transition must preserve a well-founded history of consumed resources and remaining duties, so that fabricated funding or a circular proof cannot become legitimate genesis. The history proof must also connect to current-state consumption; a valid past does not create a second right to spend.

MPLR-028 · Consent-preserving semantic evolution

Evolution changes the objects on which earlier consent relied. A new program, verifier, policy or continuation representation must preserve the signed semantics, duties, recovery rights, privacy and resource constraints, or obtain the applicable amendment authority. Compatibility of formats alone cannot justify a semantically weaker successor.

MPLR-029 · Authenticated completeness of private state

Claims of absence or uniqueness need an authenticated domain in which they are true. A private transition must therefore bind the authenticated state domain and establish the required nonmembership or completeness property under explicit ledger assumptions. A witness containing no competing reservation does not establish that no reservation exists outside the witness.

MPLR-030 · Common evidence statement and federation trust

When the federation combines proofs, attestations and signatures, each must concern the same intention, settlement domain, stage, epoch, artifact and exact effects. The resulting claim must expose its threshold, hardware, observation and recovery assumptions. Combining several mechanisms is useful only when their bindings agree and their possible failures, including correlated failures, remain visible.

MPLR-031 · Delegated solver capabilities

A solver acts through authority delegated by an owner. The accepted effect must stay within that delegation across agent, wallet and signer boundaries, including the permitted assets, destinations, amounts and operations. Interoperability through an open wallet interface must preserve those limits rather than handing an unrestricted fund-control key to the solver.

MPLR-032 · Concurrent service budgets

Concurrent tasks draw on one authenticated account of spent, pending and residual funds. Reservations must include fees and sponsored execution where applicable, and reconcile on completion or recovery. Retries and new request identifiers cannot each claim the same remaining allowance as though the other tasks did not exist.

MPLR-033 · Payment and service completion

Paying for a service, making its result available and delivering that result to the authorized recipient are separate events. A purchased service must preserve the obligations attached to each event until they are discharged. An x402 payment receipt can therefore establish payment without proving that the requested computation finished or that its result reached the owner.

MPLR-034 · Logical paid-request identity

A retried service request must retain one authenticated logical obligation. The workflow must distinguish recovery or retrieval of an already purchased result from authorization of new billable work. Otherwise a network interruption can turn one request into several charges while leaving the original delivery duty unresolved.

MPLR-035 · Constraint-preserving solver completion

A solver completes an intention by choosing within its authenticated constraints. Completion, combination and routing must refine every applicable signed constraint and permitted choice, bind introduced effects and disclosures, and retain prior commitments and residual duties; an out-of-scope change needs a separately authorized amendment. An uncommitted candidate remains distinct from an accepted partial stage: abandoning it cannot roll back effects already committed elsewhere. No developer or solver membership list may replace proof checking. Proof of a permitted solution establishes neither optimality nor eventual completion.

Proposed ZKIRv4 contract

The next backend contract must express the relations required by the language and preserve their meaning through verification and settlement. Financial concepts need not become new opcodes when existing constraints can express them faithfully. These sixteen requirements apply to the native Midnight proof stack; they do not require an additional Lean theorem prover or a replacement of Midnight’s proof system with Mina’s. The Mina-derived recursion refinements (MNR01–MNR08) below borrow lessons from Mina’s recursive verification while retaining Midnight’s native backend.

ZR01 · Versioned interface

A proof has meaning relative to the instruction semantics, compiler, transcript, keys, parameters and verifier that produced and check it. The native interface must identify this complete compatible set, including any accumulator and final decider, and reject unsupported combinations before financial acceptance. Independent verification then has a definite object to reproduce rather than an unspecified toolchain to trust.

ZR02 · General program relations

Recursion must apply to developer-defined bounded relations expressible in the supported profile. It must permit repeated steps of one relation and composition of different relations whose compatibility is established. This requirement makes new financial programs possible without a maintainer allowlist, while preserving the obligation to reject a valid proof of the wrong relation.

ZR03 · Complete statement binding

The recursive statement must bind program and semantics, property, signed intent, settlement domain, predecessors, observations, complete effects, residual duties and resource limits. Where representations differ, the application must enforce a pinned correspondence or refinement relation; within one canonical representation, the relevant identities must agree. Permitted witness variation remains possible without permitting an unauthorized change of meaning.

ZR04 · Genesis and well-foundedness

The compliance relation must distinguish legitimate genesis from recursive continuation and enforce well-founded descent or an equivalent sound induction structure. Funding, authority and initialization require authentication at the base case; recursive steps cannot manufacture them by relabeling a proof or cycling through predecessors. Finite tests can expose mistakes, but the absence of such cycles requires an argument about the relation itself.

ZR05 · Verifier identity

Every predecessor must be verified under a relation and verification key allowed by the bound program policy, together with the corresponding accumulator and decider identities. Both fixed keys and policy-checked dynamic keys may satisfy this obligation. A prover must not obtain acceptance by substituting a weaker circuit whose proof is valid but whose claim is irrelevant.

ZR06 · Adversarial-witness soundness

For every accepted witness in the supported profile, satisfaction of the target constraints must imply a valid semantic execution. Types, ranges, Boolean selectors, encodings, material inactive-branch values and output shape belong in that implication. Preconditions enforced only by honest witness preparation leave an adversarial witness outside the intended guarantee.

ZR07 · Mandatory verification/control

Every mandatory predecessor proof and intention predicate must constrain acceptance across the supported control flow. A disabled verification guard or a discarded false result cannot discharge an active obligation. Deliberately optional parents remain possible when the program specifies their inactive semantics and prevents that option from bypassing a required check.

ZR08 · Complete deferred verification

Recursive verification may defer cryptographic work, but financial acceptance must discharge every obligation needed by the statement. Accumulators, pairing checks and final deciders must remain bound to the outer proof and reach the complete verification path. Safe batching is permitted; an outer-proof success with unfinished obligations must never be reported as final validity.

ZR09 · Bounded multi-parent joins

A stage must support a declared bounded number of parents, including at least two for private split-and-join composition. Its join relation must preserve compatible intentions, resource partitioning, all duties and aggregate budgets without counting common ancestry as new spending authority. Bounded fan-in permits growing finite histories, provided their authorized per-run and lifetime budgets remain intact.

ZR10 · State and ledger consumption

The ledger must connect proved resource identities to authenticated current state and enforce the declared consumption or nonce rules. Two valid successors of one spendable state cannot both consume it. Imported certificates need authenticated anchors, and an earlier valid proof cannot establish present availability, absence or entitlement without the corresponding current-state checks.

ZR11 · Compiler/effect correspondence

The source-to-target relation must preserve stage results, rejection, arithmetic, effects and residual state through Midnight's guaranteed and fallible phases. If the fallible business action fails, any retained guaranteed effects and fees must still agree with the signed policy. This is a correspondence obligation for all accepted witnesses, not a claim that a successful example establishes the whole compiler.

ZR12 · Public boundedness

Resource bounds must cover the actual admitted stage, parent arity, public inputs, proof parsing, accumulators, relevant memory structures and late-bound components under a pinned model. Exceeding a bound must produce explicit rejection or another specified outcome without weakening verification. Termination of this bounded computation remains distinct from eventual settlement of the surrounding workflow.

ZR13 · Honest cost and feasibility reporting

Feasibility must be measured over the complete supported path, including deferred verification work. Reports must identify rows or degree, proof and key sizes, accumulator size, time and peak memory on named hardware, and relate ledger charges to the work performed. Performance objectives remain separate from correctness and cannot justify omitting a required check.

ZR14 · Private continuation interface

Private continuation requires both binding state commitments and a usable handoff between separately controlled participants. The interface must specify which witness, proof and key artifacts a successor needs, what each observer may learn and what happens when material is withheld. Possessing a reusable proof supplies neither missing secret state nor spending authority.

ZR15 · Semantic migration

Changing a program, relation, key, verifier or continuation schema must preserve authenticated state, consumed resources, duties, recovery and disclosure constraints under the signed migration policy, or obtain amendment authority. An interface-compatible upgrade can still weaken consent. An immutable deployment is a valid initial choice.

ZR16 · Independent reproducibility and assurance

Tooling must distinguish compilation, constraint synthesis, proving, outer verification, complete verification and ledger finality. It must retain sufficient statements, proofs, keys and parameters for an independent compatible verifier to repeat the final check. Reproducibility concerns the verified claim and its artifacts, without demanding identical bytes from randomized proof generation.

Recursion workstream

A recursive statement must justify a history from an authenticated origin while allowing each new stage to remain bounded. The full target includes private handoff and at least two-parent split-and-join composition, with all effects, consumed resources and remaining duties preserved. Ordinary ledger induction or aggregation of unrelated proofs cannot discharge these native history obligations.

Comprehensive Midnight recursion around March 2027 is a project planning assumption recorded on September 19, 2026, not a verified release commitment. The workstream must qualify the actual compatible release, establish recursive soundness and source-to-ledger correspondence, demonstrate independently controlled private continuation, and measure the complete verification path. The following Mina-derived refinements specify additional points that this work must settle.

MNR01 · Transcript and challenge specification

The transcript must specify what is absorbed, in which order and encoding, under which domain tags, and how challenges are derived. Native and in-circuit verifiers must implement the same contract, including verifier identity and recursive messages, with field or limb conversion specified wherever non-native arithmetic is used. Mina motivates this obligation without prescribing its Pasta or IPA representations for Midnight.

MNR02 · Deferred obligations, base cases and active masks

Each deferred obligation needs an identified producer, authenticated inputs, consumer and invariant linking those steps. Final acceptance must discharge every active obligation, while branch choice, parent count, padding and base-case masks remain constrained by the authorized relation. Empty required batches, dummy parents and a recursive case relabeled as genesis must not create an escape from verification.

MNR03 · Heterogeneous compatibility and key authorization

Composition of different relations must bind statement schema, field encoding, child proof shape, supported features, key and parameter identity, and the shape limits used by the selected backend. Key bytes must agree with their claimed identity, and that identity must separately be authorized by owner or program policy. Compatible dimensions alone establish neither permission nor semantic compatibility.

MNR04 · Verified values and auxiliary output

A parsed proof exposes data before it establishes the truth of that data. Tooling must distinguish parsed fields, verified statements, witness-only or auxiliary values, predicted effects and authenticated actual effects. Every required dependency must be constrained and verified, and settlement must impose authoritative current-state preconditions rather than trusting a valid but stale predecessor.

MNR05 · Production proof-mode separation

Evidence intended to justify production acceptance must identify a configuration that performs real proving and complete verification. Simulation, dummy proofs, disabled-proof modes and empty test keys must remain visibly separate. A test-mode success never qualifies as production acceptance; the qualification record must demonstrate real proving and independent complete verification.

MNR06 · Parameter and cache provenance

A cached artifact is useful only when its content, build and semantic versions, purpose and expected identity agree with the requested computation. Parameter sets used together must share an authenticated identity or an established compatibility relation; equal dimensions are insufficient. Stale or corrupt entries must be rejected or safely rebuilt, including after authorized version changes.

MNR07 · Batching and join soundness

Batch verification must state its challenge or coefficient generation, binding to the complete batch, randomness assumptions, required cardinality and composed soundness error. A tree of bounded joins must preserve the same required parents, financial duties and budgets as the relation it implements. Tests for omitted or invalid members accompany the probabilistic soundness argument; they cannot replace it.

MNR08 · Specification and component assurance coverage

Assurance must follow each used feature from its mathematical statement through specification and compiler constraints to the final verifier. The record must identify the exact coverage and gaps of audits and theorems, including dependencies, gates, lookups, bindings and recursion. A historical audit of one component cannot qualify a changed implementation or transfer another protocol's guarantees to Midnight.

Implementation direction

The consolidated U0–U7 sequence is planned to establish the financial semantics and target compatibility matrix (U0), then the certified primitive basis and native feasibility (U1). It is to proceed through general supported single-stage programs on Midnight (U2), partial and conditional settlement with recovery (U3), full native history and private composition (U4), and optional federation integration and constrained solver services (U5). Financial-library conformance (U6) and a reproducible public developer release (U7) complete the agenda. The project’s earlier SP01–SP12 sprint contracts retain their acceptance obligations within this sequence; early subsets do not retire the full recursion or private-composition requirements.

From requirements to evidence

Each implemented slice needs positive and hostile cases, a precise semantic claim, a pinned compiler and verifier, and retained artifacts that an independent checker can inspect. Where the claim reaches a ledger or an external chain, the evidence must reach that boundary too. The source requirement snapshot retains the underlying behavioral clauses and backend contract; the current syntax and formal semantics record the narrower language profiles available for implementation work today.
