# Gottlieb edit: 08-constraints-and-verdicts.md

Line numbers refer to the original chapter.

## Critical findings
1. **Semicolon as the default joint.** Twenty-four semicolons in 2,900 words, and most of them join two sentences that stand alone: lines 5, 49, 55, 57, 59, 65 (twice), 82, 84, 94, 106, 126 and 141. The reader learns that every second thought will be hitched to the one before it, and the rhythm flattens into one long clause chain. Fix: a period where the second clause is a new statement, "and" where the two are parallel facts of equal weight. Keep the semicolons that carry a real contrast between two parallel structures (line 88, "the static check ... follow `preprocess`; the gates follow ..."; line 117 inside the protected section; line 65's `assert` sentence can lose its own).
2. **Sentence-length uniformity (rolling window).** The "Width limits" section, lines 90 and 94, is the worst window: nine consecutive sentences of 20 to 34 words, each carrying a name, a bound, a message string and a case tag. Nothing in the run resets the reader. Fix: break line 94 at its natural joints ("The `div_mod_power_of_two` gate does not repeat that check." can stand alone; the `preprocess`/in-circuit contrast and the `FR_BITS` underflow are separate statements), and let the `k05` and `f04` cases in line 90 land in their own shorter sentences.
3. **Machinery in the prose.** Line 55, last sentence: "Failures are `violated(...)` (case `k01`; off-circuit Jubjub decompression uses only the parity of `x`, K1 in 13-known-divergences.md) and `violated(...)` (case `f11`, the order-2 Curve25519 point)." One sentence carries two message strings, two case tags, a cross-reference and a fact about off-circuit decompression, all inside parentheses. Fix: one sentence per failure, with the decompression fact and its K1 reference as a plain sentence of their own.

## Important findings
4. **Voice slips into the workshop (verbs).** "demands" at lines 55 and 65 gives a K function an appetite. Fix: "requires". "emission lives in `zkir-vm.k`" (line 5): "the emission rules are in".
5. **Assertion by adverb.** Line 141, "gates that were actually emitted": the paragraph before it has just distinguished `no-such-gate` from an inferred `holds`, so "emitted" alone certifies the claim. Fix: cut "actually". "the exact `(x, y)`" (lines 55 and 117) stays: "exact" contrasts with parity-only decompression and is doing work.
6. **Participial tails.** "giving one `verdict(...)` per emitted constraint" (line 3), "matching `max(bits + bits % 2, 4)`" (line 90), "matching the `encode_incircuit` loop" (line 106). Each is a factual correspondence hung off the sentence as a participle. Fix: a finite clause ("and each emitted constraint receives one verdict", "which matches").
7. **Paragraph doing two jobs.** Line 20 lists the four cells and the `job` sequence, then switches to what happens after a witness failure. Fix: two paragraphs, split before "Instructions still emit"; the `job` sentence moves first so the paragraph does not close on a stub.
8. **Reversed emphasis.** Line 106, last sentence: the reason the `synthErr` branch exists ("so that `eval` is total") arrives after a 30-word explanation of why it is unreachable. Fix: lead with the reason, then the unreachability as the explanation after a colon.
9. **The contrast reflex.** "unsatisfiable, not unbuildable" (57), "value equality, not a permutation argument" (116), "`no-such-gate`, not an inferred `holds`" (139), "`unknown` rather than `violated`" (20), "`unknown` rather than `holds`" (122). Five in one chapter is a habit, but every one of them separates two outcomes the reader must not confuse. All stay.
10. **Docent's elbow (mild).** "`commGate` is described below" (line 47). The promise of three gates in the sentence before makes the pointer necessary. Reworded to "has its own section below"; kept.

## Tell audit
- Content: none. No inflated significance, no promotional framing, no vague authority. Every number is tied to a receipt, a case tag or a K symbol.
- Language: no stock AI vocabulary. No copula avoidance (the chapter is happy with "is"). No forced triads: the three "not that ..." clauses in line 3 and the three items in line 141 are real enumerations. No false ranges. The contrast reflex (finding 9) is present but earned.
- Style: no dashes, no decorative bold, no lists where prose belongs (the one bullet list, lines 116 to 120, is a list of omissions and should stay a list). Semicolon saturation (finding 1). Uniform rhythm in "Width limits" (finding 2).
- Communication: one self-pointer (finding 10). No recaps after headings, no warm-up sentences. The closing paragraph (line 141) restates the opening's "and nothing more", but it lands the two-part evidence requirement (status and an empty `violations` list) that the harness section set up, so it is a coda that argues rather than re-lists. Kept.
- Filler and hedging: "actually" (finding 5), "the point itself" (line 55, harmless, kept for the contrast with the pair `(x, y)`). No stacked qualifiers, no signposting.

## Notes on voice and cadence
The chapter knows when to be short. "None of them is a PLONKish gate row." (line 9), "`eval` is total." (line 37), "`isSpecialEmit` is true only for `impact` and `output`." (line 33) and "No cofactor is cleared before that test or before the output comparison." (line 55) each arrive after a long sentence and reset the reader. The parallel openings of the two constructor sections ("`Constraint` has six constructors." / "`Outcome` has five constructors.") are a deliberate echo and help a reader who is scanning. The "What `holds` establishes" section is the best-built passage: one 75-word sentence stating the whole positive claim, then a 12-word sentence turning to the omissions, then a list. That long sentence is the load the section carries and is left exactly as written. The `f05` paragraph (line 108) is the model for the rest: short opening, one long explanatory sentence, the case, and a one-sentence consequence.

## Verdict
A precise reference chapter whose facts are in the right order and whose tables carry the parallel material, so the prose is not doing a list's job anywhere. Nothing needs deleting except one adverb. The work is punctuation and joints: two dozen semicolons where most should be periods, one parenthetical that has swallowed a sentence, a paragraph carrying two subjects, and one section where every sentence has the same length and load. The fix is rewriting at the sentence level in about a dozen places; the section on what `holds` establishes changes only one connective and not one word of its claim.
