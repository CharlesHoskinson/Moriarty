# Gottlieb edit: 04-values-and-encoding.md

Line numbers refer to the original chapter.

## Critical findings
1. **Sentence-length uniformity.** The prose runs at a mean of 43 words a sentence with almost no short sentence to reset the reader. Lines 82 (six sentences, about 150 words, none under 20), 85, 105, 107, 113 (one 63-word sentence carrying an appositive list and a parenthetical between "which accepts" and "and otherwise fails") and 115 stack long, semicolon-jointed sentences one after another. Fix: break at the natural joints (the semicolon, the "which", the "so"), and let one plain statement stand on its own in each of those paragraphs ("At 2ffe2d1 there is no assertion." "A Jubjub scalar is one element.").
2. **Machinery in the prose.** Rust symbols and file names interrupt clauses mid-thought: line 3 has two parentheticals in one sentence, line 50 stops for `NUM_BITS_SUBGROUP` between "252 bits" and "into 254-bit batches", line 52 ends on an absolute, line 113 wedges `#encodeAll` inside the `#commCheck` sentence. The symbols must stay (the chapter rules require file and symbol for every rule described), so the fix is placement, not deletion: give the symbol its own clause or sentence, or move it to the end of the clause it documents.
3. **The absolute-clause tic.** "r being the order" (line 3), "their identity being `pt(0, 1)`" (line 7), "the capacity of the BLS12-381 scalar field being 254" (line 52): three "X being Y" constructions in the first fifty lines, each hanging a fact off the back of a sentence that has already finished. Fix: "where r is", "their identity is", a separate sentence for the capacity.

## Important findings
4. **Semicolon as the default joint.** Thirty-two semicolons in sixty-odd sentences; line 25 chains four definitions on semicolons, lines 79, 81, 83, 85, 99, 105, 107, 111, 117 each carry one where the clauses are independent facts. Fix: keep a semicolon where the second clause qualifies the first (line 99 "92e8bdd3 does not check", line 115 "barring a Poseidon collision; case ... records this"); convert the rest to full stops.
5. **Colon stacking.** Line 44: two colon sentences in a row, the second ending in a third colon that introduces the code block. Fix: make the middle sentence a plain statement ("Bytes32 is the hand-written arm of `encode_offcircuit`.") and let only the last colon introduce the block.
6. **Loose appositives.** "into 254-bit batches, a single batch," (line 50); "returns `None` under the same conditions, an ordinary `Failed to decode` error" (line 82); "and byte 31 a second" with the verb elided (line 44); "the first 31 bytes read little-endian form one element" (line 44), where "read little-endian" is a reduced relative the ear takes as the main verb. Fix: "which here means a single batch"; a colon before "an ordinary ... error"; "byte 31 forms a second"; commas around "read little-endian".
7. **A refrain with two wordings.** "returns `.List` and only keeps the function total" (line 31) and "only keep the functions total" (line 67) say the same thing, which is right, but the first reads as if returning `.List` were the way the arm keeps the function total. Fix: "that arm exists only to keep the function total" in both places.
8. **"as" doing the work of "because".** Line 109, "as `preprocess` slices before checking", uses the same "as X does" shape that elsewhere in the chapter means "in the same way as" (lines 50, 83, 85). Here it gives the reason for the panic. Fix: "because".

## Tell audit
- Content: no inflated significance, no promotional framing; the one framing sentence (line 3, "crosses the boundary between program and proof") is earned and occurs once.
- Language: no stock vocabulary, no false ranges, no synonym cycling. "not a sort constraint" (line 25) and "never values" (line 107) are real distinctions, not the reflex. The three-item lists (lines 52, 85) are the actual number of steps or checks.
- Style: no dashes (the spaced hyphens in "x - 1" and "p - 1" are minus signs), no decorative bold, tables carry parallel facts as they should.
- Communication: "This chapter covers" (line 3) is a single scope sentence at the top, which reference documentation needs; there is no recap and no cheerleading.
- Filler and hedging: none. "exactly" (lines 79, 86, 115) is the mathematical sense each time, and "unconditionally" (line 99) is a fact about the rule.

## Notes on voice and cadence
The short sentences at line 65 ("Because of the shift, 1 encodes as `[0, 0]` and 0 as the limbs of p - 1. The shift applies to every foreign type, base and scalar alike, and never to Native or JubjubScalar.") show the register the rest of the chapter should reach more often: a fact, its consequence, its scope, each in its own breath. The decoding bullets (lines 81 to 87) open uniformly with the type name and the condition, which is the right uniformity for a reference list. The encoding table and the cross-check table do the parallel work that would otherwise bloat the prose. The voice never editorialises and never hedges; every claim is anchored to a symbol.

## Verdict
This is a sound reference chapter with nothing to cut: every sentence carries a fact, and the facts are the point. Its one real defect is monotony of shape. Nearly every sentence is long, jointed by semicolons and colons, and hung with parentheticals in the middle of its clauses, so the reader has no place to breathe between the Bytes32 panic, the Jubjub parity rule and the commitment mismatch. The fix is rewriting at the joints, not deletion: split the long chains into statements of one fact each, move the citation apparatus to the ends of clauses, drop the three absolutes, and let a few plain six-word sentences stand. Keep the tables, the bullet openers and the line-65 cadence exactly as they are.
