# P3 review — compilation, proving and AI ergonomics

## Verdict

**MIL/1 has the right boundary but should not be frozen as written.** Signed conditions, explicit evidence classes and bounded stages fit Moriarty’s permissionless proof-carrying design. A finite predicate tree can compile to ZKIRv3. The unsound parts are the escrow exit rule, the claim that hole polarity proves refinement, and the proposed fallback that evaluates only selected signed clauses. U0 can freeze a versioned signed-condition format and its enforcement contract; it should not promise the full predicate evaluator, static solver, Episode proof system and AI diagnostics at U0. This is a design review, not a compiler, proof or ledger result; I ran none of those checks.

## Findings

1. **Canonical authentication is achievable, but a public digest alone authenticates nothing.** ZKIRv3 is straight-line IR with arithmetic, comparisons, hashing and transcript operations; it has no recursive AST execution primitive ([zkir-instruction-set.md:19–35](/home/charl/Moriarty/wiki/zkir/zkir-instruction-set.md:19), [zkir-instruction-set.md:116–135](/home/charl/Moriarty/wiki/zkir/zkir-instruction-set.md:116)). Compile a bounded tree into a fixed instruction sequence, or use a bounded interpreter with constrained opcode selectors. Either way, constrain canonical decoding, tree evaluation, the digest, and equality to the signed message or a ledger-verified signature commitment. Bind the relation and verifier key too: otherwise a prover can offer a circuit that reports the right digest while omitting a condition ([DESIGN.md:203–207](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/DESIGN.md:203), [CONSOLIDATED-DESIGN.md:42–46](/home/charl/Moriarty/docs/MORIARTY-CONSOLIDATED-DESIGN.md:42)).

   **Cost estimate, not measurement:** a fixed 64-node tree serialized at an average 64 bytes per node is about 4 KiB; SHA-256 over exactly 4 KiB takes **65 compression blocks including padding**. Evaluation is roughly linear in nodes for a statically lowered tree, while a general interpreter pays opcode selection at every node. Range-checked wide products, `mulDiv`, signatures and authenticated reads may dominate either cost. ZKIRv3 has Poseidon and SHA-256 operations ([zkir-instruction-set.md:396–421](/home/charl/Moriarty/wiki/zkir/zkir-instruction-set.md:396)); its 34 instructions and 13 types do not yield a row estimate. Specify a small initial node/byte bound and measure the complete circuit before calling it affordable. Depth alone is insufficient: a full binary tree of depth eight can contain 511 nodes.

2. **The fallback is unsound unless “effect-binding” means every clause that can change acceptance.** A clause governing authority, fees, disclosure, refund, evidence or persistent duties binds effects even when it is not adjacent to an `Effect` node. Committing to the entire tree but evaluating a selected subset lets a valid signature authorize an execution the signer’s unevaluated clauses forbid. A Merkle commitment helps open signed clauses; it does not prove that omitted clauses are irrelevant. Require a compiler-checked dependency closure and prove that its evaluated result equals the full signed acceptance predicate, or reject unsupported trees. The fallback in [DESIGN.md:295–297](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/DESIGN.md:295) cannot preserve the design’s “intent is the specification” claim without that equivalence proof.

3. **Enforcement needs an explicit division of labor.** A host may calculate candidates and diagnostics, but never supply the acceptance Boolean ([CONSOLIDATED-DESIGN.md:44–48](/home/charl/Moriarty/docs/MORIARTY-CONSOLIDATED-DESIGN.md:44)).

   | Construct | Required enforcement |
   | --- | --- |
   | Escrow release | Circuit proves the signed condition over authenticated inputs and gates the selected transition; ledger atomically consumes or updates the local escrow. |
   | Refund | Same, plus a ledger tombstone excluding later release. An external timeout is only a trigger for an authorized recovery attempt, not proof of foreign non-execution. |
   | Footprint disjointness | Static set intersection for fixed cells; constrained alias/equality checks for symbolic cells. Bind **actual** reads and writes, then let the ledger serialize shared state. |
   | Hole monotonicity | Static refinement proof for the admitted fragment; circuit or ledger binds the signed template, exact filling, declared bounds and unchanged fields. |
   | Conservation | Circuit checks finite-width sums over complete effect lines; ledger checks that those are the effects it applies, including supply changes. |
   | Evidence class | Type checker rejects illegal use; circuit or ledger authenticates the actual state proof, signature or attestation and its policy identity. “Imported” remains a trust assumption, not local truth. |
   | Authority kind | Type checker checks permitted operations; circuit checks right, scope, budget and window against authenticated state; ledger consumes authority and applies revocation. |
   | Replay | Circuit binds intent, domain and logical-request identity; ledger enforces unique current-state or nonce consumption. A digest is an identifier, not a replay lock. |

   The expensive cases are dynamic footprints, imported-evidence verification, signature checks and wide arithmetic—not the enum tags. No available measurement establishes that any is unaffordable. A general foreign-chain non-execution proof should not be presumed available ([DESIGN.md:111–119](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/DESIGN.md:111), [PRODUCT-CONTRACT.md:43–47](/home/charl/Moriarty/docs/MORIARTY-PRODUCT-CONTRACT.md:43)).

4. **The assumed static checks have different decision procedures.** For a *fixed finite-width, finite-set, bounded-state* Φ, test disjointness by SAT/SMT on `release ∧ refund`, and exhaustiveness by SAT/SMT on the negation of `release ∨ refund ∨ after(deadline)`. Bit-vector multiplication and `mulDiv` require bit-blasting or an SMT bit-vector encoding; worst-case search grows exponentially with the symbolic inputs. Fixed-cell footprint overlap is elementary set intersection; symbolic aliases need equality reasoning or the same solver. A syntactic polarity pass is decidable and cheap, but proves refinement only for a deliberately restricted grammar. Bounded Episode reachability is bounded model checking over the transition relation, not a Φ syntax check. Unbounded recovery viability and external inclusion are not decided by this procedure. A compile-time SMT result need not enter the *runtime* trusted computing base if a small checker validates its certificate or the compiler’s static decision is covered by source-to-target correctness; an unchecked solver answer does become a compiler trust premise ([DESIGN.md:121–147](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/DESIGN.md:121), [DESIGN.md:185–201](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/DESIGN.md:185)).

5. **An Episode should be a linked sequence of accepted stages at U2/U3, not one proof of its whole DAG.** Each stage proof can bind the episode ID, signed intent, predecessor commitment(s), cumulative debit/fee counters, outstanding duties and terminal status. Midnight state consumption links resident stages; a join checks compatible parents and no double-spent resources. Portable or private multi-parent history needs U4’s native recursive evidence. Requiring a single Episode certificate for U2 would import U4’s unresolved recursion dependency, contrary to the roadmap’s scoped ledger-induced U2 history ([DESIGN.md:26–41](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/DESIGN.md:26), [ROADMAP.md:23–25](/home/charl/Moriarty/ROADMAP.md:23), [CONSOLIDATED-DESIGN.md:79–87](/home/charl/Moriarty/docs/MORIARTY-CONSOLIDATED-DESIGN.md:79)). Stage Φ evaluation is bounded by node count; footprint work by cell count; compile-time escrow analysis is outside a stage. A realistic two-asset escrow might use 20–50 predicate nodes, a few observations and fewer than ten effects—**an illustrative sizing assumption, not a measured profile**.

## Defects

- **The escrow rule rejects normal waiting and still fails to guarantee an exit.** With `release = delivered` and `refund = after(deadline)`, a funded escrow awaiting delivery *before* the deadline satisfies none of `release ∨ refund ∨ after(deadline)`, so the proposed validity check rejects it. After the deadline, the final disjunct is true even if no authorized refund transition can run. Worse, `delivered ∧ after(deadline)` makes release and refund overlap. These are ordinary states of the design’s own example ([DESIGN.md:185–187](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/DESIGN.md:185), [CATEGORY-MAP.md:15–21](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/CATEGORY-MAP.md:15)). Model pending as legitimate; define branch priority or a checked exclusivity condition at **exit**, and state recovery assumptions separately.

- **Polarity does not establish the claimed semantic refinement for full Φ.** Subtraction, products, `min`/`max`, Boolean alternatives and set-valued holes defeat a simple “increasing a hole” rule. Indeed the implication `Φ_guarantee[σ] ⇒ Φ_guarantee` is not well-typed as a closed obligation while the right-hand formula still contains a hole. Define intent denotations and require `Accepted(completion) ⊆ Accepted(signed template)`. For each concrete fill, prove or check `completed ∧ ¬original` unsatisfiable; a restricted polarity discipline can be a conservative shortcut ([DESIGN.md:126–143](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/DESIGN.md:126), [DESIGN.md:191–201](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/DESIGN.md:191)).

- **Several examples and category verdicts exceed the defined language.** Φ lacks the shown `never`, `not exercised`, `failed(route)`, `attested(…)` and `equity(…)` forms ([DESIGN.md:133–143](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/DESIGN.md:133), [CATEGORY-MAP.md:43–59](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/CATEGORY-MAP.md:43), [CATEGORY-MAP.md:103–111](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/CATEGORY-MAP.md:103)). More materially, an `Encumbrance` has one `against: ObligationId`, while the map says one encumbrance against several obligations is expressible ([DESIGN.md:100–106](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/DESIGN.md:100), [CATEGORY-MAP.md:205–206](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/CATEGORY-MAP.md:205)). A per-line foreign-domain effect can state an intended credit, but one Midnight stage cannot thereby prove that a foreign ledger applied it ([DESIGN.md:209–221](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/DESIGN.md:209)).

- **Residue cannot turn an unrepresented real effect into an acceptable one.** The proposal says residue never affects validity and suggests otherwise-unrecordable effects become “residual” ([DESIGN.md:221–223](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/DESIGN.md:221)). Complete actual effects are mandatory. Treat residue as signed, visible disclosure of an *unexpressed wish or risk*; reject an effect that the accepted relation cannot account for. Free text cannot be used as an acceptance escape hatch.

## Missing and disagreements

**Must be in MIL/1:** an exact canonical byte format with hash/domain separation, resolved signing time in place of `now`, signature-to-statement binding, opcode/version rules, and a closed supported-condition subset; a branch policy for release/refund races; the state and proof boundary for dynamic footprints; and explicit cumulative budget and duty fields in continuations. These determine what a signer authorizes ([DESIGN.md:203–207](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/DESIGN.md:203), [PRODUCT-CONTRACT.md:39–47](/home/charl/Moriarty/docs/MORIARTY-PRODUCT-CONTRACT.md:39)). **Can be libraries:** particular interest, vault, governance-voting and slash-priority policies, provided the core can express their state transitions. **Later milestone:** broad Φ arithmetic, synthesis, private joins and native recursive certificates; their semantics can be reserved now without claiming U0 delivery.

I disagree with placing the *whole* Φ, holes, static solver, footprints, authority kinds and canonical form in U0 ([DESIGN.md:299–310](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/DESIGN.md:299)). Freeze the signed AST envelope, identifiers, trust labels, authority tags, footprint contract and a minimal Boolean/comparison/time/delivery subset. Freeze unknown-opcode rejection and version migration. Defer `mulDiv`, general sets, synthesis and Episode proving. Deferring the **envelope or condition commitment** would break signatures when escrow arrives; deferring advanced opcodes under a versioned envelope would not. U2’s promised escrow also conflicts with the roadmap’s U3 programmable-escrow exit ([ROADMAP.md:23–24](/home/charl/Moriarty/ROADMAP.md:23)).

Of the five declared open items ([CATEGORY-MAP.md:249](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/CATEGORY-MAP.md:249)): n-party clearing needs a multi-intent **atomic stage** for shared state, since a join of disjoint footprints cannot clear a shared book; reserve that arity now. Flash loans need bounded ordered steps inside one atomic stage, not an Episode spanning transactions. A liquidation latency guarantee is an external liveness promise; encode triggers and remedies, not guaranteed inclusion. Competing slashes need a signed priority and unique-consumption rule; finite ordering is decidable once that rule is given. Policy state over reachability belongs in the core continuation contract now, because amendment and revocation already depend on it. Governance process itself can remain a library ([CATEGORY-MAP.md:151–165](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/CATEGORY-MAP.md:151)).

AI ergonomics are mixed. Keyword syntax, declared holes and distinct outcomes are useful specifications. “Minimal unsatisfied clause with a witness” is not generally available: a false disjunction needs multiple failed leaves, while a minimal unsatisfiable core is a separate solver computation and may expose private values. Return a typed failing obligation, authenticated input references and a replayable, disclosure-controlled counterexample when available; distinguish that from a solver-produced core. Per-clause operation counts can be static, but proof rows and time require a pinned whole-circuit cost model. Canonical bytes make identical **encodings** equal, not semantically equivalent formulas equal ([DESIGN.md:276–289](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/DESIGN.md:276)).

## Top three edits

1. **Replace §6’s exhaustiveness theorem** with an executable escrow transition relation: legitimate pending state, mutually exclusive terminal consumption, explicit late-result race policy, and recovery assumptions. Remove the claim that a deadline proves an available refund.
2. **Replace §§7–8 and §12’s fallback** with a signed-template refinement definition and one complete authenticated acceptance relation. Specify canonical bytes, signature locus, relation/key binding and exact in-circuit versus ledger checks; reject any unsupported clause instead of silently omitting it.
3. **Split §13’s U0 freeze** into a minimal versioned signed syntax and enforcement contract, then place advanced Φ solving and cost certification in U1, local linked escrow stages in U3, and portable/private Episode proof composition in U4. Include a small numeric node/byte/effect bound for the first circuit measurement.
