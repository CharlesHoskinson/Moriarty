# MIL/1 review — P1: type system and semantics

**Verdict.** MIL/1 has the right architectural direction: signed outcome constraints, bounded stages, explicit evidence, and persistent obligations fit Moriarty’s product contract. It is **not sound enough to freeze at U0**. The proposed monotonicity rule does not preserve financial guarantees; the escrow exit rule proves neither an exit nor a refund entitlement; and observation projections erase the evidence distinction on which the trust claim depends. U0 can freeze a smaller, typed intent and stage relation after these rules are made precise. The category map should then distinguish what that core can express from what its libraries and later milestones must establish. These are findings about the specified design; I ran no compiler, proof, or ledger checks.

## Findings and defects

1. **The completion theorem is undefined as written, and polarity checks the wrong property.** The claimed rule is `Φ_guarantee[σ] ⇒ Φ_guarantee`, but the right-hand formula still contains holes and has no stated semantics. If an unfilled intent denotes the union of its permitted completions, substitution is a refinement provided the filling satisfies its signed bounds; polarity is unnecessary. If it denotes fixed guarantees, those guarantees must be separate from the holes. More seriously, `refund = after(h)` is syntactically antitone as `h` increases: choosing a later `h` makes the predicate true in fewer states. Yet it removes the owner’s earlier recovery opportunity. Increasing a hole used as a `k_of_n` recovery threshold has the same shape. Thus antitonicity of a Boolean clause is **not** monotonicity of the signer’s financial protection. A hole in a `mulDiv` denominator additionally needs a positivity proof; a hole multiplied by a signed term has no fixed polarity; and a hole on both sides of a comparison needs relational reasoning, not a local sign. Define completion against the *whole acceptance relation*: `∀t. Accept(I[σ],t) ⇒ Authorized(I,t)`, with fixed signed hard constraints and checked hole bounds. 2. **The escrow “exhaustiveness” condition is false as a recovery rule.** `release ∨ refund ∨ after(deadline)` cannot hold in an ordinary funded, waiting state before the deadline unless release or refund is already enabled. If it is checked only after the deadline, `after(deadline)` can make the formula true while both transitions remain disabled. A timeout is a clock fact, not an exit, evidence of foreign nonexecution, or refund entitlement. The bridge example nevertheless sets `refund = after(deadline)`. Disjointness must apply to *enabled terminal transitions over authenticated state*, with an explicit pending state and a late-result race rule. Viable recovery requires a separately stated reachability claim under named availability assumptions; it cannot follow from this propositional formula. The lending example’s `refund never; deadline none` also contradicts the mandatory `deadline: Instant` form. 3. **Evidence non-laundering does not follow from the proposed type.** `Obs<T>` stores `evidence` as a field, but `obs.value` enters Φ as an ordinary `T`. On the stated grammar, `min(imported.value, anchored.value)` is just a term; a later predicate cannot recover which source influenced it. Nor does a Boolean `anchored(obs)` prove that a witness supplied the executing domain’s authenticated read. The necessary judgment is closer to `Γ ⊢ e : T ! Sources`, with source sets propagated through every operation and an indexed, verified evidence constructor bound to a specific proposition, domain, and policy. A local escrow record may be anchored evidence that *a foreign claim was recorded under policy P*; it is not anchored evidence that the foreign event occurred. Hole substitution, residual computation, and Episode edges must preserve that distinction. A type label alone cannot supply the required circuit constraint or ledger authentication. 4. **Φ is finite, but it is not yet a total Boolean language or a closed substitution language.** Checked subtraction can underflow; `mulDiv` can divide by zero or overflow its intermediate; `Instant + Duration` can overflow; and `fresh` needs `observedAt ≤ stageTime` on the *same clock*, or a future observation may count as fresh. Binary `min`/`max` have no empty-set problem in this grammar; a later aggregate would need a nonempty proof or an option result. Ratio cross-multiplication needs positive denominators—`1/0 ≤ 1/0` must not become the true inequality `0 ≤ 0`—and a specified intermediate width. Define evaluation as total into `Value | Reject(error)`, with rejection enforced by the acceptance relation. Typed, closed **value** substitutions with size and cost bounds can preserve Φ; arbitrary expression fillings need a substitution theorem and may exceed the signed tree bound. Finite-domain evaluation is decidable, but nonlinear satisfiability and static proof cost do not follow from “no recursion or negation.” The examples also use `not exercised`, `never`, and `failed(route)` without Φ rules; a verified terminal status or bounded negation is needed, since absence of evidence cannot mean failure. 5. **The nominal and dimensional types need repair.** Two deployed tokens can share domain, issuer, symbol, representation, and decimals; the proposed `Asset` tuple would identify them as one asset. Use an immutable ledger asset identifier, with symbol and decimals as checked metadata. `Share<P>` prevents cross-pool addition only if `P` names a *pool instance and share class*, not merely its program; the design never defines `Pool`, an authenticated share-balance cell, or how `Share<P>` becomes the asset accepted by `issue` and `supply`. The given `mulDiv` returns `Qty<A>` and cannot type the inverse deposit-to-shares conversion or a two-asset LP basket by itself. `Signed<A>` is said to be for positions only, then used for supply delta; use distinct `Delta<A>` and `Position<Instrument>` sorts. Otherwise positions in different instruments on ETH are mixed, despite the map’s “per instrument” invariant. The derivatives example also types an ETH position with `Price<ETH,USD>` while its USD equity needs USD-per-ETH under MIL’s base-per-quote convention. These are type errors, not missing implementation. 6. **The substructural rules and footprints do not compose yet.** A receipt must be allocated to at most one parallel branch; two branches cannot each type-check by seeing the same pre-fork context and then both consume it. At a fork, require a partition `Δ = Δ₁ ⊎ Δ₂` of linear resources, with a join that consumes each predecessor exactly once. Affine authority also needs a shared, nonduplicable *budget* across branches even when either branch may leave it unused. An encumbrance is a unique state claim with seizure and release transitions, not merely a receipt-like value. The advertised independence test is unsound for those resources because `Cell` omits receipts, encumbrances, authority allowance, positions, and share balances: two operations can have disjoint declared footprints while consuming the same receipt or collateral. Derive complete read/write sets from typed effects and prove symbolic keys disjoint after holes are filled. 7. **Conservation and liability separation are stated more strongly than their rules support.** `Σ Δbalance(d,A) = Δsupply(d,A)` is the right equation only when the quantified accounts include escrow custody and reserves; otherwise funding an escrow removes spendable balance without an accounted destination. For each owner and asset, require `Σ active locks ≤ balance` and `spend ≤ balance − Σ active locks`; checking each encumbrance separately permits double pledging. The obligation roll-forward omits the design’s own `create`, `forgive`, and `transfer` operations. Forgiveness needs creditor authority and a distinct event from repayment; creation and enforcement need a debtor-consented commitment to exact terms and policy. Separate `Obligation` and `Asset` constructors help, but no stated typing or transition rule prevents minting a tokenized debt claim without its liability, or treating default as discharge. The category map’s claim that one encumbrance can back several obligations directly contradicts `against: ObligationId`. ## Missing rules and metatheory

**MIL/1 must contain** nominal asset, pool, and instrument identities; typed deltas and unit-preserving arithmetic; definedness and failure judgments; indexed evidence with provenance-preserving substitution; debtor consent; complete custody accounting; linear fork/join contexts; and exclusive terminal transitions. **Libraries can contain** particular vault formulas, AMM invariants, option payoffs, and governance voting, once those primitives exist. **Later milestones can demonstrate** multi-signer clearing, bounded intra-stage lending callbacks, cross-domain compensation, and native history proofs; U0 should reserve their stage and authority semantics without claiming their behavior. The roadmap assigns native primitive evidence to U1 and end-to-end escrow evidence to U2/U3. [ROADMAP.md:21](/home/charl/Moriarty/ROADMAP.md:21), [ROADMAP.md:24](/home/charl/Moriarty/ROADMAP.md:24), [DESIGN.md:301](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/intent-language-design-2026-09-28/DESIGN.md:301).

Demand preservation of typed values **and linear contexts** across transitions; progress to a value, explicit rejection, or authenticated pending continuation within the stage bound; per-domain conservation and obligation roll-forward as invariants; completion refinement; and evidence non-laundering. Error-aware preservation, progress, and accounting are plausible K reachability/induction targets after the rules exist. **Completion monotonicity and non-laundering are false as currently stated.** Proving them for source semantics would still not establish that ZKIRv3 constrains every witness field or that Midnight binds the resulting effects: source-to-target correspondence, native constraints, signatures, and ledger reads need separate evidence. ## Disagreements and ranked edits

Of the five declared open items, **n-party clearing** cannot be supplied by a join requiring disjoint footprints when participants share a pool or order book; reserve a bounded multi-intent atomic-stage form. **Flash loans** are not excluded by atomic publication: a bounded internal effect trace with a final repayment invariant is compatible with a finite stage. **Liquidation latency** cannot be guaranteed by a language without chain inclusion or actor liveness; specify an enforcement window as safety, not a completion promise. **Competing slashes** over a bounded set are decidable; their priority and allocation are a policy choice that should be fixed or rejected as ambiguous. **Versioned policy state** belongs in U0’s transition relation because in-flight protection depends on it; governance process can remain a library. I also disagree that the entire Φ and category algebra can be frozen as one U0 item before their typing and failure rules are specified. 1. **Replace §7** with a semantics for unfilled intents, typed bounded substitutions, fixed signed hard constraints, and a whole-acceptance refinement obligation; remove polarity as the claimed guarantee.
2. **Replace §§3.4 and 6** with indexed, authenticated evidence and explicit pending/terminal escrow transitions, including late-result exclusivity and a separately qualified recovery claim.
