# Agent finance and execution kernels — bounded market review

As of 19 September 2026, existing systems already cover substantial parts of Moriarty's proposed purpose. A claim that nobody binds agent authority to execution, offers confidential agents, or routes outcome-based multichain actions would be false or unsupported. This review did not establish one deployed platform combining the full requested package: a permissionless programming language for authenticated formal intentions, bounded compilation to Midnight ZKIR v3, private staged settlement with residual obligations, and explicit ZK/MPC/TEE federation. That is a bounded evidence result, not proof of market absence or of Moriarty's implementation readiness.

Scope: 12 search queries across three rounds; 14 selected primary-source acquisition attempts, 13 successful substantive sources. Sources were captured with Scrapling. PACE's 18 PDF pages were rendered with PixelRAG; pages 6 and 13 were inspected visually. Reading ranges, exact source hashes and claim locators are in `sources.json`, `claims.json`, and `reading-coverage.json`. Publication dates are distinguished from the retrieval date. Current documentation is evidence of what vendors document, not independent deployment verification. No transactions, benchmarks or attacks were executed.

## What already exists

**NEAR** is strong competition for intent settlement plus distributed signing. Its current overview describes accepted solver quotes reaching the verifier. Chain Signatures supplies MPC signatures for external accounts, but the documentation explicitly separates outbound signing from external-state access and warns about network replay binding. A returned signature is not foreign-chain finality. [Intents](https://docs.near.org/chain-abstraction/intents/overview), [Chain Signatures](https://docs.near.org/chain-abstraction/chain-signatures).

The earlier code bank at `/home/charl/research/near-teardown-2026-09-19/studies/intents-kernel/` reviewed near/intents commit `32a7836f825e8c984c26149f4456793ec7e3d49a`: fixed intent handlers, authenticated payloads, internal balances/deltas and asynchronous external calls. Its distinction between verifier-transaction atomicity and later withdrawal/bridge settlement still matters. This is reused historical pin evidence, not a claim that current docs were matched to a newly checked repository head.

**Giza/ARMA** spans different guarantees. The older agents framework waits for a prediction proof before contract interaction. ARMA's security documentation describes restricted session keys; its current hub offers account/lifecycle/yield-management tools and partner-credential onboarding. These demonstrate inference verification and bounded delegation, not their equivalence to a proof that every execution refines an owner's complete financial intention. The security mirror may lag the current product, whose alternative endpoint failed acquisition. A hosted API credential requirement is not proof that the underlying language is permissioned. [Agents](https://github.com/gizatechxyz/giza-agents), [Security](https://giza.mintlify.app/docs/security), [Hub](https://github.com/gizatechxyz/giza-hub).

**Enso** competes directly with route construction and outcome-oriented orchestration. Its August 25 article discusses combining solvers, public liquidity, RFQ and protocol calls, checking simulated outcomes, and failures caused by stale quotes or changing cross-chain conditions. Simulation is valuable execution engineering; it is not a theorem of global best execution or of the eventual inclusion-state result. [Enso](https://blog.enso.build/intents-solvers-rfq-and-fillers-where-enso-fits-in-onchain-execution/).

**LI.FI** offers agent integration and Composer's atomic multi-call execution on a supported EVM chain. Its status vocabulary supplies a concrete semantic trap: `DONE/PARTIAL` can mean receiving a different token, while `DONE/REFUNDED` means funds returned. A proof-consuming application must interpret these outcomes against the authorized branch instead of treating a generic success flag as satisfaction. Cross-chain progress remains staged. [Composer](https://docs.li.fi/composer/overview), [Agent integration](https://docs.li.fi/agents/overview).

**Phala** is the strongest selected operational overlap with confidential agent federation. It documents compose-hash-bound credentials, attested peers, signed action logs and a wallet integration, with production counts reported by the vendor. Those counts were not independently checked. Its inference documentation is more precise: receipts bind request/response hashes to a workload and record provider verification. External tools remain outside the TEE boundary. Attesting the program that sent a payment does not establish that its recipient delivered a promised service or that the program implements the owner's intended policy correctly. [Agents](https://phala.com/solutions/ai-agents), [Confidential AI](https://docs.phala.com/phala-cloud/confidential-ai/overview).

**Flashbots/SUAVE** remains a major architectural comparator. The February 14 roadmap describes entering replicated privacy and experimenting with modular distributed building; global permissionless building is a later phase. It would be incorrect to turn that roadmap into evidence of a fully deployed global SUAVE network. Its emphasis on private competitive construction overlaps the solver side of Moriarty, without establishing the specified language or target compiler. [Updated roadmap](https://writings.flashbots.net/decentralized-building-wat-do).

**EigenCloud** argues for neutral verification, attested execution and economic accountability for agent payments. This is a real competing purpose, not merely inference infrastructure. However, the May 14 article calls EigenVerify proposed, and its stronger statements about unique architectural sufficiency, regulation and competitors are vendor assertions, not findings adopted here. A slashable operator needs an objectively adjudicable predicate and evidence; collateral does not invent either. The selected EigenAI PDF URL returned 404 and was not reviewed, so inference-proof details remain an explicit gap. [Agentic accountability](https://www.eigenlabs.org/blog/agentic-payments-accountability/).

## Closest counterposition: PACE

The August 18 preprint proposes typed transaction intents, a deterministic policy verifier, signed policy/simulation/calldata records and on-chain account enforcement. This directly defeats a broad novelty claim about separating an untrusted agent from a deterministic authorization layer. Its own limits are useful: trusted field decoding and simulation, byte fidelity rather than outcome binding, and admitted harmful sequences whose individual legs satisfy policy. Page 13 reports all eight policy-valid harmful cases admitted, including a five-leg drain under per-leg limits. These are scoped research results, not a reproduced deployment assessment. [PACE paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17220v1), especially pages 4–6 and 13.

The strongest existing-system counterproposal is compositional: a PACE-like authority boundary, NEAR signing/settlement or LI.FI/Enso execution, and Phala/EigenCloud verification services could meet many practical agent-finance requirements. Moriarty needs to demonstrate why a certified language relation adds a property this assembled stack lacks. Merely describing that combination with new vocabulary is insufficient differentiation.

## Concrete security and proof obligations for the proposed language

The following are research requirements inferred from the comparison, not claims of completed Moriarty proofs:

1. **Authenticated meaning.** Bind owner identity, canonical policy/program version, permitted branch, domain and resource envelope to authorization. An LLM-generated policy is not authenticated user consent merely because it parses.
2. **Semantic refinement.** Prove that accepted execution satisfies every mandatory predicate of the selected authorized branch. Prove valid witnesses separately for non-vacuity. Byte hashes alone cannot show that a typed spend field faithfully denotes decoded calldata and actual effects.
3. **Cumulative authority.** Carry consumed budget, live allowances, outstanding commitments and replay state across stages. Five individually permitted spends must not reset a ten-unit total budget. Include fees and reservations, with concurrency-safe accounting.
4. **Staged settlement.** Source-chain acceptance, signature production, broadcasting, destination finality, delivery and compensation are distinct states. A failure after an irrevocable step leaves a residual obligation; it cannot invoke simulated global rollback. A refund may close a compensation branch without satisfying the purchase branch.
5. **Evidence-specific federation.** A ZK proof certifies a stated relation; an MPC signature authenticates a payload under threshold assumptions; TEE evidence binds workload identity under hardware and provisioning assumptions. Compose their statements explicitly rather than treating all three as interchangeable proof of financial correctness.
6. **Recursive statements.** Initial proof must establish authenticated policy, starting resources and empty/declared obligations. Each recursive step must preserve authority and conservation while updating obligations, domains and evidence freshness. Terminal proof must close the selected branch's obligations. Reject a proof whose policy/circuit/version/domain differs even when its mathematical verification succeeds.
7. **Private and permissionless participation.** Public statements must expose enough to prevent double use and verify obligations without disclosing unrelated secrets. Permissionless developers and solvers can submit bounded programs/witnesses; that does not require unrestricted access to every private datum or delegated credential.

A bounded language helps constrain resource use and establish termination. It cannot make an incomplete policy economically complete, make an oracle truthful, guarantee an external service, or ensure solver liveness. Midnight ZKIR v3 lowering, runtime correspondence, public-input binding and adapter finality all require their own evidence. None was established by this market review.
