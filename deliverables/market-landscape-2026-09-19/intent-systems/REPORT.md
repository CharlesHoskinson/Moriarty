# Intent-centric systems closest to Moriarty

**Anoma is the strongest direct counterposition; Valence is the strongest proof-driven orchestration comparator; Pint is the closest declarative-language comparator in this track.** CoW and UniswapX already solve the narrower commercial problem of letting users sign desired swap outcomes while independent optimizers find execution paths. Moriarty should not claim invention of intent programming, proof-based intent enforcement, private resource transactions or solver separation. The potentially distinctive target is a particular integrated assurance contract, especially complete staged duties and correspondence to actual Midnight ZKIRv3—not a category without competitors.

## The comparison target

Moriarty's intended product is a permissionless programming language for supported financial programs, compiling to Midnight ZKIRv3 and carrying proof of signed formal intention. It is intended to support bounded accepted stages, recursive compliance, private evidence, persistent obligations through partial outcomes, conditional multichain delivery and adversarial AI solvers. Conditional settlement here means submitting/funding at a destination, then waiting for the specified combination of signatures, documents, proofs, recipient action and other supported conditions; recorded funding is not final delivery. The proposed kernel combines ZK, MPC and TEE mechanisms under separate trust assumptions.

Three comparisons must stay separate: (1) an existing system expresses the same desired outcome, (2) it proves or checks that outcome under its own semantics, and (3) it establishes the full source/target/ledger theorem claimed for Moriarty. Existing systems can win the first two without using Midnight. Conversely, a different target chain alone does not make Moriarty an original financial-intent architecture.

## Anoma / ARM / Juvix: direct proof-based intent prior art

The ARM specification explicitly separates balance proofs, compliance proofs and resource-logic proofs for user constraints. Users provide unbalanced transactions and solvers compose balanced ones; ordering and state update occur separately. Privacy depends on the ARM instantiation, with a shielded flavor defined to hide both data and function. This is stronger overlap than a solver merely signing a route. It already occupies the central idea of expressing constraints and accepting proof-backed satisfactions. **I01–I03.** [ARM specification](https://specs.anoma.net/main/arch/system/state/resource_machine/index.html)

Anoma is also no longer fairly summarized using only its 2025 devnet announcements. The adapter page, dated February 18, 2026, lists Ethereum, Arbitrum, Base and Optimism mainnets and links contracts/transactions. AnomaPay documentation reports live V1 shielded payments and proof-verifying adapter custody across listed EVM networks. These are current official deployment/product claims; this study did not independently inspect chain state or bytecode. The April 2 update label must not be used to date every currently listed network launch. Multiple deployed adapters also do not establish atomic arbitrary multichain workflows. **I04–I05.** [Protocol adapter](https://docs.anoma.net/anoma-protocol-adapter), [AnomaPay](https://docs.anoma.net/anoma-pay-introduction)

Juvix defaults to termination, coverage and positivity checks, but its tutorial documents `terminating` as a bypass of termination checking. A restricted certified profile could disallow that escape; the unrestricted language cannot be advertised as automatically providing Moriarty's intended bounded-execution discipline. Totality would still not supply practical cost bounds. This track has not established the exact deployed compiler path from Juvix to each ARM implementation, a general signed-intention envelope or recursively complete business-duty lineage. These are questions for source review, not evidence that Anoma cannot support them. **I06.** [Juvix totality tutorial](https://docs.juvix.org/latest/tutorials/learn)

**Strongest counterclaim:** Anoma already provides the core generalized intent/resource/proof architecture and real private-payment deployments; Moriarty might be a stricter financial profile, language frontend or Midnight adapter rather than an entirely new system. Test that possibility before choosing a new foundation.

## Essential / Pint: language-level outcome constraints

Pint is a constraint-modeling language over decision variables that can represent blockchain state at different times. Contracts and predicates are content-addressed. Proposed atomic mutations must satisfy an identified predicate, and a contract adding restrictions to another contract must also satisfy the state owner's constraints. This directly overlaps writing financial intentions as permitted state transitions rather than imperative routing code. “Executionless” here does not mean no computation: Boolean constraints are still checked. **I07–I08.** [Pint](https://docs.essential.builders/protocol-overview/pint.md), [Contracts and predicates](https://docs.essential.builders/protocol-overview/contracts-predicates-and-constraints.md)

The current build guide labels the protocol very early development, with missing tooling and frequent changes. Older search results refer to pre-alpha devnet/testnet, but the old documentation paths have moved and the official 2024 blog capture failed at the origin with HTTP 525. This corpus therefore does not establish current live network status. Its language documents also do not establish ZK privacy, recursive proof carriage or a Midnight compiler theorem; that is a scoped evidence gap, not a claim these are impossible extensions. **I09–I10.** [Build guide](https://docs.essential.builders/introduction/build-on-essential.md)

**Strongest counterclaim:** If Moriarty's essential purpose is a typed language in which solvers satisfy financial constraints, Pint already offers that design direction. Moriarty's stronger security obligations must become concrete certified semantics rather than a longer feature list.

## Valence / Timewave: proven messages plus multidomain workflows

Valence programs arrange accounts and libraries across domains, but each subroutine executes in one domain. Atomic and non-atomic subroutines, fine-grained authorization and callback-oriented processing are explicit. Backend maturity differs: CosmWasm documents queued execution with permissionless ticking, retries and expiration; EVM's implemented Lite Processor runs immediately, while the full queued processor is scaffolded. The reviewed material establishes documented mechanisms, not independently verified current mainnet deployment. **I11–I12.** [Programs and authorizations](https://docs.valence.zone/components/programs_and_authorizations.html)

The ZK path is a close architectural competitor: a registry selects verification material; proof public output carries a processor message; an SP1 switch verifies both program execution and domain evidence. Recursive domain-history proofs and root pinning are documented. Replay checking can be optional, and a zero authorization-contract address can intentionally allow multiple destinations. Those policy choices must not be silently described as universal binding. Program-specific submitter permissions also do not automatically imply a project-wide developer gate. **I13–I14.** [On-chain integration](https://docs.valence.zone/zk/03_onchain_integration.html), [System overview](https://docs.valence.zone/zk/01_system_overview.html)

Full state encoders and cross-chain coordination are expressly described as active-development goals, with simpler current templates. A conceptual example checks a signature in the controller, then has the circuit run a trusted transfer without carrying that check into the shown witness relation. Under an adversarial witness generator, that isolated circuit would not establish consent. This is a documentation-level negative witness—not a demonstrated deployed exploit, and not proof that a larger attested construction cannot bind controller execution. It illustrates precisely why “a ZK proof was generated” is weaker than the full intended acceptance theorem. **I15–I16.** [State encoders and implementation status](https://docs.valence.zone/zk/07_state_encoding_and_encoders.html)

**Strongest counterclaim:** The proposed Moriarty kernel may substantially duplicate Valence's separation of authorization, proven off-chain computation, domain evidence and processor dispatch. A useful distinction would be an explicit total financial language, complete adverse-outcome accounting and verified intent-to-target correspondence; those need demonstrations, not assertions.

## CoW and UniswapX: deployed-protocol substitutes for narrower needs

CoW's core settlement documentation checks order authentication, expiry, remaining fillability and price constraints, including partial fills and arbitrary solver interactions. Its `appData` is signed but not otherwise enforced by that core contract; attaching documentary or business meaning there does not make it an acceptance predicate. Settlement solvers are allowlisted and bonded. Historical fill mapping entries may be cleared after expiry, so a missing entry is not evidence of never having filled. These are concrete runtime safeguards and lifecycle limits, not a general proof-carrying PL. This track consulted current production-contract documentation but did not independently verify deployment addresses or usage. **I17–I19.** [CoW settlement](https://docs.cow.fi/cow-protocol/reference/contracts/core/settlement)

UniswapX permits users to sign auction/output constraints and lets fillers choose strategies to satisfy them. Its documented public-order flow is permissionless for filling. The current official deployment page lists mainnet and other network contracts, with metadata updated September 16, 2026. This is enough to reject any novelty claim for permissionless signed-outcome execution, but it does not establish a general private contingent-settlement language or cross-chain atomicity merely from having multiple deployments. **I20–I21.** [UniswapX overview](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/liquidity/uniswapx/overview), [Deployments](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/liquidity/uniswapx/deployments)

For customers who only need token swaps with price bounds, these protocols are practical substitutes for a broad new language. For complex documentary escrow, residual liabilities and private multistage financial contracts, the fit is less direct. AI is not intrinsically a new acceptance mechanism: a filler or solver may use AI while the protocol still checks an ordinary signed order. Proving optimizer quality, evidence truth or faithful translation from human instructions is an additional problem.

## What remains credible for Moriarty

The corpus does not establish an existing implementation of the **entire conjunction** of Moriarty's intended guarantees, including actual Midnight ZKIRv3 correspondence. It also does not justify uniqueness: Anoma overlaps at the central proof-based intent abstraction, Pint at the language abstraction, and Valence at proof-driven orchestration and recursive state evidence. The integration itself may be engineering composition rather than new theory. **I22.**

Use one demanding benchmark across these systems: a private buyer/seller workflow with standing scoped delegation, a destination-funded escrow, documentary and recipient predicates, two partial fills, capped contingent fees, a late callback after recovery begins, and an authorized semantic upgrade. Ask each candidate to identify exactly what its proof or runtime enforces, what the ledger prevents, which principal can bypass checks, what survives each failure and what remains assumed. Include forged genesis, omitted liabilities, wrong verifier, stale domain root and replayed effects. A working existing-system implementation would defeat broad differentiation claims; a demonstrable missing theorem or unusable restriction would support a precise contribution.
