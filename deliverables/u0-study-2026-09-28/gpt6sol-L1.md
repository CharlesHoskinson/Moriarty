# U0 proposal — L1 semantics

## 1. Goal

U0 should give U1 and U2 one versioned meaning for a financial stage: how source becomes Core, how Core combines with a signed intention and authenticated execution context, what each of the six judgments checks, and what each result commits or rejects. It should freeze the **first supported slice** and record the meaning and owner of every field in the wider target relation. U1 can then certify the arithmetic and test native feasibility against that slice; U2 can enforce it through a pinned source-to-Midnight path. That division follows the roadmap’s distinct U0, U1 and U2 exits ([ROADMAP.md:19](/home/charl/Moriarty/ROADMAP.md:19)).

I reran the stage, K and exit checkers read-only; all passed with those open counts. The stage checker verifies declarations and profile membership, not semantic adequacy ([check_u0_stage_schema.py:1](/home/charl/Moriarty/scripts/check_u0_stage_schema.py:1)). These are sound inventories, not demonstrations of the missing relation.

- **The 84-leaf denominator mixes different kinds of value.** The embedding record names source/5 and lifecycle Core/1, yet all 12 `signedIntent` leaves, all 12 `judgments` leaves, circuit identity, domain and lifecycle IDs are absent from those profiles (`source-core-embeddings.json` `$.rows`). That identifies an integration gap, but it does not mean compiler pins, ledger context or derived verdicts should become source syntax. The current classification offers only a source citation and a Core citation per leaf ([check_u0_stage_schema.py:63](/home/charl/Moriarty/scripts/check_u0_stage_schema.py:63)). It needs a third dimension: **who supplies the value and where its binding is checked**.

- **The schema is a shape, not yet a financial statement.** `signedIntent.grossDebitCap`, `feeCap` and `minNetOutcome` are unqualified decimal strings; the pattern even permits negative and noncanonical spellings ([stage-relation.schema.json:116](/home/charl/Moriarty/openspec/changes/consolidated-language-kernel/schemas/stage-relation.schema.json:116)). Effect lines have asset, account and amount but no encoded debit/credit direction or account-domain type ([stage-relation.schema.json:270](/home/charl/Moriarty/openspec/changes/consolidated-language-kernel/schemas/stage-relation.schema.json:270)). The six `status` fields can say `held`, `violated` or `unchecked`; the schema does not derive those claims ([stage-relation.schema.json:553](/home/charl/Moriarty/openspec/changes/consolidated-language-kernel/schemas/stage-relation.schema.json:553)).

- **The judgments need executable rules.** `judgments.json` assigns fields and prose definitions to stage, intent, effect, authority, history and failure, but states no inference rule, accepted-result condition or precedence between rejection and committed partial failure ([judgments.json:5](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/u0-semantic-contract-2026-09-23/judgments.json:5), [judgments.json:109](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/u0-semantic-contract-2026-09-23/judgments.json:109)). The existing `/5` path can prepare Transfer, Repay, Originate and Accrue, but explicitly uses local state and performs no K execution, proof or public transaction ([financial-agreement-source-v5.md:37](/home/charl/Moriarty/experiments/moriarty-language/spec/successor/financial-agreement-source-v5.md:37), [financial-agreement-source-v5.md:49](/home/charl/Moriarty/experiments/moriarty-language/spec/successor/financial-agreement-source-v5.md:49)). This is a useful source/Core starting point.

- **K provides a real, narrower reference.** Recorded K execution compared complete state, effects, work, allowance, IDs and rollback for a four-step loan; it makes no general theorem or Midnight claim ([RESULT.md:3](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/k-lifecycle-execution-2026-09-17/RESULT.md:3), [RESULT.md:32](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/k-lifecycle-execution-2026-09-17/RESULT.md:32)). The reconciliation correctly marks effect, authority and failure partial, and stage, intent and history uncovered (`k-reconciliation.json` `$.rows[?id=="stage"|"intent"|"history"]`). Its UNI rows also ask K to cover a public pipeline and native backend ([k-reconciliation.json:17](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/u0-semantic-contract-2026-09-23/k-reconciliation.json:17), [k-reconciliation.json:211](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/u0-semantic-contract-2026-09-23/k-reconciliation.json:211)); those require other evidence owners.

- **Repayment exposes the risk of freezing examples as laws.** The `/5` loan example contains fixture-specific balance and debt `ensures` ([loan-lifecycle.mori:118](/home/charl/Moriarty/experiments/moriarty-language/spec/successor/examples/loan-lifecycle.mori:118)). Treat that as a **provisional defect reproducer and proposed repair**, then replay it through the actual evaluator and K before accepting a semantic freeze. The current source already distinguishes checked lifecycle values from signed authority ([financial-lifecycle.ts:4](/home/charl/Moriarty/experiments/moriarty-language/src/successor/financial-lifecycle.ts:4)).

## 4. Exit distinction

**U0 contract frozen for the supported slice** should require typed field origins, canonical bytes, six executable local judgments with explicit premises, source/Core/K agreement on the transfer and repayment controls, and a current target matrix that labels unverified pins and trust premises. U0 must **demonstrate local semantic behavior**, not merely list declarations. It need not show native signature verification, ZKIR soundness or Preview settlement; U1 owns the native primitive/signature feasibility evidence, and U2 owns general supported single-stage source-to-ledger enforcement. U3 owns retained phase effects and recovery; U4 owns full recursive and private history. A deferred field must have a named owner and cannot be silently accepted in an earlier slice.

## 5. Risks and things not to do

Do not fill all 66 absent leaves with source keywords or strings just to improve the count. Do not allow a supplied `judgments.*.status="held"` to serve as proof of its predicate. Do not count a K function citation, matching fixture or checker pass as whole-judgment coverage; the current K checker itself limits its claim to citations and quotes ([check_u0_k_reconciliation.py:21](/home/charl/Moriarty/scripts/check_u0_k_reconciliation.py:21)). Do not turn fixture constants into universal financial laws, and do not hide a debit above its gross cap behind refunds or netting. Preserve the original U0 receipt and K evidence as historical records when issuing a successor gate.

## 6. Disagreements

I disagree with reading “84 source/Core embeddings” as “84 fields must appear in source/Core.” Deployment identity, authenticated ledger context and derived verdicts belong to the assembled statement and its acceptance boundary. I also disagree with treating 23/23 K rows as a U0 completion target: a permissionless public pipeline, native proof correspondence and release assurance cannot be established by K execution. Keep those rows visible and uncovered, while defining honest **slice-level K coverage** for the semantic predicates K can execute. Finally, the current exit checker’s success should retain its precise label, “contract recorded, capabilities open,” until the local slice runs ([EXIT-GATE.md:20](/home/charl/Moriarty/deliverables/u0-semantic-contract-2026-09-23/EXIT-GATE.md:20)).

## 7. Top three recommendations

1. **Define field origin and executable judgment rules before adding syntax.** This resolves what an embedding must actually preserve.
2. **Demonstrate one fee-bearing transfer and one liability-changing repayment through source, Core and K**, including hostile controls and the reported repayment defect.
3. **Split the U0 gate into frozen slice, recorded full target and demonstrated downstream capabilities**, with explicit U1–U4 owners for every deferred predicate.
