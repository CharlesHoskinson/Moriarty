## 2. What the kernel abstraction requires for this category

The DeFi Kernel states the bridge category's abstractions in three places that must be kept apart: (a) the Lean kernel's own state/transition model, (b) the Atlas's element/bond/law vocabulary, and (c) the kernel's own roadmap, which classifies most of (b) as *not yet built*. Honest comparison needs all three.

### 2.1 The kernel's state model makes the domain the first index — and then confines every operation to one domain

`README.md:23` — "The ledger records a nonnegative balance for each **domain, account, and asset**. A domain identifies an execution context, such as a modeled chain."

The transition checker then **requires** a single-domain footprint per operation:

`lean/DefiKernel/Typed/Transition.lean:127-131`
```
/-- Required state reads and actual net movement stay within the invocation domain.
Environment observations may explicitly refer to other domains; their truth is adapter supplied. -/
def Evaluated.domainOK (e : Evaluated Party Asset Domain) (domain : Domain) : Bool :=
  decide ((∀ c ∈ e.requiredStateReads, c.1 = domain) ∧
    (∀ c, e.effect c ≠ 0 → c.1 = domain) ∧ ∀ d a, e.supply d a ≠ 0 → d = domain)
```

and conservation is likewise *per domain*:

`lean/DefiKernel/Typed/Transition.lean:145-146`
```
def Evaluated.accountingOK (e : Evaluated Party Asset Domain) : Bool :=
  decide (∀ d a, ∑ p, e.effect (d, p, a) = e.supply d a)
```
(`lean/DefiKernel/Typed/Types.lean:36-38` defines `total` per `(domain, asset)` the same way.)

**The consequence is the category's central abstraction requirement.** A cross-domain transfer cannot be one template. It is at minimum two transitions — a source-domain debit with negative supply and a destination-domain credit with positive supply — and the kernel's conservation check *cannot see that they are the same transfer*. Whatever links them must be a new relation above the transition, over two domains. The comment at `:128` says explicitly that foreign-domain truth is "adapter supplied", i.e. a trust input, not a checked fact.

### 2.2 Atomic settlement deliberately refuses to net across domains

`lean/DefiKernel/Atomic/Policy.lean:8-15` keys a settlement `Lane` by `(domain, asset, vault)`; `:58` detects duplicate lanes on `(domain, asset)`. `lean/DefiKernel/Atomic/Execution.lean:138-139` commits only when the residual list is empty, otherwise `.aborted … (.unsettled remaining)`.

The fixture is explicit: `lean/DefiKernel/Atomic/Examples.lean:186-187, 204-209, 221-222` draws USD on `.main` and returns USD on `.other`, with `domainPolicy` tracking both lanes; the expected result at `lean/DefiKernel/Atomic/Tests.lean:102-103` (`"atomic.fixture.settlement.cross.domain"`) is **abort** with residuals `[⟨usdLane, .alice, 7⟩, ⟨otherLane, .alice, -7⟩]`. A value return in another domain does not discharge an obligation in the source domain. The kernel models cross-domain value return as *unsettled*, by construction.

## 4. Category verdict

**Verdict: absent as language, partial as design, and the design's best content is not in the contract.** Moriarty's stage relation names a domain but does not use it: `domain.{chainId,domainId,stateFrameRef}` is a single stage-level tag (`schema:96-115`) over an effects ledger whose lines carry no domain at all (`schema:280-300, 352-375`), and all three leaves are `absent` in source/Core and `NOT_ENFORCED`. Against the kernel's ledger, which is indexed by `(domain, account, asset)` and enforces per-domain conservation and a single-domain footprint (`README.md:23`; `Transition.lean:129-131, 146`), Moriarty is one abstraction short: it can say *which* domain a stage ran in, not *that value moved between two*. None of the Atlas's category vocabulary — `Xf{form}`, `Xm{trust_domain}`, `Of` with a bonded filler and a dispute window, L8/L9/L18/L19/L25/L26/L29, X7/X12/X13/X19, the §15 spectrum as a typed level — appears in the language, the schema, the judgments or ROADMAP's evidence columns. What Moriarty does have is strong and should not be undervalued: the anchored-versus-imported rule (`defi-kernel-multichain-2026-09-11/REPORT.md:169`) is the correct destination-side abstraction and is stated more sharply than anything in the Atlas; the refusal to promise cross-chain atomicity (`CONSOLIDATED-DESIGN.md:72`; `sdk-interface-design.md:291, 409`) is exactly §15's discipline; the stranding rule with `E_RECOVERY_UNSAFE` (`sdk-interface-design.md:844, 1076`) has no defiformal counterpart at all; and the residue discipline (B17) exceeds the Atlas's. But all of that lives in two September 11–12 research artifacts and a design doc, none of which the U0 semantic contract cites. The fair charge is therefore not "Moriarty ignored bridges" — it is that Moriarty already did this thinking and then froze a contract that does not carry it. Scope boundaries must also be granted in both directions: S0 is deliberately one domain and one settlement asset (`UNIFIED-PROPOSAL.md:71`), Moriarty's target is explicitly Midnight-centric with a single-lineage ledger-induced history profile (`ROADMAP.md:34`; `CONSOLIDATED-DESIGN.md:79`), and defiformal has not built this category either — its `Async/` (P26) and `CrossDomain/` (P29) packages are Planned and the directories do not exist (`openspec/ROADMAP.md:55, 58`; confirmed by `ls lean/DefiKernel/`).

## 5. Gaps that would change the language design

Ranked by how much new language/kernel/judgment/numeric-profile/enforcement machinery they require, not by financial importance. Library or example work is excluded.

1. **Asset identity must become a typed record carrying domain, issuer and representation form.** Today `effects.*[].asset` is `type: string` (`schema:291-293, 363-365`) and the implemented language enforces the *opposite* — one unit name per settlement asset (`docs/MORIARTY-LANGUAGE-REQUIREMENTS-ALIGNMENT.md:8`). Moriarty's own research already found this blocking (`defi-kernel-multichain-2026-09-11/REPORT.md:179`; `sdk-interface-design.md:40`). This is a type-system change that reaches the numeric profile (unit dimensions, `UNIFIED-PROPOSAL.md:146`), the S0 law set (E1's per-asset conservation becomes per-(domain,asset)), the intent's `assetIdentities[]`, and the signing display. Nothing downstream — B2, B3, B8, L25, X12, X19 — is expressible until this lands. It is also the prerequisite for defiformal's ledger key, so it is the single highest-leverage change in this category.
2. **A cross-domain settlement relation spanning two stages, with a paired-obligation judgment.** The kernel's own structure forces this: per-domain conservation cannot link a source burn to a destination mint (`Transition.lean:129-131, 146`), and the atomic settlement policy treats a cross-domain return as *unsettled* by construction (`Atomic/Policy.lean:8-15`; `Atomic/Tests.lean:102-103`). Moriarty's current relation is single-stage and single-domain, and its research explicitly declines the equality (`sdk-interface-design.md:409`). Discharging L8/L9 and X7's independent supply invariant needs a new object — a bridge/transfer identity with a source-debit commitment and a destination-credit obligation, plus a seventh judgment (or an extension of `history`) that carries it — not a library. Note that defiformal owes the same object to P29.
3. **`observations[].finality` and `domain.*` must become enumerated, discharge-bearing types, not free strings.** This is blocking in a precise way that I did not expect to find: `UNIFIED-PROPOSAL.md:119` restricts S3 judgment clauses to "linear integer arithmetic and set membership over relation paths". A free-string `finality` is not in that fragment, so **no S3 clause can ever evaluate the finality of an observation** as the contract currently stands. L18's "explicit finality" and L1's "named finality assumption" therefore need a schema change (an enumerated finality level with its dispute/challenge window as an integer, along the lines of `sdk-interface-design.md:53` `Final`/`Contingent`) before the U0 mechanism can express them at all. The same applies to `failurePolicy`'s three free strings (`schema:510-528`) if a §15 atomicity level is ever to be checked.
4. **An evidence-class discriminator on observations, enforcing "an imported fact may never be presented as an anchored one."** Moriarty has the rule (`defi-kernel-multichain-2026-09-11/REPORT.md:169`) and the analogous rule for lineage (UNI-009, `spec.md:92`: "ledger induction SHALL NOT count as native recursive or private split/join evidence"), but the stage relation has no leaf that distinguishes an authenticated local state read from a threshold-attested foreign claim. This is enforcement machinery — it must fail closed in the effect judgment, not in the router — because the whole X7 hazard is a destination accepting an unverified source assertion. It also needs a replay/nullifier discipline for imported statements distinct from `authority.replayState`.
5. **A typed atomicity level on the stage, with per-level repair obligations.** `CONSOLIDATED-DESIGN.md:72` already enumerates Moriarty's five composition modes and forbids the §15 error; turning that into an enumerated field with an obligation ("anything claiming atomicity below same-transaction names its repair bond", `ELEMENT-TABLE:1025`) is a schema + judgment change, and it is the cheapest of these five. MPLR-024 owns the requirement and is `specified-only`.
6. **A contingent-reimbursement claim with a bonded underwriter and a dispute clock** (`Of`/L19/B6). Ranked lowest of the language-level items because `liabilities.{opening,closing}[]` already carries `{liabilityId, debtor, creditor, asset, amount}` (`schema:378-400`), so the *claim* is half-expressible. What is not expressible is the bond, the slash condition, the challenge window and (L29) the residual claimant of the spread. The dispute clock in particular is language-level, since it interacts with `signedIntent.validity` and `failurePolicy`; the bond and slash could plausibly start as a library over an existing liability type.

Explicitly **not** listed as language gaps: bridge-hazard *thresholds* (X13's "exceeding slashable stake") — the Atlas concedes these are undefined terms and registered work (`ELEMENT-TABLE:829-837`), so Moriarty cannot be charged with missing a predicate the kernel has not written; and source-domain reorg handling (B13), which I rank as unowned research rather than a known design change, because neither repository has an abstraction for retracting an accepted imported fact.

## Architecture axis (follow-up)

Added after a coordinator follow-up. Sections 1–6 above are unchanged. Re-read `ROADMAP.md` (56 lines) and `docs/MORIARTY-CONSOLIDATED-DESIGN.md` (123 lines) in full; all line numbers re-verified by `sed -n`.

**1. Placement.** The architecture puts cross-domain work in the **Federated DeFi Kernel and settlement adapters**, explicitly and optionally. `CONSOLIDATED-DESIGN.md:11`: the kernel "discovers and coordinates solvers, obtains evidence, operates optional threshold custody, **routes cross-chain effects, observes finality and executes recovery**… Moriarty can also run on Midnight without this federation." `:38`: "**Settlement adapters establish concrete effects and their domain limits.** The federated runtime coordinates them." `ROADMAP.md:3` and `:26` (U5) own it; `:38` confirms "U3 needs no federation". It is **not** a library family: neither `CONSOLIDATED-DESIGN.md:93` nor `ROADMAP.md:48` lists a cross-domain family among the nine/ten named. Per the three-column responsibility table, a bridge splits: liquidity/counterparty search → kernel (`:26` col2); attestation and observation collection → kernel (`:30` col2), issuer truth → external (`:30` col3); asynchronous multi-domain workflow **and compensation** → kernel (`:32` col2), with atomic boundaries set by "each domain's finality" → external (`:32` col3) and the *distinction* between atomic batch, committed prefix, fork/join and interleaving retained by the language (`:32` col1); witness availability and reconciliation → kernel (`:31` col2), "each external domain provides only its stated evidence" → external (`:31` col3), genesis/predecessor identity/uniqueness → language (`:31` col1); remedy authorization → language (`:34` col1), remedy execution → kernel (`:34` col2). This placement is coherent and, for my category, largely correct.

**2. Core mechanisms the language still needs.** *Domain-qualified effect line* — **named, no carrier**: `:52` "Assets and domain-qualified quantities have exact identities and units" and `:42` binds "chain/domain and authenticated state frame", but at stage granularity only (see 4). *Imported-fact evidence class* — **named for history, not for facts**: `:79` gives ledger induction to a Midnight-resident lineage, `:83` routes "imported histories" to "the compatible native certificate relation and legitimate origin/predecessor proofs", realizer U4. For imported *observations* (a foreign fill) there is only the negative at `:85` and the trust assumption at `:30` col3; no component is designated. *Cross-domain conservation or its refusal* — **neither**: `:54` says "Token conservation alone cannot prove these properties" and `:72` forbids claiming global rollback, but nothing states whether a source debit must equal a destination credit, and nothing refuses the question. *Compensation* — **named and placed**: `:32` col2, `:72`, `:34`, `ROADMAP.md:24` (U3). This is the best-architected piece in my category.

**3. Architectural holes** (a requirement asserted with no component on the other side) — the key output.

- **H1.** `:72` "Cross-domain workflow uses **authenticated progress**, conditional release and compensation". No component authenticates foreign progress. The kernel "observes finality" (`:11`), but observation is not authentication, and `:30` col3 leaves issuers as trust assumptions.
- **H2.** `:85` "Any bridge or federation attestation has the exact conditional trust meaning **specified by its policy**." A policy object is presupposed and never defined, carried or checked. It is absent from the stage statement (`:42` has observations with issuer/domain/time/finality but no policy reference), from the library list (`:93`), and from U5's evidence column, which names federation policy for membership, threshold, ordering, equivocation, availability and epoch change — not attestation trust meaning (`ROADMAP.md:26`).
- **H3.** *Partial correction to my §3 finding.* The design **does** carry the anchored/imported rule's *history* half (`:79` vs `:83`, realized by U4 native certificates). It does **not** carry its *observation* half. So `deliverables/defi-kernel-multichain-2026-09-11/REPORT.md:169` is orphaned only for imported facts, not for imported lineage.
- **H4.** `:52` promises domain-qualified quantities while `:42` gives the stage exactly one "chain/domain". The requirement has nowhere to land.
- **H5.** `ROADMAP.md:40` lists "wrong asset/recipient/**domain**" as a control to test. A control implies a checker; none is named — consistent with `enforcement-map.json` recording `domain.*` as `NOT_ENFORCED`.

**4. Canonical stage statement (`:42`).** It binds "chain/domain and authenticated state frame", "typed observations with issuer, domain, time and finality", "complete gross and net effects including fees and supply changes", predecessor/obligation commitments and the failure/phase policy. Omissions for this category: (a) **effect lines carry no domain** — domain attaches to the stage and to observations, never to an effect or supply-change line, so one relation cannot express a debit in D1 and a credit in D2 (confirmed in the schema at `openspec/changes/consolidated-language-kernel/schemas/stage-relation.schema.json:280-300, 352-375`); (b) no evidence class or anchored/imported flag on an observation; (c) no representation form on asset identity, so canonical and wrapped are the same asset; (d) no atomicity level; (e) no bridge or counterparty-stage identity linking two stages. (a) is load-bearing — without it none of the rest can be stated.

**5. Ownership of the architecture work.** Routing, adapters and federation policy → **U5** (`ROADMAP.md:26`; UNI-011/MPLR-030 at `traceability.md:86`). Imported-history certificate relation → **U4** (`ROADMAP.md:25`; design `:83`). Compensation, recovery, partiality → **U3** (`ROADMAP.md:24`; MPLR-005/010 at `traceability.md:61, 66`). Atomicity boundary and explicit settlement domains → **U3/U5** via MPLR-024 (`traceability.md:80`). **Unassigned:** domain-qualified effect lines, the imported-fact evidence class, cross-domain conservation or its refusal, and the bridge attestation-policy object. U0's evidence column freezes the stage relation and numeric profile but names none of them (`ROADMAP.md:21`), and U6 is library conformance over a family list with no cross-domain member (`:27`, `:48`).

**6. Minimum addition.** Four architecture-level items, none of which moves routing into the language: (i) put a domain on the effect line — `{asset, account, amount}` becomes `{domain, asset, account, amount}`, and the stage's `domain` becomes the *executing* domain rather than the only one; (ii) give asset identity an issuer and a representation form, which `:52` already promises and only lacks a carrier; (iii) add an evidence-class discriminator on observations plus the written rule that an imported fact may not be presented as anchored, and name the effect judgment (language column) as its checker, mirroring what `:83` already does for imported history; (iv) record the cross-domain conservation position as a decision — either a paired source-debit/destination-credit obligation or an explicit refusal, promoting `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-11-defi-kernel-sdk-interface-design.md:409` into the design so later work knows it was decided rather than overlooked. With those four, routing, liquidity, attestation collection and compensation execution can stay in U5's kernel exactly where the architecture puts them, and a bridge-aware program becomes expressible without reopening the core.
