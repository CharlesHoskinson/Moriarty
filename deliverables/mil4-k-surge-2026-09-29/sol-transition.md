# MIL/4 transition K proposal

**Status:** specified only, 2026-09-29. [sol-transition.k](sol-transition.k) defines importable `M4T-SYNTAX` and `M4T-TRANSITION` modules. It declares no K configuration so a containing definition can supply one. No parse, execution, differential, native proof, or Midnight ledger result is claimed. `M4T` is an isolated prefix; these constructors are a proposed bounded slice, not admitted Core/4 syntax.

## Semantic slice

The module models one episode, one domain, one asset, one owner, one payee, one escrow and one protocol reserve. An authenticated state carries a head, stage number, phase, four balances, cumulative gross debit, cumulative fees, cumulative net delivery, unpaid duty, residual spend authority, residual episode work, consumed receipt IDs and an escrow tombstone. The policy fixes episode and participant identities, gross and fee caps, net goal, work cap, late-race priority and signed intent digest. Asset, domain, numeric profile, program and verifier IDs need a fuller statement encoding; the string fields here do not supply canonical bytes.

`m4tEvaluate(m4tRun(policy, pre, newHead, event, proposedEffects, proposedPost, native, ledger))` computes an expected plan from the predecessor, then compares **the complete ten-field effect vector and the entire proposed post state**. A successful result is `m4tPrepared`; otherwise it is `m4tRejected(code)`. K `Int` is mathematical and unbounded here. This proposal does not yet establish `u128` or intermediate-width correspondence with a target circuit; that needs explicit checked arithmetic and a native range map. An embedding must reject negative, oversized or malformed encoded values before invoking this relation.

The four balance deltas are owner, escrow, payee and protocol reserve. Their sum must be zero. Gross increments on funding and never decreases on refund. The current relation admits **zero release fee** until W-D4 fixes a fee formula and beneficiary. Net delivery increments only on a payee credit. Duty decreases by the same delivered amount. The derived returned amount is `gross − net − fees − escrow`; shape requires it to be nonnegative. It rises on a refund or a final surplus return, without restoring spend authority or gross headroom. Funding, partial release and unknown marking retain at least one work slot for settlement. A receipt ID enters the authenticated consumed set exactly once.

| Event | Phase and evidence | Exact effect and successor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `m4tFund(q,id)` | Unfunded; owner has `q`, unspent authority and gross headroom; `q ≥ duty`; at least two work slots | Owner −q, escrow +q, gross +q, authority −q; phase pending. |
| `m4tMarkUnknown(id)` | Pending only; at least two work slots | Zero financial effects; phase unknown, work −1. This is an accepted knowledge transition, not evidence of success or nonreceipt. Repeated unknown marking is rejected. |
| `m4tRelease(q,0,id,obs)` | Pending or unknown; certified success, or a simultaneous observation under signed `release` priority | Partial: escrow −q, payee +q, duty −q, with positive escrow and one closing work slot retained. Final: escrow empties, payee receives the remaining duty and any surplus returns to owner; gross stays charged. Nonzero caller fee rejects. |
| `m4tRefund(id,obs)` | Pending or unknown; positive escrow; certified no receipt when net is zero, or certified nonreceipt of the exact remaining duty after partial delivery | All remaining escrow returns to owner. Gross, fees, authority and unpaid duty stay unchanged. Escrow is tombstoned; the episode enters recovering with its residual duty. |

There is no stage that interprets a passed deadline as nonreceipt. A pending state is legitimate. An unknown external result can be recorded, but cannot release or refund escrow. Release and refund compete for the same authenticated predecessor and escrow tombstone. Under a sound verifier and ledger one-use check, only one successor can be accepted. A late success supplied before a refund with simultaneous admissible observations follows the signed priority. A success reported after a committed refund cannot spend the escrow; the remaining duty is visible in recovering and needs a separately authorized remedy. The synthetic `m4tBoth` observation only exposes the priority rule. Its real admission must define how apparently conflicting foreign evidence can coexist and what finality rule makes that safe.

## Named trust premises

`m4tNativeQualified(m4tStatement(...))` is an **unimplemented native verification premise**. The statement binds profile tag, the **entire policy** including owner, payee, asset and race priority, predecessor head, stage, event including its observation, exact effects and entire successor state. `m4tNativeAbsent()` rejects. K deliberately has no rule that derives `m4tNativeQualified` from a witness, signature string or host Boolean. Only a pinned native proof and authentication boundary may construct it. That boundary must validate the signed policy and filling, authenticated origin/predecessor and obligations, selected foreign observation issuer/domain/epoch/finality, target circuit/key/public inputs, and correspondence to the actual ledger effects. Merely constructing the K term is an assumption, not a proof.

`m4tLedgerReserved(receipt,predecessorHead)` is a separate **unimplemented ledger consumption premise**. `m4tLedgerAbsent()` rejects. The target ledger must compare the current authenticated head and atomically consume the receipt with publication of the successor. A local consumed-ID set catches serial replay in this model; it cannot prevent competing branches without that ledger rule. The successor head is a deterministic, length-prefixed **symbolic extension** over the full policy, predecessor state, event and effects. It cannot be an arbitrary different string or cycle back to a previous head under an unchanged symbolic encoding. This string is not a cryptographic commitment; the native hash, canonical bytes, collision resistance, ledger binding and genesis rule remain open.

The premise split matters. Native history validity alone does not establish global uniqueness. Ledger ordering alone does not prove financial semantics or foreign nonreceipt. Neither premise defaults to true. A real acceptance implementation must enforce both at the same boundary as its actual effect lines; an off-chain report cannot supply either one.

## Expected discriminators

These are specified examples, **not executed tests**:

1. With gross cap 100, net goal 90 and owner balance 100, fund 100. Releasing 40 with fee 0 leaves escrow 60, delivered 40, duty 50, gross 100 and authority 0. The final release of 50 returns the surplus 10 to the owner and closes with escrow and duty zero; the derived returned amount becomes 10 while gross remains 100.
2. Supply a caller fee of 1, omit an effect line, or change only the successor duty. The fee fails at planning; exact effect/post equality rejects the other changes even when a projection of the payee credit looks acceptable.
3. Refund after certified terminal nonreceipt returns positive escrow while gross and consumed authority stay fixed. After partial delivery, the evidence must name the exact remaining duty. Retrying the same funding receipt or releasing from the tombstoned state rejects.
4. After an unknown observation, a refund proposal with `m4tUnknown` or `m4tPending` rejects. A timed-out host call has the same status until a qualified nonreceipt premise exists.
5. The same candidate with `m4tNativeAbsent` rejects as `NATIVE_PREMISE_ABSENT`; with `m4tLedgerAbsent` it rejects as `LEDGER_CONSUMPTION_ABSENT`. Replacing the predecessor head, signed policy, effects or post state breaks statement equality. A different release amount from the same predecessor and receipt produces a different symbolic successor head.

## Limits before a design gate

This slice does not settle multiasset conversion, per-line typed units, numeric widths and rounding, complete arbitrary cell footprints, multiowner consent, concurrent work reservations, partial foreign installments, source/Core elaboration, recursive history, private handoff, or a recovery action that discharges the retained duty. The complete effect vector is complete **for the four balances and counters registered in this slice**, not for a general MIL/4 program. A refund remains a prepared *retained-duty transition*, not successful agreement completion; the receiving application must inspect its recovering phase and duty. A closure reserve beyond one slot, successor episodes, recovery liveness, two-asset custody against the owner's free option, general grant revocation, and an unambiguous protocol-reserve fee policy remain open. Full MIL/4 must derive footprints from the selected program and reject every undeclared or omitted aggregate, custody, supply, policy, receipt and liability cell. It must specify a canonical statement encoding and a target-specific enforcement map before these K terms can support native or Preview acceptance.

The proposal follows the six obligations in [DESIGN-MIL4-WORKING.md](../mil4-successor-2026-09-29/DESIGN-MIL4-WORKING.md): stage construction, signed-intent binding, complete effects, authority use, history extension and explicit failure. It takes MIL/2's ledger-linked episode and pending escrow transition as the baseline, while leaving its native proof claim open. The broader [consolidated design](../../docs/MORIARTY-CONSOLIDATED-DESIGN.md) requires actual effects and residual duties to remain bound through acceptance; this K model is a source-level statement of that requirement.
