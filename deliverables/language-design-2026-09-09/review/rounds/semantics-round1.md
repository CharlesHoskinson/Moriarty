## Position

**Recommendation.** Adopt a small TypeScript-style external language whose familiar syntax lowers to pure, bounded financial transitions. Borrow Elm's explicit input/update/requested-effect boundary and Unison's immutable references behind readable names. These are useful features, not merely hazards to exclude. Make a partial payment with surviving debt the first end-to-end authoring example. Preserve the distinction between a valid proposal and accepted settlement at every stage.

The source should expose financial decisions and let tooling derive incidental bookkeeping. Authors should write an amount, creditor, allocation policy, obligation and bounded action; they should not manually construct a proof statement, hash manifest or continuation accounting record. Those derived records still need complete, inspectable meanings and independently checked correspondence.

**Repository observation.** The successor proposal already specifies immutable `pre`, single-write `next`, suffix-only `post` conditions, residual duties, bounded work and quantitative authority. Its semantics are proposals, and the README distinguishes the syntax frontend from the narrower funded source preparation subset. This panel does not credit parsing or local preparation as full successor execution.

## Source-grounded reasons and limits

**Source facts, directly inspected.** Historical FElm's normalization theorem covers the first stage; its later signal graph explicitly uses unbounded FIFO queues (S01, extracted lines 348–359, paper §3). Simply RaTT distinguishes explicit unbounded accumulator growth from implicit space leaks (S03, extracted lines 368–371); its productivity theorem supports ongoing streams, not a finite agreement budget. Koka's divergence theorem relates absence of `div` to termination in its own calculus (S05, extracted lines 926–931), without specifying a Moriarty work charge. These sources justify borrowing pure and explicit structure while specifying finite reactivity independently.

**Source facts, directly inspected.** Elm's captured command/subscription guide separates functional updates from runtime interaction (D02). Unison's reference permits discarding or invoking a continuation multiple times (D08, extracted line 348), making multiplicity a real semantic design issue. Its short hashes resolve in an environment (D07, Short Hashes); its unique types use an identifier in hashing rather than relying on display names (D14). These support bounded messages, readable full-reference resolution and nominal identity, with no need to import an entire language environment.

**Inference from the frozen atlases.** Nix and Build Systems à la Carte motivate explicit dependency inputs, output identity and invalidation assumptions; this panel inspected their atlas accounts, not their full raw papers. The usability studies motivate measured comprehension/repair work and caution about unchecked escape hatches; they do not prove this checklist improves usability. The inaccessible source families remain inaccessible, and this panel adds no claims based on them.

**Repository constraint.** ContractInvariant, IntentRefinement, TransitionValidity and HistoryCompliance remain mandatory. Source/Core elaboration, Core/K/evaluator agreement, compiler correspondence, native proof validity and ledger acceptance are separate predicates. Admission must bind canonical bounds and relevant observations; history proofs do not replace ledger consumption or external-input trust. The checklist does not freeze a successor profile or bypass the existing acceptance/resource gates.

## Challenges for the other panelists

1. **To usability: preserve accessibility without duplicating financial decisions.** I disagree in advance with requiring authors to handwrite both a debt update and a discharge effect if two inconsistent descriptions can exist. The first example needs one defined allocation rule that determines the surviving duty, with redundant `ensures` used as checks. Which source form exposes the allocation and creditor clearly with the least duplicate intent?
2. **To architecture: start smaller than a general reactive calculus.** A bounded observation-to-action path and one finite schedule satisfy a meaningful part of the Elm request. I would oppose requiring temporal modalities or a full subscription compiler before a partial-payment example. Which scheduled ACTUS or pending DeFi example needs more than these two forms now?
3. **To both: surface work bounds as contract limits, derive machine accounting.** Authors should not subtract an instruction counter on every action. A compiler can conservatively derive charges, but the accepted lifetime and recovery reserve must remain explicit and inspectable. Where should an author choose the horizon and recovery policy without being shown backend circuit machinery?
4. **To architecture: do not silently equate hash-normalization choices.** The report's alias-rename aspirations need an exact distinction between local binders, public action identifiers and nominal asset bindings. I prefer normalizing local binders only in the initial scheme, preserving public identifiers until their role is specified. Is a more aggressive rule needed for the first dependency example?
5. **To usability: effect summaries need not mean more mandatory punctuation.** I support compiler-derived summaries checked against declared interface limits. Requiring an author to repeat every emitted effect kind adds maintenance effort without granting authority. Compare the inferred summary with an explicit annotation before adopting a new effect-row syntax.

These are first-round positions and questions, not claims of convergence. The smallest next demonstration is one payment of 30 against an identified debt of 100: explicit USD transfer/allocation, surviving duty 70, bounded work decrease, and complete proposal inspection, followed by independent negative cases. Proof and ledger milestones remain separate.
