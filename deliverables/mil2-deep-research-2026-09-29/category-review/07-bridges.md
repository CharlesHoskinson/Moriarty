# Category 7 — bridges and cross-domain settlement under MIL/2

**Status:** specified-only design review, 2026-09-29. “Covered” means MIL/2 states an adequate narrow source rule, not that any compiler, proof or ledger enforces it. “Partial” means a carrier exists but a material rule or binding is missing. “Open” means the needed relation is not defined. “Out of scope” is an explicit product boundary. Sources are the [MIL/2 design](../../../concepts/intent-language/DESIGN-MIL2.md), [proposed semantics](../MIL2-PROPOSED-SEMANTICS.tex), [research findings](../RESEARCH-FINDINGS.md), [roadmap](../../../ROADMAP.md), and [earlier R7 audit](../../u0-study-2026-09-28/defi-coverage/R7-bridges.md). The [MIL/1 category map](../../../concepts/intent-language/CATEGORY-MAP.md) is historical and is reassessed below.

## A realistic bridge workflow

An owner escrows 100 units of a source asset on domain S and authorizes a solver to deliver at least 99 units of a named destination representation on domain D, with at most 1 unit of fees. A source stage records the local lock or burn. A relay supplies evidence of that accepted stage to D; a separate destination stage issues or releases the destination asset under its own authority. The owner may accept a fast fill from a bonded liquidity provider before source finality, with a signed dispute and reimbursement policy. If delivery remains unresolved at a deadline, source funds stay pending until an authenticated negative destination result or the exact signed trusted-recovery condition permits closure. Each domain may make irreversible progress independently. This is a **target scenario and inference** from the bridge requirements in R7 §§2–5, MIL/2 §§2, 5, 7, 10, and the IBC comparison; it is not an implemented MIL/2 program.

## Corrections to the MIL/1 category map

`CATEGORY-MAP.md:167–192` labels lock-mint and burn-mint, optimistic verification, filler fast fill and cross-domain conservation “expressible.” MIL/2 does not justify those full verdicts. Escrow plus `issue` can describe local legs, but no typed transfer form or unique paired claim links them. `imported(Policy)` names a trust premise, while verifier mode, finality evidence and replay discipline remain to be specified. `after(Instant)` and `challenged(e)` permit guards, while bonded filler reimbursement, slash conditions and dispute economics have no defined effect relation. Per-domain conservation says nothing about backing on another domain. The map's displayed Bitcoin-source escrow also violates MIL/2's one-executing-domain rule if read as a Midnight stage mutating Bitcoin; a source-domain event must be authenticated as imported evidence for a separate local stage (§§2, 10, lines 31–46 and 252–256). The map's refusal of global rollback remains correct.

MIL/2 does repair several R7 findings at the design level: nominal domain-qualified representation replaces a bare asset string (R7 B8); every local effect has a domain (B1); evidence class becomes a type index with a provenance obligation (B10); pending escrow, race priority and named recovery assumptions replace an informal deadline (B11–B12). These are **repository observations about the new proposal**, not demonstrations that the older U0 schema, source/Core, K, native verifier or Preview ledger now enforce them. The MIL/2 status line says exactly that (`DESIGN-MIL2.md:1–5`), and the proposed semantics states the same limit (lines 39–42, 156–169, 257).

## Priority obligations for this category

1. **U0 relation and type freeze:** add a unique cross-domain transfer/claim identity, transfer form and paired source/destination obligation; type verifier/finality policy and imported replay; reconcile nominal assets and domain-qualified effects with the older stage schema. The existing MIL/2 milestone table assigns general types and stage public inputs to U0 (`DESIGN-MIL2.md:340–346`), but does **not** assign the paired bridge relation; that is a recommendation here.
2. **Local semantic proofs:** derive bridge escrow footprints including custody, recipients, receipt and replay cells; prove source-set preservation, one-shot terminal consumption, lock/reservation safety and debt persistence. The proposed semantics names O1–O6 at lines 239–255; none is discharged by this review. The current MIL/2 showcase's transfer footprints are incomplete (proposed semantics lines 208–227), so copying that example would fail its own containment rule.
3. **Native and ledger evidence:** bind exact signed intent, transfer identity, imported evidence bytes/policy, predecessor head, domain, complete effects, fees, supply and tombstone to the stage public statement and authenticated ledger state (proposed semantics lines 239–257). Run adversarial double-mint, wrong-domain, stale/replayed attestation, late receipt/refund and reorg/partition cases before claiming a bridge path. These are future acceptance cases, not tests run for this report.
4. **Bounded profile decision:** measure the proposed eight-stage Episode, two-parent join, 16-effect and 32-footprint caps (`DESIGN-MIL2.md:268–283`) against a real bridge lifecycle, including dispute and recovery. U3 covers ledger-linked joins and recovery; U4 owns imported-evidence verification; neither milestone currently promises a complete cross-domain bridge (`DESIGN-MIL2.md:347–350`; `ROADMAP.md:38–48`).

**Review limit.** This is a source review. The required guarded status reported `SP01.6 loan-swap-subset`, unresolved operational history, and no pending transactions. I did not run tests, proofs, compilation or network calls. I used the prior R7 audit as a requirements inventory and rederived its verdicts against MIL/2 and the companion semantics; the IBC sources are comparative protocol evidence, not evidence of a Midnight bridge adapter.
