Trading overview

GMX is a decentralized exchange that lets you trade without a username or password. The platform uses oracle-based pricing sourced from aggregated exchange data, which reduces the risk of liquidations from temporary wicks. For details on how pricing works, see Pricing on GMX.

GMX V2 uses linear, USD-based PnL with flexible collateral, so your overall exposure can differ from a standard stablecoin-margined linear perp. For a practical explanation, see Are GMX perps linear or inverse?.

Adding a wallet​

If you don't have a wallet yet, you can use Rabby or create an integrated wallet from the GMX connection flow. The integrated wallet supports passkey setup with Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello, or Android biometrics, without requiring an email address or seed phrase for that setup path.

Connecting and funding your wallet​

After you have a wallet, connect it by pressing the "Connect wallet" button on the Trade page. If your wallet app supports WalletConnect, you can use the WalletConnect option and scan the QR code from your wallet.

The account menu also provides in-app Send and Receive flows for your connected wallet. Receive displays your wallet address as text and a QR code that you can copy or scan. Send transfers supported tokens to an address and shows the estimated network fee before confirmation.

The Receive flow may also let you buy crypto with a debit or credit card, Apple Pay, or Google Pay, or transfer funds from another wallet, exchange, or chain. Availability depends on your region and the configured provider. These options fund your connected wallet; depositing into a GMX Account is a separate step.

You can also use the buying, bridging, and on-ramp options provided by your wallet. If you don't have the required gas token for the network, you can still trade using Express Trading.

Multichain trading​

GMX lets you trade from multiple blockchain networks. The method you use depends on which chain your wallet is connected to: direct wallet trading on chains where GMX markets are deployed, or the GMX Account for cross-chain access from other supported networks.

Wallet funds and GMX Account funds​

When you open or increase a position, GMX can use funds from either your connected wallet or your GMX Account as the position margin, depending on which balance you select in the interface.

  • Wallet funds are funds held directly in your connected wallet. Direct wallet trading uses these funds as margin.
  • GMX Account funds are a separate trading balance linked to the same connected wallet. To use this balance as margin, first have funds in your connected wallet on a supported source chain, then deposit them into your GMX Account. If needed, the deposit flow bridges those funds to the GMX Account on Arbitrum.

These are two ways to provide funds for positions, not two separate position accounts. Positions belong to the connected wallet and are shown together, whether they were opened with wallet funds or GMX Account funds.

Direct wallet trading​

Active GMX markets are available on Arbitrum, Avalanche, and MegaETH. When your wallet is connected to any of these chains, you can trade directly using the funds in your wallet — no additional setup required.

GMX Account (multichain)​

The GMX Account lets you trade on GMX from chains that don't have GMX markets deployed, such as Ethereum, Base, or BNB. You can also use it when connected to Arbitrum. Arbitrum is the only supported settlement chain — all trades through the GMX Account execute on Arbitrum markets.

Here's how it works:

  • Your GMX Account balance lives on Arbitrum.
  • Deposit from your connected wallet on any supported source chain — funds are automatically bridged using Stargate (token transfers) and LayerZero (cross-chain messaging).
  • Trade on Arbitrum markets using your GMX Account balance.
  • Withdraw to any supported chain, regardless of where you originally deposited.
  • Follow submitted deposits and withdrawals from Transfer history in the account menu.

Think of the GMX Account as a separate trading balance on Arbitrum that you can fund and withdraw to from anywhere.

note

The GMX Account is not available on Avalanche or MegaETH; direct wallet trading is used on those chains. Compatible smart wallets, including supported Safe and Coinbase Smart Wallet setups, can use Express Trading, One-Click Trading, and GMX Account flows. Compatibility depends on the wallet's typed-data signing support and deployment state on the required chain, so it isn't universal across contract accounts.

TradFi markets​

GOLD/USD, SILVER/USD, WTIOIL/USD, BRENTOIL/USD, NATGAS/USD, SPCX/USD, QQQ/USD, and SPY/USD are available under the TradFi category in the market dropdown. The commodity markets can be found under TradFi > Commodities and searched by common name (for example, "GOLD", "SILVER", "WTI", "BRENT", "NATGAS") or ticker symbol (for example, "XAU", "XAG", "CL", "BZ", "NG"). SPCX/USD is under TradFi > Stocks and can be found with "SPCX", "Space", or "X". On Arbitrum, QQQ/USD and SPY/USD are 24/7 perpetual markets under TradFi > Indices. You can provide liquidity through GLV [WETH-USDC]; direct deposits into their GM pools are disabled. Search for QQQ/USD with "QQQ", "Nasdaq", "Nasdaq-100", "NDX", or "Invesco", and search for SPY/USD with "SPY", "S&P", "S&P 500", "SPX", or "SPDR".

Trading hours​

TradFi markets are tradeable 24/7, including weekends. However, risk parameters shift between on-hours and off-hours configurations.

Precious metals and energy commodities use the following schedule (all times UTC):

EventTime (UTC)Config applied
Daily maintenance startMon–Thu 20:45Off-hours
Daily maintenance endMon–Thu 22:15On-hours
Weekly closeFri 20:45Off-hours
Weekly openSun 22:15On-hours

These windows include a 15-minute buffer before the CME close (21:00 UTC) and after the CME open (22:00 UTC). WTIOIL/USD, BRENTOIL/USD, and NATGAS/USD follow the same CME Globex schedule as GOLD/USD and SILVER/USD — identical cron job, identical buffers, identical holiday calendar.

SPCX/USD, QQQ/USD, and SPY/USD use a separate US-equity risk window:

PeriodNew York timeCurrent daylight-time UTC equivalentConfig applied
US-equity risk windowMon–Fri 09:45–15:45Mon–Fri 13:45–19:45On-hours
Outside the window and NYSE full-day holidaysAll other timesAll other timesOff-hours

The UTC equivalent changes when New York switches between daylight and standard time.

Off-hours behavior​

During off-hours, affected parameters are adjusted to account for reduced market liquidity:

  • Commodity markets reduce max leverage, reduce open interest caps, and use higher price impact, position fee, and borrow fee settings
  • GOLD/USD, SILVER/USD, WTIOIL/USD, and BRENTOIL/USD increase their liquidation factor from 0.5% to 0.8% during off-hours
  • SPCX/USD reduces open interest caps and increases its position fee and borrow fee settings; its 10x max leverage does not change by session
  • The QQQ/USD and SPY/USD switchover increases their position fee and borrow fee settings; the GMX Risk Oracle manages their price impact and open interest caps independently of the schedule

note

Existing positions remain open when off-hours begin. For GOLD/USD, SILVER/USD, WTIOIL/USD, and BRENTOIL/USD, the liquidation factor increases from 0.5% to 0.8%, so a position's liquidation price can move automatically when the market switches from on-hours to off-hours. Positions with high leverage are at increased risk of liquidation during off-hours. Traders cannot increase a commodity position above the off-hours max leverage shown in the table above — only decrease. When on-hours resume, the commodity liquidation factors and other session-based thresholds relax back to normal. For NATGAS/USD, the liquidation factor stays at 1% in both sessions. For SPCX/USD, off-hours reduces open interest caps and increases position fee and borrow fee settings, while its 10x max leverage and 1% liquidation factor remain unchanged. For QQQ/USD and SPY/USD, the current 25x max leverage and 1% liquidation factor remain unchanged between sessions, while their position fee and borrow fee settings increase during off-hours.

The interface warns when a new trade or existing position could become close to liquidation after a commodity market switches to off-hours risk parameters. Position rows can also show an estimated off-hours liquidation price so traders can add margin or reduce leverage before the off-hours change takes effect.

The oracle keeper also applies token-specific freshness thresholds when matching realtime feed reports. Off-hours thresholds remain in effect for 10 minutes after a market reopens, which avoids immediately rejecting reports around the session transition.

Express Trading and One-Click Trading​

GMX provides different modes to suit trader preferences: Classic Trading, Express Trading, and Express + One-Click Trading. We recommend using Express or Express + One-Click, as they provide the best experience, and trading fees are the same on all modes.

ModeSigning methodRPC infrastructureGas payments
Classic TradingOn-chain: wallet signing popup for each tradeUses your own wallet's RPCETH on Arbitrum and MegaETH, AVAX on Avalanche
Express TradingOff-chain: you sign messages locally; the active relay broadcasts on-chainRelay-managed submission infrastructureUSDC or WETH on Arbitrum, USDC or WAVAX on Avalanche, USDM or WETH on MegaETH
Express + One-Click TradingOff-chain: auto-signed with a locally stored sub-account key (no manual confirmations)Relay-managed submission infrastructureUSDC or WETH on Arbitrum, USDC or WAVAX on Avalanche, USDM or WETH on MegaETH

GMX Relay is being rolled out to a subset of interface submissions. Gelato Relay remains available as the fallback.

note

Compatible smart wallets can use Express Trading and One-Click Trading through adaptive typed-data signing. Support depends on the contract wallet and its deployment state on the required chain. If the interface can't verify or complete the required signature flow, use Classic Trading instead; GMX Account flows require Express Trading.

Enabling One-Click Trading​

One-Click Trading can be enabled through the settings menu in the top right of the interface. Enabling this feature lets you trade instantly without a wallet signing popup for each trade.

If you are building a delegated trading or one-click trading integration on top of GMX, see Delegated trading integration.

Safety features​

  • Funds from decreasing positions, closing positions, or swaps can only be sent back to your wallet.
  • Trades executed without signing popups are limited by the maximum number you authorize. For instance, if you authorize 10 actions, after 10 trades, a wallet signing popup appears to re-authorize further trades.

Risks​

  • This feature uses a sub-account key stored locally in your browser. If your browser is compromised, the key could potentially leak, allowing trades to be executed.
  • The previously authorized trade limit acts as a safeguard. Even if compromised, a malicious actor can only execute trades up to the authorized limit.

RPC URLs​

GMX uses different RPC URLs for querying (reading data) and submitting transactions (writing data).

  • Reading RPCs are set automatically by the GMX interface and selected from a curated list to ensure fast and reliable data loading.

  • Writing RPCs:

    • Classic Trading: Set by your wallet.
    • Express Trading and Express + One-Click Trading: Submitted through the active relay path; you don't select a writing RPC.

If you're experiencing issues while trading in Classic Trading, consider switching to Express or Express + One-Click Trading through the settings menu. Alternatively, you can manually select another RPC URL via your wallet from options provided on Chainlist.
