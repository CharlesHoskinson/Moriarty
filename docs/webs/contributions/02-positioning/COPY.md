# Bring the required proofs to the transition.

Proposed proof-carrying financial workflows, with explicit conditions before settlement. This page explores the Cardano experiment; Moriarty’s broader target is Midnight.

A Web is the proposed script-bound collection of Strands a consuming contract requires for one transition. Each Strand is an individual proof with its claim, statement, and binding metadata. Start with exact assets, recipients, fees, and duties; make the handoff to the consuming script inspectable.

## Dapp teams

Describe the action before building its transaction. Use exact assets, recipients, gross debit, fees, and remaining duties to explain what a partial payment changes and what it still owes.

## Wallet and transaction builders

Show what the owner is authorizing. Connect an inspectable statement to required proofs, context provenance, the consuming script, transaction construction, and effect readback. Keep amounts and fee bounds visible throughout.

## Asset issuers

Explain issuance and continuing servicing. Describe supply limits and recipient amounts alongside any redemption duties. Unissued capacity remains capacity; servicing promises need their own explicit financial terms.

## See the payment, fee, and remainder.

Illustrative invoice: payment 35 CASH plus fee 2 debits escrow by 37. Invoice duty falls from 100 to 65; reserve duty stays 10. Source funding 110 leaves cash 73 against duties 75.

Proposed funding 112 backs those duties for one partial only. Later partials add fees. These are worksheet amounts, not execution results or an all-in transaction quote.

## Give every proof a defined job.

Follow a claim specification from its assertion and subject to a concrete Strand and its consuming script. Inspect which statement, context, and consumer must agree.

A claim specification defines a requirement. Illustrated proof roles are not concrete Strands; assembling the illustration establishes no settlement.
