# Editing rules for Moriarty Webs

These rules address the problems in this page. They are an editing checklist, not an AI detector. Short sentences, lists and parallel constructions can be useful; repeating them instead of explaining a subject is the problem.

Three independent agent assignments examined editorial practice, newcomer comprehension and existing Moriarty branding. Web sources were retrieved through Scrapling. The recommendations below are editorial judgments, not results from a human usability study.

| # | Problem to look for | What to do on this page |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Architecture before a reason to care | Start with an invoice payment and show the amount left unpaid. |
| 2 | An unexplained project noun inside another definition | Define a Strand in ordinary terms before listing binding fields. |
| 3 | “Transition” without a referent | Say payment or transaction first; explain state changes when needed. |
| 4 | Slogan fragments used as headings | Replace “Pay part. Keep the remainder visible.” with “Paying part of an invoice.” |
| 5 | Forced line breaks pretending to add significance | Remove “One action. Its complete effects.” Use a complete, informative heading. |
| 6 | A heading about the author's intention | Replace “Keep the distinctions useful” with a topic the reader can recognize. |
| 7 | Parallel predicates standing in for an explanation | Develop the invoice calculation and its consequence across connected sentences. |
| 8 | A compressed tour of the entire system | Explain the program, equations, witness and proof where each becomes relevant. |
| 9 | Choppy sentences that could form one clear thought | Combine “Together they form the statement. They are not secret.” into an explanation of public inputs. |
| 10 | Repeated openings with no development | Explain why unpaid obligations carry forward, then give an invoice or loan example. |
| 11 | Metaphorical verbs hiding the mechanism | Say Groth16 produces a proof; remove “seals a proof” and “Proof meets ledger.” |
| 12 | A definition crowded with internal metadata | Introduce the concept first. Keep the exact binding inventory available later. |
| 13 | A control without an understandable result | Explain that selecting a check shows its claim and input source. |
| 14 | A UI label that changes the meaning of the action | Remove proof coverage, not the contract's requirement. |
| 15 | Familiar finance obscured by internal vocabulary | Use “invoice still owed” before introducing the technical term duty. |
| 16 | A source citation treated as prior knowledge | Explain the architecture without requiring the reader to know Proving Nothing. Put its credit in Sources. |
| 17 | A technical label without a useful description | Pair Witness with the prover's private inputs; pair Arithmetization with the equations being checked. |
| 18 | Claims of savings without a comparison | Explain the extra Issue transactions and minimum ADA. Cost conclusions require measurements. |
| 19 | Archival distinctions in ordinary controls | Label the funding modes by the presence or absence of the fee allowance; retain source provenance in technical notes. |
| 20 | A forbidden-word list used instead of judgment | Read the heading outline, then follow the payment. Check whether each paragraph adds understanding. |

## Sources

- [Google: voice and tone](https://developers.google.com/style/tone) addresses choppy or long-winded sentences, repeated openings, jargon and distracting references.
- [Google: audience](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/audience) explains how to account for the knowledge a reader still needs.
- [Google: paragraphs](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/paragraphs) supports connected development of one subject.
- [Google: headings and titles](https://developers.google.com/style/headings) recommends headings that describe a topic or task.
- [Google: organizing documents](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/documents) supports answering essential questions early.
- [W3C WAI: headings](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/page-structure/headings/) explains the role of a useful heading hierarchy.
- [GOV.UK Design System: details](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/details/) guides the use of disclosure for information only some readers need.
- [Nielsen Norman Group: how users read on the web](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/) supports informative subheadings and factual copy; its original early-web study is not a current universal benchmark.
- [PLOS One: syntactic comparison of ChatGPT and native-speaker essays](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0329410) reports more coordination and parallel constructions in its bounded ChatGPT corpus. It does not establish that a particular sentence is AI-generated.

## Brand sources

The page adapts the tracked [Moriarty banner](https://github.com/CharlesHoskinson/Moriarty/blob/dafcf596507753725f0b6971a419e81d4bd4b2ba/docs/assets/moriarty-banner.png) and the [published product site's design tokens](https://github.com/CharlesHoskinson/Moriarty/blob/8174e52975d8db9bf59636fea0fa338c0f47c855/site/src/styles.css). Charcoal and ivory come from the site; antique gold and the crescent reference come from the banner. The first editorial candidate used the banner portrait; the user then requested the generated Web and Strands artwork used in the current hero. The page's crescent mark is a vector adaptation of the banner emblem, not a newly discovered official logo asset. Its Midnight tagline is outside the image crop on this Cardano page.

The research did not find a formal brand manual. The new treatment is an adaptation of verified project material. It replaces the unsupported polygon emblem, forest palette and “field guide” identity.
