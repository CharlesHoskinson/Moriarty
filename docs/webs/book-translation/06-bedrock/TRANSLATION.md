# Layer 6: Cryptographic Bedrock — translation proposals

This layer asks which mathematical objects and assumptions the proof core uses: fields for arithmetic, groups and curves for some commitments and proof checks, and hashes for commitments and transcript challenges. It helps explain why choices in Layers 4–6 constrain one another. It does not promise that a primitive, verifier, or proof is accepted by a particular chain.

The book presents the seven layers as interdependent and describes Layers 4–6 as a tightly coupled proof core. Keep that useful teaching model, while presenting it as a map of concerns rather than a universal sequence. For Moriarty, distinguish three claims: a chain exposes a primitive; a verifier implements a complete proof-checking relation over compatible parameters and encodings; and an actual consumer accepts a proof in its execution context. Evidence for one does not establish the next.

The supplied Cardano CIP-0381 document describes Plutus operations for BLS12-381, including group operations, Miller loops, final verification, and serialization. That is relevant compatibility context for Cardano. It does not establish that a specific Groth16 verifier is implemented or accepts a proof. Moriarty's supplied records separately describe one local compiled-C2-wrapper acceptance with fabricated context and a forgeable test setup; they record no on-chain transaction. Keep those evidence limits with the example. Do not transfer Cardano curve support or a curve assumption to Midnight, or silently assume BN254 compatibility.

One likely book/source mismatch needs Opus adjudication before exact security copy is adopted: Chapter 7 says “KZG powers Groth16” (L6 excerpt, “KZG (Kate-Zaverucha-Goldberg)”). The supplied Groth paper G05 is Jens Groth's 2016 paper on pairing-based non-interactive arguments; its abstract discusses a three-group-element construction and one pairing-product equation, but it does not identify that construction as Groth16 or establish that Groth16 uses KZG. The paragraph may conflate pairing-based proof systems with a specific polynomial commitment. Preserve this as an explicit fact-check item; do not repair it by silently substituting a claim.

The interactive ideas below are explanatory copy proposals, not security conclusions. Avoid quantified security bits or quantum timelines. In product copy, state relevant limits plainly: security depends on assumptions and setup where applicable; supported curve operations are not the same as proof acceptance; and the current Moriarty evidence is local and scoped.
