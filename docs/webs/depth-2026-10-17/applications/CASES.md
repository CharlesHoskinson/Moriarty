# Worked application cases

These four cases explain proposed applications through their financial outcomes. DEX, issuance and loan figures are worked examples without supplied application source programs. Treasury arithmetic comes from S13; its 25 A / 10 B opening custody is a proposed backing premise. None of these pages records an executed application transaction. A required check is a condition to prove, not an already available Strand. As the developer-experience source puts it: “A whole-execution instance may cover several specifications. A separate fee or liability proof requires an actual relation.” [A03; A05]

## Exchange orders: two owner outcomes in one batch

A batch builder combines exchange orders so they can share a pool transition. The useful question for each trader is still individual: how much of my asset leaves, and how much of the requested asset reaches me after the applicable fees? A batch total cannot answer that question for both owners. Each owner has authorized their own limits, and a satisfactory combined total could conceal an unsatisfactory individual result. [A01, dex; A02, “Five application cases”]

Start with two orders. Each offers 100 A and requires at least 90 B net. The worked outcome assigns 92 B to each owner. The owner-side arithmetic therefore shows two debits of 100 A and two receipts of 92 B: 200 A offered across the batch and 184 B received by the owners. Each receipt exceeds its own 90 B floor by 2 B. That 2 B difference is headroom above the minimum; the example does not identify it as a fee. The pool’s opening reserves, pricing formula, fee amounts and rounding rule are not supplied, so the 92 B outcomes are premises of this illustration rather than outputs of a demonstrated pool calculation. [A01, dex; A04, dex]

The computation has two connected jobs. Order checks compare each net receipt with that owner’s signed floor. The pool check must explain the shared reserve transition under the chosen fee and rounding rules. Batch accounting must use the same amounts in both places. Otherwise a displayed order result and a claimed pool result could describe different transfers. The illustration assigns a separate payment output to each order; combined payments require a separately selected allocation policy. [A02; A05, CL-22]

The authoritative division of responsibility is explicit: “Claim specifications cover order execution and pool arithmetic. Per-order output allocation is enforced by the consuming validator over actual transaction outputs”; “a proof can attest arithmetic only over values the validator recomputes.” These are attributed source-design statements from Opus ERR-23, not an execution result. The checks shown here consequently describe required proof coverage, while the consumer still checks which actual output pays each order. The three check labels do not specify three proofs. [A05, ERR-23]

After the illustrated full exchanges, each owner has received 92 B. The pool needs a continuing state consistent with both orders, but its balances cannot be filled in from these figures alone. A partial-fill extension would also have to carry the unfilled quantity and remaining duties forward; cancellation is a separate action. [A04, dex]

**Status and remaining questions.** This is a proposed application. Its pool relation, actual proof producer, exact assets and recipients, complete fee and reserve worksheet, and chosen output-allocation policy remain to be supplied. No pool reserves or price formula are implied by the diagram. [A04, dex]

## Token issuance: new supply, recipient distribution and unused capacity

An issuer needs to explain both how much new supply an operation creates and where that supply goes. Recipients need an amount they can reconcile with the actual mint and their outputs. A cap adds a further lifecycle question: how much room remains for later issuance? Those quantities describe related parts of the same operation, but unused room under a cap is not an outstanding payment promise. [A02; A03, “Journey B”]

This example is a first issue of 300 application-token units under a cap of 1,000. Recipient one receives 180 units and recipient two receives 120. The distribution accounts for the whole issue because 180 + 120 = 300. The issue stays within the cap because 300 ≤ 1,000, leaving 1,000 − 300 = 700 units of unissued capacity. The after-state has 300 units issued and 700 units of capacity left. That capacity is an accounting limit for possible later issues; it is not an asset output sent to a third recipient. [A01, issuance; A04, issuance]

There are therefore two asset-facing checks: the actual new supply must be 300 units of the specified application asset, and the recipient outputs must account for those 300 units as 180 and 120. Supply-state continuation gives later operations a predecessor against which to check the cumulative cap. This explanation does not choose a datum layout or whether that predecessor is spent or read through another mechanism. The supplied concept requires supply continuation, but supplies no concrete issuance transaction or application minting policy. [A02; A03]

The proposed proof requirements are the supplied claims “The number of tokens issued stays within the cap” and “The amounts sent to the recipients add up to the number of tokens issued.” The validator responsibilities in that same copy are: “The application asset’s own minting policy enforces the cap and continues the supply state from its predecessor” and “The minting policy and the consuming validator check the actual mint and recipient outputs.” A supply calculation is thus evidence about the required relation; it does not replace the asset policy or create the application token by itself. These are requirements for the proposed application, not observed policy behavior. [A01, issuance]

If an issuer also promises redemption, that promise needs its own financial duty: named parties, an exact repayment asset, and rules for creation and discharge. No redemption agreement is supplied here. The 700 units of remaining capacity create no such duty. Separately, issuing a Web proof claim records evidence; it does not mint the 300 application tokens. [A04, issuance]

**Status and remaining questions.** The application’s mint/burn semantics, policy, supply predecessor representation, issuer authority, concrete proof producer and any servicing terms remain unspecified. The sources establish no signer matrix or executed mint. The diagram shows the proposed supply and distribution accounting only. [A03; A04]

## Loan repayment: cash paid and debt discharged

A loan servicer must reconcile the money leaving the repaying account with the amount removed from the debt. The borrower needs to see the whole debit before authorizing it; the lender needs to see how much arrives as repayment; both need an accurate remaining obligation. A fee makes these quantities differ even when they use the same asset. In this example, the repaying account is called the payer throughout. [A01, loan; A04, loan]

The opening debt is 100 CASH. The example supplies an accrual of 5 CASH for the period, taking the amount owed before payment to 105 CASH. It then supplies a debt repayment of 35 CASH and a separate fee of 2 CASH. The payer funds 37 CASH in total, the lender receives the 35 CASH repayment, and the fee recipient receives 2 CASH. The fee is part of the payer’s cost of this action. It does not discharge any of the loan. [A01, loan; A02]

The remaining-debt calculation follows the obligation rather than the total cash debit: 100 + 5 − 35 = 70 CASH. Subtracting all 37 CASH would record 68 CASH and incorrectly treat the fee as repayment. The debt continuation must therefore carry 70 CASH while the payment accounting records the separate 35 and 2 CASH transfers. A duty is the amount still owed; discharge is the explicitly recorded reduction of that duty through repayment. Cash movement and duty movement must agree on the 35 CASH reduction without giving the fee the same meaning. [A04, loan; A06]

The proposed proof claims are “The debt increases by the period’s accrued amount”, “The payer funds the debt repayment and its separate fee”, and “Only the repayment reduces the debt; the fee is accounted for separately.” The source assigns these continuing checks to the application: “Payer, lender, fee recipient and asset match the payer’s signed intent and the transaction’s actual outputs”; “The continuing debt carries over from the predecessor state through explicit accrual and discharge entries; collateral release follows its own policy.” Those statements identify the calculation and transaction checks required; they do not establish a concrete loan proof or consumer. [A01, loan]

The 70 CASH result is a total outstanding duty. These figures do not identify an interest-first, principal-first or proportional allocation of the 35 CASH payment. Nor do they supply the rate, period length, day-count convention or rounding that would generate the 5 CASH accrual. A display must not turn the supplied 100 CASH debt into an invented principal field or label the 70 CASH entirely as principal. [A04, loan; A08]

**Status and remaining questions.** This is illustrative servicing arithmetic. A loan source program, accrual terms, payment-allocation rule, exact parties and asset, actual authorization and continuing-state representation remain to be supplied. Origination, payoff, collateral release and ACTUS conformance are separate work; the diagram stops at 70 CASH still owed. [A03; A04, loan]

## Treasury allocation: pay beneficiaries and carry two duties

A treasury allocation must account for beneficiary payments and the duties that remain. Keeping a positive balance differs from retaining enough of each asset to cover those duties. The proposed opening of 25 A and 10 B lets the reader inspect both outgoing payments and continuing backing. The source action itself requires only non-negative custody. [A02; A05, ERR-21]

The source call is `allocate(true,23,7,2)`: approval is true, units is 23, quotient is 7 and remainder is 2. The opening source state has `allocated=false` and `rounds=0`. The action requires approval and no previous allocation, checks a remainder below 3, and checks 23 = 7 × 3 + 2. It sets alpha’s A payment to twice the quotient, 14 A, and beta’s to one quotient, 7 A. The action separately specifies a fixed 2 A fee and a fixed 4 B payment to alpha. The B payment does not come from dividing 23. [A07, allocate]

The actual proposed transfers therefore debit treasury custody by 14 + 7 + 2 = 23 A and by 4 B. Alpha receives 14 A and 4 B; beta receives 7 A; the fee recipient receives 2 A. Allocation also creates a reserve duty of `remainder`, here 2 A, and a rebate duty of 6 B. The fee and reserve happen to have the same amount in this call, but their formulas and effects differ: the fee is a fixed outgoing payment; the reserve duty uses the remainder and remains owed to the owner. The units parameter is checked arithmetically, not debited as another transfer. [A07; A05, ERR-21]

With the stated backing premise, treasury custody closes at 25 − 23 = 2 A and 10 − 4 = 6 B. Those retained amounts match the newly created duties asset by asset. The source state advances to `allocated=true` and `rounds=1`; marker remains 77. The proposed proof requirements concern the quotient/remainder equations, the transfer calculation and the duty continuation. The source’s attributed limit remains: “Neither S13 nor the supplied V2 source requires custody to back liabilities.” The 25 A / 10 B backing choice therefore needs a consumer-enforced application policy. [A07; A05, ERR-21]

The supplied validator-facing requirement is: “Treasury custody, the identities of A and B and the beneficiary outputs are checked by the consuming validator.” `approved=false` fails the source precondition. Opus ERR-24 states: “In the proposed V2 consumer the Boolean is covered by the required signers' intents; that is the debited account's consent, not a separate governance decision.” No separate governance approver is supplied. [A01, treasury; A07; A05, ERR-24]

**Status and remaining questions.** Allocation discharges neither new duty. `rebate(amount)` can reduce the B rebate through a payment to the owner. S13 has no reserve-duty discharge action; the attributed V2 zero-duty Close rule leaves closing blocked while that reserve remains. Backing enforcement, distinct governance authentication and a complete reserve-release or migration lifecycle remain separate work. This is source arithmetic and a proposed backing worksheet, not an executed treasury transaction. [A04; A05; A07]
