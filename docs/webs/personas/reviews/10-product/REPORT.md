# Persona 10: Skeptical product lead

## 30-second teach-back

Moriarty Webs proposes a way for a financial application to name the proof roles a consuming transition requires and show how those roles relate to claim definitions, proof statements, context, and the consumer. A Web is described as the script-bound collection of Strands required for one transition; a Strand is an individual proof with its claim, statement, and binding metadata; a claim specification is the reusable definition of an assertion, subject, premises, and intended relation. The practical value is making assets, recipients, authority, fees, and remaining duties visible in the handoff.

What exists on this page today is an editorial concept with interactive role selection and worksheet arithmetic. The page reports a limited local evidence packet, but says no on-chain transaction is recorded, no general Web integration or genuine intent authorization is established, and its interactive examples are illustrations rather than execution or settlement.

## Product questions

- Who is the first intended user: a protocol designer, contract developer, wallet integrator, or financial application reviewer? The page does not name a primary audience.
- What can a developer use today beyond the editorial walkthrough and its local examples? The page says general integration is unestablished but does not identify a separately usable developer artifact or supported path.
- Does the hero's proof-to-transition promise describe a design goal or a currently available product capability? The nearby caveat answers that this page does not run proofs, signatures, or transactions, but the first-view promise is more aspirational than operational.
- Are the explorer's three rows producers or proof instances? The explorer answers this clearly: they are proposed role requirements, without concrete instances or independent producers.
- Is 112 CASH full backing part of current source behavior? The worksheet answers clearly: it is a proposal requiring a program change, not an S12 action.

## Findings

The main product gap is audience and first-use clarity. I can restate the design goal, but I cannot tell whose workflow should make this valuable first. The maturity caveat is candid, yet its expanded state puts the most decision-relevant status facts into a dense paragraph with many evidence identifiers. I would add a short plain-language “today / proposed / not yet” summary before that detail.

The worksheet's clear separation between “Source behavior (S12, not executed)” and “PROPOSED full-backing policy” is effective. Changing the payment amount updates the arithmetic while retaining a visible disclaimer that this is not execution, proof verification, or settlement. I found no observed interaction defect in the product questions I checked. Preferences and confusion notes are recorded separately in `FEEDBACK.json`.

## Review limits

This is one model persona's review, not real human user research. I used a fresh isolated Chromium browser with the frozen local HTML, blocked external requests, and inspected the supplied desktop and mobile top screenshots plus the explorer reference. I did not inspect implementation code, other persona reviews, or external evidence. This review reports the page's own maturity statements and makes no cryptographic or security correctness judgment.
