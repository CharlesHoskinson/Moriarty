## In one paragraph

The core should stay as strict as it is, and the September 19 product contract now says so: 'natural-language wishes require explicit formalization and an inspectable signing display'. Since September 30 the repository ships the engineer-facing half of a usable toolchain: an authoring beta with a readable checker, decimal unit literals, scenario and test files, starter templates, a signing display, a language server and a tutorial site, plus a Rust `mori` toolchain whose diagnostics name the mistake, point at it and suggest the fix. What is missing is the finance-facing half, and no file in the repository considers a finance professional or a non-engineer as a user. This issue names six gaps with evidence and a way to close each. None of it touches the U1/U2 implementation slice or weakens a gate. Backing analysis: [docs/research/2026-10-08-moriarty-for-finance-professionals.md](https://github.com/rober-m/Moriarty/blob/research/finance-professionals-2026-10-08/docs/research/2026-10-08-moriarty-for-finance-professionals.md).

## Where we stand (October 8, 2026)

- Product and roadmap: Moriarty is 'a programming language for all DeFi developers on Midnight', compiled to ZKIRv3, with developer tooling inside the product. The roadmap is one U0 to U7 sequence; MC08 and SP12 map to U7. The focus is 'one implementation slice on U1/U2'. The beta and the Rust toolchain were delivered as precursors.
- Authoring beta (`moriarty-beta/1`): `init`, `check`, `fmt`, `inspect`, `expand`, `simulate`, `test`, `intent`, `verify-intent`, `lsp`, `mcp`. Only transfer and interest-first repayment prepare locally, as `PreparedUnqualified`. The eight DeFi family examples are structure-only.
- Rust toolchain: `check` and `format` implemented with miette diagnostics; nine subcommands are stubs; `RUST_GUIDELINES.md` says 'the surface syntax is not frozen'.
- Midnight Preview: the September 17 loan run finalized four stages, paid 533,972,602 test-asset units, exited zero, with both independent approvals. The September 10 swap stands.
- Native proof: on October 2 a fixed fixture produced a finalized proof that applied with real verification and was independently verified, on a hand-specialized kernel with a trusted development genesis. No public transaction.
- Tests: TypeScript language experiment 938 tests, 934 pass (four need the Compact compiler). Rust workspace 146 tests, all pass. Beta package: 193 tests, 115 pass, 12 skipped; all 66 failures need the native signature verifier binary, which is not built on this machine.
- Usability evidence: six model-run trials (three Sonnet 5.5, three Grok 4.7). The result file says 'these are artificial-user trials, not a human usability study' and 'a measured human onboarding study remains unperformed'.

## What is already solved

A one-character typo, `Treasry` for `Treasury`, through the Rust binary:

```
mori::check::unknown_name
  × I cannot find a declaration named `Treasry`.
    ╭─[typo.mori:13:56]
 13 │   operation: transfer(from: Buyer, to: Seller, fee_to: Treasry, value: price, fee: fee),
    ·                                                        ───┬───
    ·                                                           ╰── not declared above
  help: Did you mean `Treasury`?
```

A plain `10` where `0.10 USD` was meant gives 'I cannot add a `USD` amount and a plain number' with both operands labeled. The TypeScript beta reports `BETA_REFERENCE at 13:56: Unknown or forward reference Treasry`. Both read the profile from the file. A wrong test expectation reports `/post/balances/0/amount`, expected 8991, actual 8990. These close the diagnostics and front-door problems from the October 7 draft for the beta surface.

## Gaps and how to close them

### 1. The vocabulary stops at amounts

The beta has `10.00 USD` literals with a declared scale, named parties and named obligations. It has 'no floating point, division, rounding or implicit asset conversion'. Accrued interest is a scenario input, not something the language computes. A cash price and a repayment amount share one type, `Qty<USD>`, so the owed-versus-paid distinction Source/5 keeps through two types is not visible. Three of the starter's 17 lines are signed plumbing (key, nonce, pre-head, hashes, six explicit empties), by design.

**How to close it.** Finance vocabulary on the beta surface, through the review the Rust guidelines already describe: 'rate 10% per period, rounded down in the lender's favor', 'period 30 days from March 10, 2026', '100 USD paid' versus '100 USD owed'. Each form elaborates to exact atoms and to accrual terms a kernel already enforces (the Source/5 lifecycle kernel has numerator, denominator, rounding and period); the beta's own full-language horizon already proposes a rounding enum with a named beneficiary. Two spellings for owed and paid become mandatory. No new profile number: `/6` is used by the MIL/4 Source/6 stage syntax and reserved by the U0 proposal for the Aeon grammar.

### 2. One action, one snapshot, JSON in and out

A scenario is a closed JSON record with amounts as atom strings (`"200000"` is 2,000.00 USD at scale 2). A test binds one source, one action, one scenario and one exact expectation. `simulate` prints about 4.9 KB of JSON. There is no sequence of events and no table.

**How to close it.** Multi-step scenarios that chain each post-state into the next call, a dated cash-flow and balance table in decimals with CSV export, and `mori init --template loan` that asks term-sheet questions and writes the signed plumbing as placeholders marked unverified, as the starter already marks its own. Tooling only. Output stays labeled local stipulation.

### 3. Invariants are enforced but invisible

The kernel's fixed effects carry the invariants (conservation, caps, interest before principal), and the roadmap already says 'obligation and trust reports accompany U0–U2'. Nothing shows a finance professional which invariants held.

**How to close it.** Invariants chosen by name, reported as held, not checked, or unsupported. Unknown or timeout never renders as success. For Source/5 the mechanism is relational postconditions; replacing the seven hard-coded postconditions in the loan's `repay` with two relational ones passes the checker, reproduces all six lifecycle outcomes and accepts a payment of 50 that the original rejects.

### 4. There is a signing display, but no agreement to approve

The beta has a labeled, escape-safe display of every signed statement field with decimal amounts. It covers one signed intent, as rows.

**How to close it.** Extend it to the whole agreement and render prose: 'Buyer pays Seller 10.00 USD with a 0.10 USD fee to Treasury. The most that can leave Buyer's account is 10.10 USD. Seller receives at least 10.00 USD.' Every bound and duty rendered, tests pin the wording of every construct, labeled non-normative, never produced by a language model.

### 5. Templates have no finance math

Two starters exist (transfer, repay). The eight family files are structure-only and reject as `SpecifiedOnly`.

**How to close it.** Pool, collateralized loan, vault, then ACTUS PAM, LAM and ANN, built as the first consumer of the U6 libraries so the generated `.mori` is checked by the same pipeline as any program. Templates never choose the profile and are never admitted directly.

### 6. Nobody has watched a human use it

The only usability evidence is six model-run trials. They found unclear units and IDs, failed tests that hid the differing field, and misleading formatting, and the fixes shipped. A human will find the next class, and a finance professional will find a different one.

**How to close it.** Once the layers above exist, a study with three to five finance professionals who do not write software. Three tasks: read the agreement rendering of a loan and find a deliberately wrong rounding rule; write a scenario from a term sheet and read the cash-flow table without help; fix a typo from the error message alone. Record time, stalls and what they think the words mean. This is the validation step for everything above, so it comes last. A short dry run on the beta as it ships today would calibrate the tasks, but it is not the deliverable. Label every result as observation of a beta with `PreparedUnqualified` output; it is not an SP12 pilot.

## What we will not do

- Start a new source profile number.
- Hide signed terms; templates fill placeholders and mark them unverified.
- Let a language model write approval text or touch admission. The beta's AI tools are read-only, and that is the right boundary.
- Claim anything beyond `PreparedUnqualified` for local results.
- Touch the U1/U2 slice, or report any of this as progress on a U milestone.

## Dependencies

Not a sequence; only what each item needs before it can start.

- Multi-step scenarios, the cash-flow table, trust reports and the agreement rendering need no language change and read public output only. Any of them can start on the beta as it is.
- Finance vocabulary needs the beta's own grammar and checker review. The surface is not frozen.
- The loan template needs the vocabulary, or it can only fill what the beta can already say.
- Templates with finance math wait for the U6 libraries.
- The usability study runs on whatever has been assembled, and last, because it measures the rest.

All of these sit under the roadmap's 'authoring tools and libraries' heading as precursors, the same status the beta has.

## Decisions needed

1. Finance vocabulary into `moriarty-beta/1` as an extension (recommended, the surface is not frozen) or into a `moriarty-beta/2`.
2. Template order: DeFi first (recommended, Preview evidence exists) or ACTUS first.
3. Who owns the English wording. It should be a finance reviewer.
4. Who recruits the finance users for the study once the layers exist.
