## Decisions

**SECURITY / constraint composition: APPROVE, source-scoped.**

Stage 1 uses the actual Sub arithmetic producer and actual Sub state lanes:

> `data: sub_value_table_from_data(&input.state0.data),`
> `pre_helper: input.state0.helper.clone(),`

in `emit_b_sub_to_publication_chain`. The helper then converts only through the exhaustive common-carrier maps for chain/P1 views. It is not relabelled as Add: stage 3 calls

> `emit_k_machine_return_right_sub_block(`

in the same function, and the Sub return-right producer guards the popped frame with `BINARY_OP_SUB`. Frames and control are preserved through stages 1–2 by the chain complements, so `RightF.op == 1` and the Sub helper mode reach the correct consumers.

Stage 2 makes an exactly-one staged-kind partition:

> `let s2_branch_sum = b.add(exit_enable.clone(), failure_enable.clone());`
> `b.eq(s2_branch_sum, enable.clone());`

Both actual Sub completion producers are called, and each receives its own complement:

> `emit_b_sub_completion_complement(`
> `&exit_enable, &input.state1, &input.s2_exit_post,`

with the corresponding failure call for `s2_fail_post`. Disabled branch posts are pinned by `constrain_stutter_unless`, and `state2` is the publish mux, matching the Add chain structure without reusing Add completion types.

The required cursor repair is present at every main and per-branch state. The chain iterates all eight views, including both branch posts:

> `for st in views {`
> `constrain_ref_cursor_scalar_matches_bytes(`

That helper ranges every byte and equates the reconstructed 16-byte LE value with the scalar unconditionally. It does not rely on the completion complement, whose cursor binding remains enable-gated.

Stage 3 discharges the stated carrier obligations on the actual shared Sub operands:

> `b.gated_eq(&legacy_enable, a2.counters.task_live.clone(), immediate(0));`
> `let fresh_next = b.add(a2.result.scalar_value.index.clone(), immediate(1));`
> `b.gated_eq(&exit_block.applied, fresh_next, a2.data.active_rows.clone());`

The legacy handoff places `task_live` at `rest[3]`, in the order

> `task_live, active_data_fields, active_env, active_value_slots`

after the three allocation lanes. The UInt-row check is also present for every exit write selector:

> `b.gated_eq(write_sel, row.tag.clone(), immediate(DATA_TAG_U128));`

Zip truncation is not left open: publish-state validation fixes frame, data, helper, result, and counter lengths before the muxes, and the primitive producers validate their posts against `leg_state3` before `constrain_legacy_mux` consumes them.

The cap path composes correctly. `legacy_enable` is `applied + failure_enable`; `prim_value_enable` is `applied`; `prim_error_enable` is `failure_enable`. On `cap_refused`, all three later enables are zero, return-right and both primitive rows stutter on their legacy lanes, outcome/data complements preserve the non-legacy lanes, and disabled P1 imposes full publication stutter. The final outcome therefore remains `Exhausted(DataNodes)`, rather than being relabelled or published.

Stage 4 honors the supplied post-site contracts:

> `expect_post_site(s4_val.post_site, BPostSiteContract::ReturnDispatch)?;`
> `expect_post_site(s4_err.post_site, BPostSiteContract::TerminalP1)?;`

At depth greater than zero it derives the dispatched site from the actual top frame and refuses the reserved tag:

> `let top_tag_core = b.less_than(`
> `eff_top_tag.clone(),`
> `immediate(FRAME_TAG_CONSTRUCTOR_FIELDS_EXTENSION_RESERVED),`
> `);`
> `b.assert(top_tag_core);`

Stage 5 enables P1 only for an error or for a value whose post-primitive depth is zero:

> `let p1_val_ready = b.mul(prim_value_enable.clone(), s4_depth_zero);`
> `let p1_enable = b.add(p1_val_ready, prim_error_enable.clone());`

Caller premises remain correctly external: unique `scope`, no other producer owning or prefix-overlapping the derived namespaces, canonical arena/StepInv/provenance, physical AB, bounds, and exactly-one upstream dispatch. The candidate does not silently claim those.
