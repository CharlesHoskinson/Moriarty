**Recommendation: use five separately accounted cost planes, two resource meters, and explicit service quotes for capital risk. Do not create a kernel token or promise recurring free work in exchange for refundable stake.**

This is a proposed interface, not an implemented or benchmarked profile. Protocol observations below come from files read during this review. All Moriarty prices, limits and bond amounts are proposed launch parameters.

**1. Cost planes**

The four-plane starting position is incomplete: foreign execution consumes resources independently of Midnight settlement and orchestration. It also incorrectly suggests that all orchestration costs can be explained by bytes and compute. Inventory, underwriting and availability have separate economic costs.

| Plane | What it prices; unit | Payer → recipient | Binding |
|---|---|---|---|
| Midnight settlement | Ledger acceptance; DUST | Transaction funder → Midnight’s resource mechanism | Outside the contract-call statement. An SDK funding offer is not circuit evidence. |
| Foreign execution | Gas, bandwidth, account creation, venue and bridge charges; native asset atoms or venue fee asset | Named leg payer → foreign mechanism, venue or intermediary | Signed `ForeignFeeBudget` and foreign payload limits; actual expenditure is imported evidence. |
| Orchestration services | Payload bytes, registered jobs, inventory commitment and observation risk; exact settlement-asset atoms | User, sponsor or operator → named service providers | `Quote`, `JobTicket`, `SponsorGrant`, escrow and accepted receipts. |
| Application | Application distribution or financial product service; settlement-asset atoms | Principal → approved application | Revocable capability, signed intent, ordinary `Fee` effect and gross-debit caps. |
| Priority | Optional ordering preference; venue-native amount or explicit rate | Explicitly opted-in payer → venue-designated recipient or burn | Separate `PriorityBid`; never inferred from an application fee. |

Priority remains separate accounting even where the foreign venue collects it with another charge. Every total is a **vector by domain-qualified asset**, not one scalar that adds DUST, TRX and stablecoins.

Use:

```text
AssetId { domain, issuer, assetReference }
Amount  { asset: AssetId, atoms: u128 }

FeeBudget {
  applicationCap,
  serviceCap,
  foreignCapsByLeg,
  priorityCapsByLeg,
  recoveryReserve,
  grossDebitCapsByAsset
}
```

`DUSTFundingOffer` is a separate SDK record. None of these records introduces a fifth language effect.

**Meter definitions.** Keep two resource dimensions:

- `PU`: one byte of canonical encoded, authorized payload delivered. Count envelope, selected quote, foreign payload and certificate bytes once per designated delivery. Exclude unsolicited quotes, redundant gossip and operator retries.
- `CU`: one registered work credit. A profile assigns fixed credits to a bounded job with an identifiable output. CU is **not** a claim about elapsed CPU time.

Proposed tariff, in one exact six-decimal settlement asset:

| Registered job | Charge |
|---|---:|
| Validate one intent envelope, at most 4 KiB | 100 CU |
| Simulate one plan within its registered bounds | 400 CU |
| Produce one valid foreign signature | 1,000 CU |
| Produce one valid attestor response | 400 CU |
| Produce one fused proof for the registered entry-point profile | 2,000 CU |
| Deliver one scheduled observation | 10 CU |
| Submit one authorized transaction and return its submission receipt | 100 CU |
| Payload delivery | Actual PU |

Fallback prices: **1 micro-unit/PU** and **100 micro-units/CU**. Thus 1,000 CU costs 0.10 settlement units. These prices are proposals; Tron’s sun prices cannot justify their dollar value.

Solver search is at the solver’s expense unless the principal separately purchases a bounded research job. A winning solver earns a quoted execution fee or disclosed spread. An attestor’s liability premium is also a quoted amount, not fabricated CU.

**Acquisition and recovery.**

Provide two methods:

```text
reserveQuote(quoteHash, payerAllocations)
buyCapacity(provider, profileHash, PUCapacity, CUCapacity, term)
```

The first prepays the quoted upper bound and reserves the corresponding resources until expiry. The second buys a fixed provider commitment. Neither requires a native kernel token.

For a capacity lease, use independent token buckets with `W = 86,400 seconds`:

```text
available_m(t) =
  min(C_m, available_m(t0) + C_m × (t − t0) / W)

available_m(after job) = available_m(before job) − consumed_m
```

Retain fractional refill credit in integer fixed-point state; reading a bucket must not alter recovery. Reservations remove availability immediately; cancellation restores it.

This is a deliberately simpler recovery rule than Tron’s implementation, which decays prior usage and, under Stake 2.0 paths, recomputes a weighted recovery window. [ResourceProcessor.java:65](/home/charl/protocol-graph/repos/tron-java/chainbase/src/main/java/org/tron/core/db/ResourceProcessor.java:65), [ResourceProcessor.java:163](/home/charl/protocol-graph/repos/tron-java/chainbase/src/main/java/org/tron/core/db/ResourceProcessor.java:163)

An illustrative 24-hour lease with `100,000 PU` and `10,000 CU`, initially full, permits at most twice those capacities during its first day. At the proposed fallback prices, **2.20 settlement units** funds that maximum service liability. A provider can quote a discount, but must fund it itself. Lease revenue pays providers; consuming included capacity does not also incur fallback charges.

Refundable collateral alone cannot finance indefinitely recurring computation. Reject that part of the stake-based starting position unless an independent revenue source is identified.

Unused fallback escrow is refundable after outstanding reservations clear. A purchased lease expires at its stated end without automatic renewal. Purchased unused capacity is not refundable except for provider breach. Security bonds follow the longer liability exit schedule below. Tron’s documented staking exit is 14 days, with resource capacity removed when unstaking begins; it is a reference for explicit exits, not a reason to lock every customer deposit. [stake-and-resources.md:58](/home/charl/protocol-graph/corpus/tron-docs/docs/clients/wallet-cli/typescript/guide/stake-and-resources.md:58)

**Payer waterfall.**

At quote acceptance, assign each charge component to a payer. Within that payer’s allocation: use designated sponsored capacity, then owned capacity, then funded fallback escrow. No implicit payer reassignment.

| Operation class | Allocation rule |
|---|---|
| Quote discovery | Solver funds its search and unsolicited responses. Application funds any advertised free discovery service. Paid simulation requires a job ticket. |
| User-requested validation, simulation, signing and proving | Required sponsor allocation first; explicitly agreed user remainder second. Sponsor failure rejects reservation. |
| Attestation and watching | Reserved observation budget funds agreed responses and availability, including valid reports that the swap failed. |
| Foreign submission | Named leg funder fronts native fees; reimbursement follows the signed leg budget. |
| Cancellation | Requester pays verified work already executed; unused reservations return. |
| Recovery | Consume the separately reserved recovery budget. Objective operator-fault liability is charged to that operator’s collateral. |
| Operator retries, malformed output, redundant submissions | Operator pays. No new user debit. |
| Claims | Claimant pays claim transaction costs, optionally batched. |

NEAR’s delegation specification explicitly has the relayer inspect whether its reward is included. It also distinguishes gas refunds to the relayer from deposit refunds to the user. Thus sponsorship cannot safely assume all refunds return to the funder. [nep-0366.md:39](/home/charl/protocol-graph/corpus/near-neps/neps/nep-0366.md:39)

```text
SponsorGrant {
  sponsor, beneficiary, grantNonce, expiry,
  allowedOperationClasses, allowedPrimitiveHashes,
  asset, totalCap, perIntentCap,
  PUCap, CUCap, maxConcurrentReservations,
  refundRecipient, requiredCoverage
}
```

Grant revocation blocks new reservations; it cannot withdraw resources already committed to an accepted job. Default sponsored execution excludes arbitrary user-supplied primitives.

**Caps and defaults.**

Proposed pilot defaults:

- `maxPayload = 65,536 PU`; `maxCompute = 20,000 CU` per intent.
- At most eight solicited quotes, four foreign legs and twenty observation samples.
- Quote validity: 30 seconds.
- Service spending cap: the quote’s maximum charge, including itemized risk premiums.
- Recovery reserve: separately quoted; 0.50 settlement units in the example below.
- Priority: disabled.
- All retries share the original aggregate budget.

A fully sponsored operation may legitimately have a **zero user cap**. The starting position should reject an unspecified budget, not prohibit explicit zero liability.

Fallback avoids failure from *capacity exhaustion alone*. It must still stop at the signed money cap. “Exhausted capacity charges; it does not hard-stop” is wrong if interpreted as unlimited debit authority.

**Failure charging and evidence.**

A `JobTicket` binds `{intentId, jobId, provider, profileHash, inputHash, outputPredicate, maximumUnits, maximumCharge, deadline}`. A `MeterReceipt` adds actual units, output hash, result tag and cumulative channel sequence.

Pay for an accepted result or explicitly purchased availability. Reject self-reported CPU time. For cancellation, bill completed bounded sub-jobs; an indivisible incomplete proof job earns nothing unless its ticket explicitly purchased a reservation.

Malformed or unauthorized requests create no user debt. Operator failures forfeit the affected fee and pay agreed replacement costs. Foreign gas already burned cannot be reversed; refunds require an actual liable funder.

Keep receipts off-chain and settle per-provider channels every **60 seconds or 256 receipts**, whichever comes first. Reserve provider-specific escrow before work so an off-chain balance cannot be spent with several providers. Each channel settlement obeys its own head read-then-write discipline. Receipt batching requires benchmarked bounds.

A signed receipt authenticates its author; it does not prove a foreign observation. Such evidence still uses the adopted import mechanism.

**Congestion.**

Tron documents a six-hour adjustment period, a base-Energy threshold of five billion, a 20% increase factor and maximum penalty factor 3.4. The decrease factor is one-quarter of the increase factor. [resource.md:254](/home/charl/protocol-graph/repos/tron-docs/docs/mechanism-algorithm/resource.md:254), [resource.md:277](/home/charl/protocol-graph/repos/tron-docs/docs/mechanism-algorithm/resource.md:277)

Proposed Moriarty rule:

```text
epoch = 6 hours
threshold = 50,000 accepted base CU per primitive per epoch
f_initial = 0
f_next = min((1 + f) × 1.20 − 1, 3.40)  when usage > threshold
f_next = max((1 + f) × 0.95 − 1, 0)     otherwise

chargedCU = ceil(baseCU × (1 + f))
```

Use integer millionths for `f`. The maximum charge is 4.4 times base CU. The 50,000 threshold is an unbenchmarked proposal, not a scale conversion from Tron.

Freeze the factor in accepted quotes. Group aliases of the same registered primitive into one congestion bucket. Exclude operator-generated retries and free synthetic traffic. Congestion revenue pays additional service capacity; no author royalty applies to the surcharge. Global overload still needs admission control and latency limits.

My proposed roles are:

| Role | Work and risk | Payment | Misbehavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solver | Prices outcomes, reserves inventory, fills foreign legs; risks inventory, price movement and locked capital | Winning quote’s fixed fee or disclosed spread; no payment for losing bids | No completion fee without fulfillment; performance collateral pays agreed replacement costs. |
| Attestor | Independently extracts foreign facts and signs the pinned observation policy; risks accepting false facts | Fixed observation fee plus separate exposure premium, independent of whether the reported outcome benefits the solver | Invalid response earns nothing. Equivocation can be objectively penalized. Other false claims require the explicit adjudication mechanism below. |
| Sponsor | Reserves fee funds or capacity; risks committed subsidy and fronted expenditure | Promotional sponsor earns zero directly; commercial sponsor receives an explicit financing/service fee | Unfunded grant rejected; committed funds cannot be withdrawn. No authority to enlarge user liabilities. |
| Relayer | Submits already authorized payloads; risks fronted native fees and failed submission | Fixed submission fee plus bounded actual-cost reimbursement | Unauthorized or duplicate submissions are unreimbursed; provable breach consumes its service collateral. |
| Primitive author | Supplies an immutable bounded artifact; risks development effort and reputation | Optional **10% of that primitive’s base compute revenue**, disclosed in the profile | Defective version loses future admission and royalty eligibility. No automatic slash for ordinary software authorship. |
| Prover | Generates the required fused proof; risks compute expenditure and deadline failure | Fixed profile-priced fee for a valid proof | Invalid proof earns zero; missed committed delivery pays agreed replacement costs from service collateral. |

| Foreign signer / MPC operator | Signs only the authorized network-bound payload; risks key compromise and unauthorized signatures | Fixed signature-job fee plus any separately quoted custody risk premium | Invalid output unpaid; unauthorized signatures trigger the bonded-authority policy. |

Pure authorship does not justify the same bond as authority over an oracle or signing key. An author who also operates a mutable oracle assumes the attestor/operator obligations.

NEAR’s signing code requires a one-yoctoNEAR deposit, but that is not evidence of a commercially adequate MPC operator wage or its distribution. **Collateral and adjudication.** Start with a fixed **5-of-7 observation quorum**, as a proposed deployment configuration. Keep it independent of the solver and application benefiting from the observation.

For pilot agreements, cap aggregate unresolved value exposure at **100,000 settlement units**. Require each observation operator’s separately reserved bond to be at least:

```text
max(10,000 settlement units, 2 × aggregate exposure / 5)
```

At the pilot ceiling, that is 40,000 per operator. Collateral must not simultaneously back another exposure pool.

This is collateral arithmetic, not a proof of safety. A dishonest quorum can agree on one false claim without equivocating. For that case, choose a separately identified, deployment-bound adjudication quorum and disclose that its verdict is another trusted import. If that trust is unacceptable, do not launch that foreign route before certificate verification exists.

Penalties compensate verified losses and replacement costs before any residual penalty is disposed of. This deliberately differs from HIP-3’s burn policy. Bonds exit no earlier than **14 days after notice and seven days after the last covered exposure closes**, with unresolved disputes extending the lock.

For solvers and ordinary service providers, reserve a per-job performance bond equal to the quoted replacement/recovery liability; do not impose a universal token stake.

There is also a documentation contradiction: the Hyperliquid node README says five-minute staking withdrawal and commission cannot increase, whereas the archived staking page says seven days and permits increases up to 1%. I do not use those disputed details as Moriarty precedent. **4. Application fee**

Adopt a proportional fee with an absolute amount ceiling:

```text
FeeCapability {
  principal, application, recipient, capabilityId, epoch,
  allowedAssetIds, allowedMarketClasses,
  maxRateTenthBps, maxAmountPerIntent, expiry
}

ApplicationFee {
  capabilityId, capabilityEpoch,
  feeAsset, basisAsset, basisKind,
  rateTenthBps, amountCap
}
```

For version one, require the fee asset and basis asset to match. Calculate:

```text
feeAtoms = floor(executedBasisAtoms × rateTenthBps / 100,000)
```

Reject a plan whose calculated fee exceeds an applicable cap. Do not silently clip the fee.

Use the externally exchanged principal amount as the swap basis, excluding fees, refunds, self-transfers and intermediate hops. Charge once per intent. The rate and basis must not multiply merely because a solver uses more legs.

Proposed protocol ceilings:

- Spot/cross-chain exchange: `1,000` tenth-bps = **1%**.
- Derivatives, if later admitted: `100` tenth-bps = **0.1%**.
- Unclassified operations: no application fee until the profile defines the basis and cap.
- Maximum ten active capabilities per principal.

These rate units, market caps and approval-count limits follow the archived Hyperliquid builder specification. [hl-builder-codes.md:11](/home/charl/Moriarty/raw/sources/defi-kernel-multichain-2026-09-11/hl-builder-codes.md:11)

Methods:

```text
approveFeeCapability(...)
revokeFeeCapability(capabilityId)
revokeAllFeeCapabilities()
claimFees(asset, amount, recipient)
```

Only the principal approves or expands capability limits. Session keys can use an existing capability within their own narrower authority.

Revocation invalidates unreserved intents. Once a pending operation has reserved its exact maximum application charge under a valid capability, revocation cannot retroactively confiscate an earned fee; it blocks new reservations and increases.

Charge only on successful settlement for the initial all-or-nothing profile. Accrue into a prefunded fee vault through the existing `Fee` effect. Claim failure must not revert the user’s completed transaction. `claimFees` uses the existing transfer mechanism.

Report:

```text
FeeSummary {
  totalsByAsset,
  applicationComponent,
  serviceComponents,
  foreignComponents,
  priorityComponents,
  payerContributions,
  refunds,
  evidenceClassByComponent
}
```

Do not add the application component to a total that already includes it. Hyperliquid’s archived fill response explicitly marks `fee` as inclusive of `builderFee`; builder fees are claimed through its reward process. [hl-api-info.md:1506](/home/charl/Moriarty/raw/sources/defi-kernel-multichain-2026-09-11/hl-api-info.md:1506), [hl-builder-codes.md:15](/home/charl/Moriarty/raw/sources/defi-kernel-multichain-2026-09-11/hl-builder-codes.md:15)

**5. Protocol value accrual**

Charge a disclosed **5% share of base metered service revenue**, included inside the tariff:

- 3% for maintenance, adapter validation and audits.
- 2% for an operational reserve.

The performing operator receives 95%, or 85% on an author-royalty-eligible compute job; the author receives the additional 10%. These are allocations of an existing charge.

Take no protocol percentage of transferred principal, native gas reimbursements, application fees, solver inventory premiums or slashed collateral. No kernel token, emissions or buyback is required.

The argument for this limited share is that common infrastructure has recurring costs even when execution is outsourced. The argument against a turnover tax is that transferred value does not measure work: moving 100,000 units need not cost 100 times as much as moving 1,000.

NEAR’s reward calculator has an explicit treasury allocation, including a one-tenth branch for its production genesis configuration. That demonstrates explicit allocation machinery, not an appropriate Moriarty rate. [reward_calculator.rs:63](/home/charl/protocol-graph/corpus/near-core/chain/epoch-manager/src/reward_calculator.rs:63)

Reject a kernel priority auction in version one. Forward supported foreign priority bids at cost. Hyperliquid does document burning both gossip and order priority fees, but that alone gives Moriarty no reason to manufacture another ordering market. [hl-priority-fees.md:13](/home/charl/Moriarty/raw/sources/defi-kernel-multichain-2026-09-11/hl-priority-fees.md:13), [hl-priority-fees.md:40](/home/charl/Moriarty/raw/sources/defi-kernel-multichain-2026-09-11/hl-priority-fees.md:40)

**7. The three hardest economic decisions**

| Decision | Options | My choice |
|---|---|---|
| What funds recurring capacity? | Native-token stake; refundable stablecoin collateral; paid fixed reservations | **Paid reservations and explicit fallback escrow.** Refundable stake cannot pay recurring operating costs without another revenue source. Keep security collateral separate. |
| Who adjudicates a false imported fact? | Same attesting quorum; independent adjudication quorum; certificate-only execution | **Independent, disclosed adjudication for a tightly capped pilot; certificates before broader exposure.** Never describe quorum signatures or bonds as objective truth. |
| Who earns revenue without authority over funds? | Mandatory royalties on every primitive; no author revenue; opt-in royalty from metered charges | **Opt-in 10% base-compute royalty, 5% common-service share, competitive operator quotes.** Bond mutable authority, not mere authorship. Exclude congestion penalties and retries from royalties. |

The largest remaining uncertainties are benchmark-derived CU weights and prices, commercially adequate attestor premiums, and enforceable loss adjudication. The sources justify the accounting boundaries and incentive distinctions; they do not establish those launch parameters as economically sufficient.
