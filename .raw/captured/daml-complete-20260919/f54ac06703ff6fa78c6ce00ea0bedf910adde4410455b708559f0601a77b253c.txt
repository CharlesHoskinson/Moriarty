Running dedicated synchronizers alongside the Global Synchronizer for mixed public and private workflows
Copy page
Copy page
A common deployment pattern on Canton Network combines dedicated synchronizers with the Global Synchronizer. Your validators connect to both, running high-volume or confidential workflows on the dedicated synchronizer while settling or sharing results through the Global Synchronizer.
​
Why use a hybrid setup
The Global Synchronizer provides network-wide interoperability and access to Canton Coin, but not every workflow benefits from running on shared infrastructure. A hybrid pattern makes sense when you have:
High-volume bilateral flows
— Frequent transactions between a small set of parties (e.g., trade confirmations between two counterparties) that would consume unnecessary traffic on the Global Synchronizer.
Confidential processing stages
— Intermediate computation steps that should stay on infrastructure you control, with only the final result published to the Global Synchronizer.
Latency-sensitive operations
— Workflows where you need lower latency than the Global Synchronizer provides, because your dedicated synchronizer runs fewer nodes.
Cost optimization
— Reducing Canton Coin traffic costs if your transaction throughput is high.
​
Architecture overview
In a hybrid deployment, each validator maintains connections to both the Global Synchronizer and one or more dedicated synchronizers. Contracts are assigned to the synchronizer appropriate for their current lifecycle stage.
Both validators can transact on either synchronizer. Contracts created on the dedicated synchronizer can later be reassigned to the Global Synchronizer (and vice versa) using Canton’s unassignment/reassignment protocol.
​
Contract assignment strategies
You control which synchronizer a contract lives on at the point of creation, and you can move contracts between synchronizers as needed.
Create on dedicated, settle on public:
Two parties create and update a trade contract on the dedicated synchronizer, where transaction volume is high and latency is low.
When the trade is ready for settlement, the submitting party reassigns the contract to the Global Synchronizer.
Settlement executes on the Global Synchronizer, where it can interact with other RWAs and contracts held by other network participants.
Create on public, process on dedicated:
A contract is created on the Global Synchronizer so all parties can discover and interact with it.
Once two parties agree to work together, they reassign the contract to a dedicated synchronizer for intensive processing.
The final result is reassigned back to the Global Synchronizer.

Considerations
Traffic costs
— Transactions on the dedicated synchronizer and the Global Synchronizer incur traffic fees.
Validator overlap
— All parties involved in a contract must have their validators connected to the synchronizer where that contract is assigned. Plan your synchronizer topology accordingly.
Ordering guarantees
— Each synchronizer provides its own total ordering. Cross-synchronizer transactions are synchronized by the Canton protocol but have higher latency than same-synchronizer transactions.
Operational overhead
— Running a dedicated synchronizer means operating sequencer and mediator infrastructure in addition to your validator.
