# Shared context for the Moriarty DeFi interface decision

## What Moriarty is

A bounded financial language that settles on Midnight. Agreements are programs; actions emit a closed set of four effects (Transfer, Fee, DueCreated, DueSettled); obligations are first class; authority is a signed intent in one of two modes (exact plan, or outcome with gross debit caps, net credit goals and permitted recipients). The language is Turing-incomplete by construction, with registered bounds enforced by content hash.

## Settlement model already adopted (do not redesign)

- Ledger-anchored certified state machine: one fused proof per entry point; history by induction over ledger acceptance; head read-then-write discipline; recursion reserved for bounded certificates.
- Entry points: Initialize, Step, Split, Join, Terminate, optional Pause; cross-contract Release, JoinFrom, Migrate, ImportFrom, Reclaim.
- DUST pays Midnight settlement, is not bound into the contract-call statement, and is invisible to the contract circuit. Under-funding is a liveness failure, not a validity failure.
- A contract-call proof cannot be an inner proof, so a foreign leg cannot be verified by recursion over a Midnight proof.

## Known gaps blocking a multichain kernel

1. Two disjoint language lineages; the rule joining them is an unregistered proposal.
2. `permittedCalls` exists in the signed outcome statement but must be empty (no external-call effect).
3. No pending state: anything needing residual progress is rejected.
4. Asset identity is bare text with no chain.
5. Predecessor fan-in is 1; split/join unsupported in the registered profile.
6. No signed liability cap; caps do not bound new nominal debt.
7. No foreign-payload signing, no solver interface, no fee accounting outside settlement.

## Prior study conclusions (the starting position, open to challenge)

Four cost planes that must not merge: settlement (DUST), application fee (an in-language Fee effect already bound by signed caps), orchestration (new: quoting, solving, foreign signing, watching), and priority (ordering, priced separately).

Reference models drawn from the three protocols:
- Tron: staked metered capacity, two meters (bytes and compute), priced burn fallback, published payer waterfall, deployer subsidy, per-contract congestion pricing, explicit exit schedule.
- Hyperliquid: application fee as a signed field of the order under a revocable ceiling approved by the principal (not a session key), protocol-level caps, itemised in the fill, accrued and claimed off the hot path; priority as its own burned lane; two operator risk classes (cheap and revocable versus bonded and slashable).
- NEAR: chain signatures with one derivation path per chain; meta-transaction sponsorship with two stated hazards (balance refunds go to the sender, and function-call key allowances are not checked through delegation); intents as balance deltas that must net to zero; a salted rotating nonce giving bulk invalidation; millisecond-scale solver auctions.

Safety core: a fact the Midnight ledger cannot see is an *import*, never an anchored step. First version imports by threshold attestation with the quorum bound at deploy time; certificates later.
