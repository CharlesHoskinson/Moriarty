**My vote is no-go for a value-moving multichain SDK under the current profile.** The starting position contains useful design patterns, but it does not establish safe cross-chain execution, bounded recovery, or enforceable off-ledger spending.

This is a source review, not a deployed-behaviour test. All interface types, errors and safeguards below are recommendations. The design itself is explicitly “specified-only,” with mandatory Midnight Preview acceptance still required. [Design:4](/home/charl/Moriarty/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-11-defi-kernel-multichain-design.md:4)

**1. Where the starting position breaks**

- **Salt rotation is not the promised kill switch.** In the inspected NEAR Intents code, `SaltRegistry.set_new()` preserves the previous salt as valid. `invalidate(salt)` is separate; `is_valid()` accepts valid previous salts. Moreover, nonces that do not decode as versioned nonces bypass the salt/deadline checks in `verify_intent_nonce()`. The actual V1 layout is a 4-byte magic prefix, 1-byte version, 4-byte salt, 8-byte deadline and 15 random bytes—not simply four salt bytes plus 28 bytes of uniqueness. Moriarty must implement explicit, monotone invalidation without a legacy bypass. [salt_registry.rs:55](/home/charl/protocol-graph/corpus/near-intents/contracts/defuse/src/contract/state/salt_registry.rs:55), [engine/mod.rs:86](/home/charl/protocol-graph/corpus/near-intents/contracts/defuse/core/src/engine/mod.rs:86), [versioned.rs:9](/home/charl/protocol-graph/corpus/near-intents/contracts/defuse/core/src/nonce/versioned.rs:9)

- **Zero-sum deltas do not establish cross-chain atomicity.** NEAR’s matcher balances transfers among accounts, per token, inside its execution machinery; it rejects unmatched deltas and overflow. That is a useful local conservation check. It does not show that independently submitted foreign legs either all execute or all disappear. Also, one user’s swap need not sum to zero per asset: counterparties and fee collectors supply the balancing entries. Moriarty needs complete participant accounting and explicit bridge/conversion relations, not cancellation of similarly named assets across chains. [deltas.rs:233](/home/charl/protocol-graph/repos/near-intents/contracts/defuse/core/src/engine/state/deltas.rs:233), [deltas.rs:267](/home/charl/protocol-graph/repos/near-intents/contracts/defuse/core/src/engine/state/deltas.rs:267)

- **“Deadline, then recovery that cannot strand value” is unproved and generally impossible without additional funding or foreign enforcement.** Consider a foreign payment signed before the deadline, with its execution temporarily unobservable. Releasing the reservation can enable double spending; retaining it can strand funds. A deadline supplies no proof of nonexecution. The design currently promises more than its observation mechanism provides. [Design:116](/home/charl/Moriarty/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-11-defi-kernel-multichain-design.md:116)

- **Opening `permittedCalls` cannot create an external-call effect.** L2 can authorize an off-ledger action, but cannot make Midnight execute or observe it. Every resulting foreign fact still enters through import. Pending state, liabilities and composition also require admitted language changes; an SDK cannot simulate their existence with local bookkeeping. [Design:16](/home/charl/Moriarty/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-11-defi-kernel-multichain-design.md:16), [Design:103](/home/charl/Moriarty/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-11-defi-kernel-multichain-design.md:103)

- **Outcome authorization and head binding need separate definitions.** An outcome relation specifies permitted results; a nonce specifies replay control. Neither, by itself, removes a head from the signed digest. The proposed multichain mode must explicitly define its state binding and reserve funds against concurrent intents. The existing exact-head rule and proposed outcome mode otherwise leave an authorization gap. [Design:20](/home/charl/Moriarty/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-11-defi-kernel-multichain-design.md:20), [Design:89](/home/charl/Moriarty/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-11-defi-kernel-multichain-design.md:89)

- **“Fallback charges; it never hard-stops” contradicts bounded spending.** Once capacity and the signed monetary budget are exhausted, execution must stop before additional work. A sponsor shortfall cannot silently become user debt. The design’s fallback rule conflicts with its sponsorship and budget rules. Two billing meters also cannot replace the inherited independent resource counters or prove actual off-ledger compute consumption. [Design:22](/home/charl/Moriarty/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-11-defi-kernel-multichain-design.md:22), [Design:46](/home/charl/Moriarty/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-11-defi-kernel-multichain-design.md:46)

- **TRON’s resource model is not deterministic reserved capacity.** Its inspected energy allocation depends on global energy limits and total staking weight. Its deployer subsidy can fall back to the caller when the deployer cannot cover its share. Those are precisely the behaviours Moriarty’s fixed quote and sponsor rules must prevent. [EnergyProcessor.java:168](/home/charl/protocol-graph/corpus/tron-java/chainbase/src/main/java/org/tron/core/db/EnergyProcessor.java:168), [set-origin-energy-limit.md:15](/home/charl/protocol-graph/corpus/tron-docs/docs/clients/wallet-cli/typescript/commands/contract/set-origin-energy-limit.md:15)

- **TRON’s `fee_limit` is not a universal transaction-cost cap.** In the fixed-ratio code path, it limits caller energy through `feeLimit / sunPerEnergy`, including available frozen energy. Bandwidth charging has a separate waterfall and balance debit. Treating one field as “maximum everything I can lose” is unsafe. [VMActuator.java:583](/home/charl/protocol-graph/repos/tron-java/actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/VMActuator.java:583), [BandwidthProcessor.java:153](/home/charl/protocol-graph/corpus/tron-java/chainbase/src/main/java/org/tron/core/db/BandwidthProcessor.java:153)

Reject that adapter until this is demonstrated; adding an SDK metadata field would not fix it. **2. Failure modes, ranked by severity**

| Rank | Failure and triggering condition | User loss |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical 1** | False import: compromised quorum, correlated RPC sources, wrong extraction or wrong network certifies a nonexistent or incorrect payment. | Irreversible release of Midnight collateral or settlement assets. |
| **Critical 2** | Timeout recovery releases funds while a previously signed foreign transaction remains executable. | Double payment, unbacked claims or insolvency. |
| **Critical 3** | Two solvers use the same authorization before an anchored reservation; receipts or nonces are reusable across instances or recovery branches. | Repeated debits or repeated collateral release. |
| **Critical 4** | Payload, asset or authority confusion: signed bytes differ from reviewed bytes; destination, spender, network or token identity is misinterpreted. | Theft up to the foreign account’s accessible balance, potentially beyond the intended trade. |
| **Critical 5** | `DueCreated`, approvals or contingent obligations escape signed liability bounds. | Debt or future spending authority without a corresponding present debit. |
| **High 6** | Foreign leg succeeds while Midnight settlement, import or recovery cannot proceed because of DUST shortage, head contention or unavailable evidence. | Locked funds, ongoing market exposure and missed deadlines. |
| **High 7** | Sponsor capacity disappears; metering races or fallback billing exceed reservations. | Unexpected charges, sponsor depletion or an unfunded recovery. |
| **High 8** | “Success” means submission, signature generation, inclusion or a successful outer transaction rather than the required final financial result. | User releases consideration without receiving the promised asset. |
| **High 9** | Revocation or fee-cap changes are applied retroactively to already dispatched obligations. | One party keeps the foreign benefit while preventing the matching settlement. |
| **Medium 10** | Quote spam, repeated proving, retry amplification or free cancellation options exhaust capacity. | Bounded fees, unavailable service and execution delay. |

A concrete example of rank 8 exists in the inspected Hyperliquid bridge: withdrawal finalization can emit `FailedWithdrawal` and return rather than revert. Successful finalization additionally requires dispute-period checks and an authorized finalizer. An outer transaction’s success cannot substitute for the specific withdrawal outcome. [Bridge2.sol:338](/home/charl/protocol-graph/corpus/hl-contracts/Bridge2.sol:338), [Bridge2.sol:376](/home/charl/protocol-graph/corpus/hl-contracts/Bridge2.sol:376), [Bridge2.sol:398](/home/charl/protocol-graph/corpus/hl-contracts/Bridge2.sol:398)

**3. The refusal contract**

The SDK is not the security boundary. Every rule protecting Midnight value must also be enforced by the relevant circuit; every foreign-signing rule must be enforced by the signing service. A modified SDK must not bypass them.

Use a closed, versioned interface. Any unregistered input or extension is rejected by default.

Proposed concrete types:

```text
DomainId {
  namespace, networkId, genesisOrDeploymentCommitment
}

AssetId {
  domain: DomainId,
  kind: Native | ContractToken,
  issuerOrContract,
  tokenId
}

Amount       = u128 atomic units
SignedAmount = { negative: bool, magnitude: u128 }
RatePpm      = u32                 // denominator exactly 1,000,000
UnixMillis   = u64
BlockHeight  = { domain: DomainId, value: u64 }

IntentId = H(
  version, midnightNetwork, instanceId, programHash,
  principal, authorizationEpoch, nonce
)

SpendLimits {
  grossDebitCap: Map<AssetId, Amount>,
  minNetCredit: Map<AssetId, Amount>,
  newLiabilityCap: Map<AssetId, Amount>,
  orchestrationCap: Map<AssetId, Amount>,
  foreignFeeCap: Map<DomainId, Amount>,
  priorityCap: Map<DomainId, Amount>
}
```

`foreignFeeCap` and `priorityCap` use each domain’s explicitly identified fee asset. Different assets never net merely because they share a ticker. Use checked arithmetic and sufficiently wide intermediates; reject unrepresentable values.

`Quote` must bind `intentId`, `solverId`, `planHash`, `adapterHashes`, `expiresAtMs`, monetary caps, capacity reservation, fee recipients and `recoveryPolicyHash`. `SponsorGrant` must bind an intent, operation classes, assets, caps, expiry, refund destinations and reservation ID.

`ImportCertificate` must bind the exact foreign request and result, source domain, transaction ID, event/output index, block identity, finality policy, extractor hash, destination instance, intent, leg, quorum policy and observation time. A native certificate lacking those bindings needs an authenticated binding mechanism; unsigned wrapper metadata is insufficient.

Return `Result<T, Refusal>`, where `Refusal` carries `code`, `stage`, `intentId`, optional `legId`, and structured details. `UnknownExecution` is a durable state, not a synonym for failure.

Three additional boundary rules matter:

1. **DUST shortage is not an invalid financial proof.** Submission may return `Blocked(SettlementFundingUnavailable)` as a liveness result. Neither the circuit nor the SDK should claim that a proof certifies DUST sufficiency. [Design:16](/home/charl/Moriarty/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-11-defi-kernel-multichain-design.md:16)

2. **Revocation stops new reservations.** It does not erase duties created by an accepted reservation or invalidate evidence needed to finish recovery.

3. **Refunds do not replenish gross debit authority.** A debit of 100 followed by a refund of 100 still consumed 100 of gross debit allowance. A second debit requires remaining authorization.

The reference protocols expose specific traps requiring these refusals:

- **NEAR sponsorship:** runtime comments state that the relayer prepays attached deposits, but deposit refunds go to the sender while gas refunds go to the relayer. The inspected delegated function-call checks constrain action count, deposit, receiver and method, but do not establish a sponsor spending cap through the key allowance. Refuse sponsor-funded refundable principal unless the refund is explicitly a gift or recoverable through an enforceable arrangement; use a separate sponsor reservation. [actions.rs:515](/home/charl/protocol-graph/repos/near-core/runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs:515), [actions.rs:670](/home/charl/protocol-graph/repos/near-core/runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs:670)

- **NEAR MPC observation policy:** the inspected `ChainEntry.quorum` is documented as stored but not consumed; the whitelist checker is log-only, and the network probe is diagnostic-only. Those mechanisms do not demonstrate the fail-closed policy Moriarty requires. This is a scoped finding about those components, not proof that every signing path lacks other checks. [foreign_chain.rs:1971](/home/charl/protocol-graph/corpus/near-mpc/crates/near-mpc-contract-interface/src/types/foreign_chain.rs:1971), [foreign_chain_whitelist_verifier.rs:1](/home/charl/protocol-graph/corpus/near-mpc/crates/node/src/foreign_chain_whitelist_verifier.rs:1), [foreign_chain_probe.rs:18](/home/charl/protocol-graph/corpus/near-mpc/crates/node/src/foreign_chain_probe.rs:18)

Generic JSON signing or post-signature normalization is unacceptable. [signing.py:174](/home/charl/protocol-graph/corpus/hl-python-sdk/hyperliquid/utils/signing.py:174)

**4. Griefing and economic attacks**

| Attacker → payer | Attack | Required prevention |
|---|---|---|
| User → sponsor | Force failed delegated calls whose attached deposits refund to the user. | Sponsor gas separately; forbid recoverable-principal sponsorship without enforceable refund ownership. |
| User → solver/prover | Request expensive solving/proving repeatedly, then abort. | Small admission quota; explicit paid-work authorization; per-intent attempt and byte bounds; operator bears unauthorized work. |
| Solver → user | Inflate computation, invent retries or bill the same artifact repeatedly. | Prefer fixed artifact prices; bind tariff and operation IDs; deduplicate billing; cap total charges. A signed receipt alone does not prove work occurred. |
| Concurrent operators → sponsor | Spend the same grant before periodic reconciliation. | Exclusive reservations or separately allocated spending rights. Periodic anchoring alone does not prevent overspending. |
| Counterparty → signer | Advance a shared head repeatedly to invalidate proofs/signatures. | Reserve once; use the admitted asynchronous intent lifecycle; bound retries. Do not charge the user indefinitely for contention. |
| User/solver → counterparty | Keep a quote as a free option, executing only after favourable price movement. | Firm acceptance point, short signed expiry, reserved inventory and explicit cancellation economics. |
| Primitive author → user | Split work into many fee-bearing operations or manufacture congestion. | Registered work definitions, per-plan caps, aggregation rules and no discretionary multiplier changes after acceptance. |
| Operator → all users | Withdraw stake or collateral immediately before outstanding obligations fail. | Separate service-capacity stake from loss collateral; prevent exit until exposure and dispute obligations are discharged. |
| Attester → user | Withhold observation after foreign funds move, then demand more fees. | Prepaid bounded observation/recovery budget, alternate admissible submitters and collateral-backed compensation where promised. |

The design cannot objectively classify every timeout as “user abort” or “operator fault.” Use signed dispatch/acceptance records and fixed contractual responsibility. Unclassifiable service failures must not authorize additional user charges.

For each asset, require:

```text
availableRecoveryCollateral
  >= worstCaseOutstandingCompensation
   + reservedRecoveryFees
   + accruedUnpaidClaims
```

Use the same asset for backing where possible. Cross-asset collateral needs a separately admitted valuation and haircut policy; none is established here.

Charging dimensions may remain distinct, but the quote must expose **all** payers and maxima: DUST funding outside the circuit; in-language fees; off-ledger service charges; foreign native costs; and priority charges. A “four-plane” diagram must not hide a fifth bill.

**5. What the user must trust outside Midnight**

- **Foreign consensus and finality:** Midnight does not establish foreign history merely by accepting an attestation.
- **Attesting quorum:** its members report the correct network, extraction, finality and outcome, and do not collude or become unavailable together.
- **Observation infrastructure:** RPC providers, configuration, common software dependencies and any shared upstream data source. Multiple signers using one faulty source are correlated witnesses.
- **Foreign signing service:** it protects keys, enforces Moriarty authorization and signs only the reserved payload. A signature certificate proves signature validity, not financial execution.
- **Adapter semantics:** transaction decoding, token behaviour, network discrimination, fee accounting and event interpretation match deployed behaviour.
- **Foreign venues and assets:** administrators, upgrade mechanisms, custody, withdrawal controls and asset redemption arrangements remain foreign trust assumptions.
- **Solvers and operators:** inventory, availability, ordering claims, off-ledger accounting and compensation promises are dependable only to the extent enforced by funded arrangements.
- **Recovery execution:** someone submits the necessary Midnight transaction with DUST and supplies admissible evidence.
- **Governance:** admission, emergency controls and any migration preserve old liabilities and evidence rules.

For the inspected Hyperliquid bridge specifically, finalization is permissioned, locking depends on locker votes, and validator signatures are accepted only above two-thirds of power. These are distinct trust and liveness conditions, not one generic “threshold” property. [Bridge2.sol:376](/home/charl/protocol-graph/corpus/hl-contracts/Bridge2.sol:376), [Bridge2.sol:442](/home/charl/protocol-graph/corpus/hl-contracts/Bridge2.sol:442), [Bridge2.sol:746](/home/charl/protocol-graph/corpus/hl-contracts/Bridge2.sol:746)

A quorum bound at deployment prevents silent substitution; it does not solve compromise or permanent unavailability. Under immutable verification keys, recovery must have a predeclared path. “We will rotate the key later” is not an acceptable unstated assumption.

**6. What must be true before shipment**

- [ ] Required L1–L8 changes are admitted for the advertised feature set. Unsupported features remain rejected; fan-in stays **1** until a higher value is admitted.
- [ ] Complete lifecycle semantics exist for reservation, signature issuance, dispatch, unknown execution, import, compensation, refund and termination.
- [ ] Every crash boundary is tested, especially immediately before/after signing, broadcast, import and payout.
- [ ] Nonce reservation, fund reservation and head update occur atomically; duplicate solvers and duplicate submissions cannot duplicate authorization.
- [ ] Foreign receipt consumption prevents reuse across intents, legs, instances and recovery branches.
- [ ] Gross debit, net credit and nominal liability caps cover the whole lifecycle, including retries, fees and compensation.
- [ ] No normal or recovery action introduces a fifth effect or presents a foreign fact as anchored.
- [ ] Each adapter has retained deployed-behaviour evidence for encoding, network binding, permissions, fee limits, finality and financial success.
- [ ] Revocation, legacy-format rejection and nonce cleanup have adversarial replay tests.
- [ ] Quorum policy defines participants, threshold, independence assumptions, freshness, extraction, disagreement handling and loss limits.
- [ ] Threshold parameters have an explicit threat model. For example, **3-of-4** can tolerate one unavailable signer and requires three compromised shares to forge; it does not protect against a common faulty data source. This is an illustration, not a recommended deployment parameter.
- [ ] Meter profiles publish byte bounds, work limits, attempt limits, tariffs, rounding, quote lifetime and recovery budget. Missing benchmarked values block admission.
- [ ] Sponsor grants cannot be concurrently overspent, and sponsor failure never silently bills the user.
- [ ] Compensation is funded before irreversible foreign authority is issued. Its asset, amount, beneficiary and late-execution treatment are explicit.
- [ ] Product states distinguish `Submitted`, `UnknownExecution`, `Imported`, `Succeeded`, `Refunded` and `Compensated`.
- [ ] Mandatory verification-enabled Midnight Preview financial acceptance is demonstrated on the exact candidate, with independent review.
- [ ] Launch exposure is numerically capped per intent, instance, asset and quorum; automatic admission stops exist when those limits are reached.

No deployed adapter tests, finality experiments or benchmark results were performed in this review. The Moriarty status CLI could not open its database; I therefore make no current deployment or acceptance-status claim beyond the supplied design’s stated status.

**7. The three risks I would not accept**

1. **Irreversible user-funded foreign execution with recovery based only on timeout.** Demand enforceable foreign conditional execution, or pre-funded compensation that remains available despite quorum and operator failure. If neither exists, refuse the route.

2. **A threshold signature granting effectively unlimited authority to release Midnight value.** Demand exact fact binding, one-time receipt consumption, a pinned and tested observation policy, explicit correlated-failure assumptions and funded exposure limits. Do not market attested foreign state as verified foreign consensus.

3. **Unbounded or retrospectively assigned charges.** Demand signed per-asset caps, exclusive reservations, deterministic tariffs and a hard stop before budget exhaustion. Operator faults and sponsorship shortfalls must never become unsigned user debt.
